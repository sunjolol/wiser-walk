# Search demand: what Christians type, and what wiserwalk.com can win

Researched 2026-09-19. Lens: search discoverability only.

## How to read this

**What I could and could not see.** I have no keyword-volume tool. Evidence here is of three kinds, and each claim is labelled:

- **[autocomplete]** Google's own suggestion endpoint (`suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&hl=en&gl=us&q=...`), queried on 2026-09-19 with about 70 seed phrases. Autocomplete shows which phrasings are common enough for Google to suggest. It does not give volumes, and order is not a ranking by volume.
- **[results]** Which sites came back for a query through the search tool, and what I saw when I opened them.
- **[inference]** My judgement. In particular, I could **not** see Google's actual results page (featured snippets, AI overviews, "people also ask" boxes), so every statement about "answered on the results page, nobody clicks" is inference from the shape of the question, not an observation.

The only traffic figure in this document: GotQuestions says of itself that it is "currently averaging 16,000,000 pageviews per month" (self-claim, read at https://www.gotquestions.org/about.html). Everything else about size is unverified and I have not guessed.

## 1. The three findings that matter most

### 1. The site already owns the data for a query family nobody serves well: "which denominations believe X"

[autocomplete] Google suggests, verbatim: "which denomination believes in predestination", "which denominations believe in speaking in tongues", "... in transubstantiation", "... in dispensationalism", "... in infant baptism", "what denominations are cessationist", "what denominations are calvinist", "what denominations are not calvinist", "what denominations believe in real presence", "what protestant denominations believe in real presence", "cessationist denominations", "denominations that speak in tongues".

[results] For "what denominations are cessationist" nothing that came back answers the question directly: a Christian Post opinion piece, two Gospel Coalition essays, Tabletalk, Wikipedia's debate article, a blog. For "which denominations believe in predestination" the direct answers are a content-farm FAQ (christianityfaq.com), Quora and Wikipedia.

Each of the six axis pages already has a section "Where the traditions sit" with 18 audited placements. That section **is** the answer to these queries. But the page titles today are "Gifts: Continuationist or Cessationist — Wiser Walk" and the H1 is the single word "Gifts". Nothing on the page says the words the searcher typed. This is the cheapest win on the site: no new data, only titles, H1s, one plain-English sentence at the top, and an H2 that repeats the question.

### 2. "Who is speaking in [chapter]" is a real, recurring, badly served question, and the Glyssen file answers it verse by verse

[autocomplete] "who is speaking in psalm 91 / 22 / 82 / 119 / 2 / 89 / 95 / 73", "who is speaking in isaiah 61 / 49 / 48 / 40 / 50 / 53 / 63", "who is speaking in proverbs 8", "who is speaking in song of solomon chapter 1 / 2 / 4 / 5", "who is speaking in deuteronomy", "who is speaking in ecclesiastes", "who is speaking in job 5", "who is talking in philippians 4". That is dozens of suggestions from four seeds, which is what a deep long tail looks like.

[results] "who is speaking in Psalm 91" returns commentaries (Enduring Word, BibleRef, EasyEnglish), a Billy Graham Cove devotional, Wikipedia and an AI-generated answer site (crosstalk.ai). "who is speaking in Proverbs 8" returns a Q&A blog, the Christian Research Institute, Quora twice, Matthew Henry. None is a structured, verse-by-verse answer.

I checked the file already in the repo (`demos/who-said-it/raw/CharacterVerse.txt`, 20,984 rows, MIT licence, SIL and Faith Comes By Hearing). It has exactly this: Psalm 91:2 narrator, 91:14-16 God; Proverbs 8:4 onward "Wisdom"; Isaiah 61:8-9 God; Song of Songs 1 split between "beloved", "Solomon (lover)" and "maidens". It even carries a confidence column (Normal, Potential, Implicit), which lets the page be honest: "Glyssen marks this as a *potential* change of speaker." A page per chapter, the public-domain text coloured by speaker, the data source named, and a "Play Who Said It?" button, is a page type nobody else has and the site can generate without a model writing a word.

Caution, and it is binding: Glyssen is a dramatisation aid for audio Bibles, not a commentary. Where traditions disagree (Isaiah 61, Psalm 22, Proverbs 8 and Christ), the page reports what the file says and that readers differ; it never rules.

### 3. The flagship is invisible to the people looking for it, and a rival is taking the name

- [autocomplete] "what denomination am i quiz", "what denomination am i christian quiz", "which christian denomination am i", "which christian denomination is right for me", "should i be orthodox or catholic quiz", "should i be catholic or protestant quiz", "am i a calvinist quiz", "am i calvinist or arminian quiz", "am i reformed quiz". The Compass answers all of these. Its title is "Theology Compass — Wiser Walk" and the page never contains the word "denomination" (confirmed by fetch).
- [autocomplete] "theology compass quiz" and "theology compass test" are themselves suggested queries. [results] They lead to **TheoCompass** (https://theocompass.com, "Match Your Beliefs to 230+ Christian Denominations", 30 questions, 13 dimensions, open beta, run by one Reddit user, with a subreddit), a YouTube video titled "I took the THEOLOGY compass test (what denomination am I?)", a Libertarian Christian Institute podcast episode about it, and a TheologyWeb forum thread. wiserwalk.com did not appear. TheoCompass cites no sources on its home page (observed) and a blogger who took it called it "made just for fun" (https://forestandfaith.org/2026/02/02/which-theology-theocompass-v1-0/). I could not verify its usage.
- The other established rival, https://denominationdifferences.com, has a quiz **and** per-pair pages (`/compare/presbyterian-vs-lutheran`, `/compare/presbyterian-vs-baptist`) with around 50 topics and 57+ footnotes per page [observed]. Its weaknesses, observed: no named author, no method, summaries rather than quotations from the confessions, "Varies widely" in many cells, and the pair page I opened had no link to its own quiz.

What this means in plain terms: the generic phrase "theology compass" is already someone else's search term. The flagship should keep its name but its title tag must carry the words people type. And the site's real difference (named method, audit log, 18 answer sheets, disputes published) should be said in the description, because no rival can say it.

**Other things seen in the audit**

1. **Axis-page H1s are single words** ("Grace", "Gifts"). The H1 is the strongest on-page statement of what a page is about. Make the H1 the question and keep the one-word label as the eyebrow above it.
2. **Axis descriptions are auto-truncated body text** ending mid-word. Write six by hand. Nobody should have to read "Monergists (God alone brings..." in a results listing.
3. **The Compass description is 236 characters.** Search engines cut at roughly 155. The strongest claim (the audit) is the part that gets cut.
4. **The seven draft `/axis/seven-deadly-sins/*` pages and `/q/seven-deadly-sins/` are in the sitemap while carrying `noindex`.** Contradictory signals. Filter drafts out of the sitemap. Also the draft axis titles are lower-case ("pride — Wiser Walk").
5. **Tradition pages run about 840 words including navigation and hold no sources** (by design, and correctly). They are the thinnest indexable pages on the site. Eighteen of 41 sitemap URLs are this type. Not a penalty risk at this scale, but they will not rank until the citation pass happens.
6. **One JSON-LD block per page** (breadcrumbs). The quiz page could add `Quiz`/`WebApplication`, axis pages `FAQPage`-style question headings. Low priority; headings matter more than markup.
7. `/tradition/` (the index) returns nothing. "List of Christian traditions compared" is a natural hub page for families 1, 3 and 8 and a home for the 18-by-6 table.
8. The flagship never uses the word "denomination" anywhere. Using it is not clickbait; it is the searcher's word. One honest sentence solves it: "Most people would call these denominations; we say traditions because several of them (Catholic, Orthodox) reject that word for themselves."

## 4. The plan: ten page families, in order

**Deliberately not on the list:** "what does the Bible say about X" topic pages, famous-misquote articles, book-by-book trivia quizzes, name meanings, and any page about purgatory, the rapture, women's ordination or sexuality. **How to know if it is working.** Verify the domain in Google Search Console (free, no cookies, nothing added to the page). After items 1-3 ship, the Queries report will show the real phrases and impressions within a few weeks. That replaces every inference in this document with observation, and it should decide whether item 4 gets 40 chapters or 400.

## 5. What I could not verify

- Any search volume, for any query. Autocomplete proves a phrasing is common, not how common.
- Whether Google shows a featured snippet or AI answer for any query here. All zero-click judgements are inference.
- Whether wiserwalk.com is indexed at all, or for what. I did not find it in any result set, but the search tool is not Google and absence there proves little.
- TheoCompass and denominationdifferences.com usage. Neither states a number on the pages I opened.
- The ProProfs "34,000 attempts" figure appeared in a search summary; I did not open the page.
- The Libertarian Christian Institute episode page returned HTTP 403; I know its title only.
- Whether "who is speaking in" demand is students with homework, preachers, or general readers. It affects tone, not whether to build.
- The exact conciliar and confessional citations proposed for the canon pages are from memory and must be opened before use.
