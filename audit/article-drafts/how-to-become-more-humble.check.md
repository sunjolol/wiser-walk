## Changes

1. **Luke 14 framing (fact).** "gave them plain advice" became "told them a parable about a wedding banquet", because Luke 14:7–8 says so.
2. **Closing of "What to expect" (fidelity to the text).** The draft said "The lower seat is not a technique for getting moved up." Luke 14:10 itself gives "so that your host will come and tell you, 'Friend, move up'" as the reason, so the sentence argued against the verse the article had just quoted. Replaced with what both texts do say: the host moves the guest, God exalts, and the timing belongs to neither guest nor reader.
3. **Opening (hedging, unprovable claim).** "it may be why Scripture hardly ever tells anyone to feel humble" guessed at a motive and made a frequency claim nobody can check. Now: Scripture's way round it is to command actions rather than feelings, which the two quotations that follow demonstrate.
4. **"Take the lower seat" (clarity).** "On a Tuesday that is the credit in the meeting..." read as if the credit were the last place. Now the best place is the credit, the parking space, the last word, and the instruction is to let someone else have it.
5. **"places of honor" outside quotation marks (spelling).** American spelling in the article's own voice, against the house British spelling. Reworded to "the best places".
6. **Capital vices sentence (fact).** "Pride heads the old list ... and it is the hardest of them to see from the inside" became "Pride comes first in the traditional list of seven capital vices." The second half was an unsupported ranking of the vices and repeated the opening paragraph. (The order pride-first is the one in the Catechism of the Catholic Church 1866; Gregory the Great's older scheme treats pride as the root of the seven, so "old list" was also loosened to "traditional".)
7. **Cross-reference (accuracy).** "Philippians 2, where every verb moves down" became "Philippians 2:5–8, where every verb moves downward", matching the published Christ-like article's scope and wording. Verse 9 moves up, so the bare chapter reference was wrong.
8. **Front matter.** `minutes: 7` became `minutes: 6`; the published articles of this length (about 680 words) carry 6.
9. **Line wrapping.** Three paragraphs had ragged or overlong lines; rewrapped. No wording change.

## Checked and left alone

- The confession paragraph. Catholic and Orthodox sacramental confession to a priest, direct confession among most Protestants, and retained private confession in some Lutheran and Anglican churches are each stated in terms the holders use, and none is ranked. "Whichever is yours, use it" takes no side.
- "he is the one who goes home justified" follows Luke 18:14 and says nothing about how justification works, so it does not enter the Catholic, Orthodox and Reformed dispute.
- "nothing suggests his list is false" (Luke 18:11–12): the parable does not dispute the Pharisee's fasting or tithing.
- No names, dates or quotations from outside Scripture appear in the article.
