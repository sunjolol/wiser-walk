## Changes made

1. Description rewritten. "Scripture says less about worry than the posters suggest" is an
   unverifiable claim and a mild sneer at other Christians. Now states what the article shows.
2. "Nobody has ever stopped worrying because they were told to" changed to "Being told to
   stop rarely stops anyone." The original is a universal factual claim that is not true.
3. Added the missing reference (Matthew 6:25) after the provision quotation.
4. "Then the last line, which is drier than its reputation" cut to "Then the last line".
   Filler, and a claim about reputation that cannot be supported.
5. "care that has come loose from trust and from usefulness" cut to "from trust".
   "Usefulness" is not in any of the texts cited; trust is (Matthew 6:30, 6:32).
6. Martha paragraph: removed "Worry multiplies the objects of attention until none of them
   gets any" (an aphorism the text does not say) and replaced it with what Luke actually
   names as the one thing, quoting Luke 10:39. The draft never said what the one thing was.
7. "Peter says the same thing" changed to "gives the same instruction". Peter does not say
   the same thing as Philippians 4:7 (the preceding paragraph); he parallels 4:6.
8. "Part of worry is the assumption that everything depends on you" softened to "Worry
   often assumes", and "is an admission" tightened to "admits". The original stated a
   psychological generalisation as fact.
9. "Is worry a sin?": the clipped fragment "your heavenly Father knows." replaced by the full
   clause with its reference, and "agree on the tone" (vague) replaced by "hear the same
   reason given for it". Both readings are still stated neutrally; neither is preferred.
10. "Persistent anxiety is also a medical matter" changed to "can also be". Not all
    persistent anxiety is clinical; the blanket statement was a medical overclaim.
11. Heading "On Tuesday" renamed "Four things to do with a worry". The original was cryptic
    and out of step with the published articles ("Four practices", "Four things that tend
    to help").
12. "Matthew 6:34 gives you permission to leave it there" changed to "says it can stay
    there". "Gives you permission" is therapy-speak.
13. Two broken line wraps repaired (the unwrapped line in "Concern is not the problem" and
    the short line before Philippians 4:7).

## Fairness read

Catholic, Orthodox, Reformed Baptist, Pentecostal: nothing in the article turns on a
disputed doctrine. The one place Christians differ (whether worry is sin to confess or
weakness to carry) is presented as two readings with no verdict. The doctor paragraph
matches the stance already published in "How to become more joyful". No tradition is named,
so none is mischaracterised. The Greek word-family claim (merimnao in Philippians 4:6,
2:20, Matthew 6; merimna in 2 Corinthians 11:28, 1 Peter 5:7) is standard lexical fact.
