Advice on forgiveness tends to fail in one of two ways. Either it tells you to feel differently
about what happened, which you cannot do on command, or it tells you to act as though
nothing happened, which would be a lie. Scripture asks for neither.

## It is a debt, and you cancel it

When Peter asks how many times he has to forgive, and offers seven,
Jesus answers "seventy-seven times" and then tells a story about money (Matthew 18:21–35).
A servant owes his king "ten thousand talents", a sum nobody could repay. The king forgives
it. The servant then goes out and chokes a colleague over "a hundred denarii".

The second debt is not small. A denarius is a day's pay in Matthew 20:2, so
a hundred of them is months of wages. The parable does not say the wrong done to you was trivial. It
says it is real, it is owed, and it sits next to a larger debt that was cancelled for you.
Paul gives the same logic: forgive each other "just as in Christ God forgave
you" (Ephesians 4:32).

So forgiveness starts as an accounting act. Someone owes you, and you stop collecting. The
Lord's Prayer uses the same picture: "as we also have forgiven our debtors" (Matthew 6:12).
Jesus' comment on that line is blunt: "if you do not forgive men their trespasses, neither
will your Father forgive yours" (Matthew 6:15). The parable ends the same way, and asks
for forgiveness "from your heart" (Matthew 18:35).

## Three things it is not

**It is not pretending you were not hurt.** Joseph's brothers sold him (Genesis 37:28).
Years later, with every means of repaying them, he says, "Am I in the place of God?" and
then, in the next breath, "what you intended against me for evil" (Genesis 50:19–20). He names it as evil.
He also wept when the subject came up (Genesis 50:17). Forgiving did not require him to
revise the facts.

**It is not giving up on justice.** Romans 12:19 says "Do not avenge yourselves", and the
reason given is not that the wrong does not matter. It is "Vengeance is Mine; I will
repay". You are handing the case to a judge, not dropping it. That also means forgiving
someone does not rule out reporting a crime or letting a court do its work.

**It is not the same as reconciliation.** Paul writes, "If it is possible on your part,
live at peace with everyone" (Romans 12:18). The condition is there on purpose. Your part
is yours; the other person's part is not. Forgiveness takes one person. A restored
relationship takes two, and trust is rebuilt by evidence over time. You can forgive someone
and still keep your distance from them, and where you are not safe, you should.

## Where Christians differ

Jesus says, "if he repents, forgive him" (Luke 17:3). He also says, "if you hold anything
against another, forgive it" (Mark 11:25), with no condition mentioned, and prays for his
executioners while they are still at work (Luke 23:34). Christians weigh these differently.
Some hold that forgiveness in the full sense is a transaction that needs the offender's
repentance, as God's forgiveness does, and that what you owe an unrepentant person is a
standing readiness to forgive and a refusal of revenge. Others hold that forgiveness is
your own release of the debt before God, owed whether or not the person ever repents, and
that repentance is the condition for reconciliation instead. In practice the two views give
nearly the same instructions: no revenge, no nursed bitterness, the door left open.

## What to do

**Say what happened, precisely.** You cannot cancel a debt you have not counted. Write it
down if that helps: what was done, what it cost. Psalm 55:12–14 does this without
softening anything.

**Hand over the case.** In prayer, give the matter to the judge Romans 12
names. Then stop prosecuting it: no rehearsing the speech, no retelling the story to
recruit a jury.

**Expect to repeat it.** In practice the seventy-seven times are often one offence,
remembered seventy-seven times. When the anger comes back, that is not proof you failed.
Ephesians 4:26 allows anger, "Be angry, yet do not sin", and forbids keeping it overnight.
Make the same decision again.

**Do one concrete good, if it is safe.** Romans 12:20–21 is practical: food, drink,
"overcome evil with good". Sometimes that is only declining to say the cutting thing.
