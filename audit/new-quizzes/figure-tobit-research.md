# Tobit: the research behind one figure entry

Companion to `figures.verified.json`. Everything below was read out of the World English Bible
British Edition on disk with `node audit/tools/webbe.mjs`, chapter by chapter, all fourteen
chapters, before any coordinate was chosen. Nothing was written from memory.

**Tobit is the father.** His son is Tobias, and WEBBE keeps the two names apart throughout, so
attribution was decidable at every verse. Two places needed care and both were checked:

- **Tobit 12:6-20 is Raphael speaking, not Tobit.** 12:5 is Tobit ("he called the angel, and said
  to him, 'Take half of all that you have brought'"); at 12:6 "he called them both privately" and
  the speaker from there to 12:20 is the angel addressing father and son. The line "It is good to
  conceal the secret of a king" (12:7, 12:11) is the angel's and must never be used for Tobit's
  voice.
- **Tobit 11:10-13 is the son acting on the father.** Tobit stumbles towards the door; Tobias
  takes hold of him and rubs the bile on his eyes. Nothing there is an act of Tobit's.

Nothing Tobias, Raphael, Anna, Sarah, Raguel or Edna does is counted: not the fish, the journey,
the marriage, the healing, Anna's weaving and the kid she was given (2:11-12), or Raguel digging
the grave (8:9).

## The argument for each coordinate

Scale: 0 is fully the left pole, 100 fully the right.

### pace 25 — Acts first

- **2:3-4, left.** The strongest single act in the book. The feast is on the table, he has not
  tasted it, the news arrives, and he is up and carrying the body indoors. "Before I had tasted
  anything, I sprang up" is verbatim.
- **1:19, left.** On learning he was being sought to be put to death he withdrew himself. Acting
  the moment he knew, in a second episode, so the axis does not rest on one scene.

Not 10 or 15, because the book also records him stopping a thing he had already set in motion
until he had checked it (5:8: call the man in first). That act is cited once, under doubt, rather
than counted twice, and 25 is where the two patterns leave him. Abraham sits at 25 on two left
items, which is the same shape.

### voice 30 — Says it

- **3:1-6, left.** After his wife's retort he wept and prayed in his sorrow and asked outright
  that his spirit be taken from him. Cited for the act of putting it into words, not for the
  content: the blueprint allows a prayer as evidence of speaking, and Moses's voice-left items in
  this roster are speech to God on the same footing.
- **14:3, left.** Grown very old, he called his son and his six grandsons in and said plainly that
  he was ready to depart out of this life.
- **1:18, right.** He buried the king's dead privately and the bodies the king searched for were
  not found. Concealing what he had done, which is how Nehemiah 2:16 is keyed in this roster.

Two left, one right, and the left acts are the more sustained. 30.

### lead 25 — Out in front

- **2:2, left.** He tells his son to go and find a poor man and says he will wait. He directs and
  stays put: "Behold, I wait for you."
- **5:3, left.** He hands over the bond, tells his son to find a man to travel with him, and says
  he will pay the wages himself. The terms are his.

Not lower, because both acts are inside his own household, and the roster's lead-left extremes
(David 15, Nehemiah 15, Deborah 15) are people taking charge of a nation. 25 is the honest size
of what the text shows.

### conflict 40 — leans Confronts, and the coordinate to question first

- **2:13, left.** He hears the kid, asks his wife where it came from and whether it is stolen, and
  tells her to give it back. He walks straight at it over a goat.
- **5:13, right.** He had pressed a stranger twice about his tribe and his name; he then tells him
  not to be angry for the asking and welcomes him as a brother. Smoothing a friction he made, the
  same shape as Abraham's conflict-right at Genesis 13:8.

**This is the coordinate a reviewer should challenge.** One act each way, and 40 sits one point
outside the 41-59 band, so it reads as placed rather than masked. The case for 40: the quarrel
runs across two verses and escalates (2:13 challenge, 2:14 he does not believe her and asks again,
and her answer in 2:14 shows how far it went), while 5:13 is one gracious sentence. The case
against: two single acts cannot really resolve an axis, and 45 would mask it. Masking it leaves
him placed on five axes, still above the four-axis floor, so either choice is safe for the engine.
I have set 40 and flagged it rather than settling it quietly.

### doubt 15 — Asks why

- **5:8, left.** His son comes in saying he has found someone. Tobit will not have it settled:
  "Call him to me, that I may know of what tribe he is, and whether he be a trustworthy man to go
  with you."
- **5:11, left.** The man deflects with a question of his own. Tobit asks again: "I want to know,
  brother, your kindred and your name." He is satisfied only at 5:13, when the name connects to
  people he knew himself.
- **2:14, left.** His wife says the kid was a gift. "I didn't believe her, and I asked her to
  return it to the owners."

Three left acts across two episodes and nothing on the other side. 15 rather than 5 or 10 because
two of the three are the same conversation, and because 2:14 is disbelief of a person rather than
the axis's purer case of needing a reason before going on.

### plan 10 — Plans ahead

- **1:14, left.** He left ten talents of silver in trust with Gabael at Rages, and it was still
  there years later.
- **4:1-2, left.** Having asked for death, he remembers the money and reasons that he should
  explain it to his son first. Settling the estate before dying.
- **10:1, left.** "Tobit his father counted every day." He knew how long the journey should take
  and kept the count against it.

Three left acts, in three separate episodes, and no cited act on the right. The whole plot is
driven by a deposit he made years earlier and a bond he still had in the house. 10.

## Considered and rejected

1. **1:6-8, the tithes, for plan-left.** A fixed annual arrangement, and Daniel 6:10 is kept in
   this roster on exactly that ground. Left out because the coordinate would then rest partly on
   devotion rather than on provisioning, and three firmer items exist. The rule that this site
   counts knowledge and never devotion governs the reader's statements, not a figure's acts, so
   this is a preference, not a prohibition.
2. **1:11, refusing the Gentiles' bread, and 1:6, going to Jerusalem alone when the tribe
   sacrificed to Baal (1:5), for conflict-left.** Standing apart from a crowd is not confronting
   anybody. Nobody is addressed in either verse.
3. **1:16-17, bread to the hungry and burial of whoever he found, for plan-right.** "If I saw any
   of my race dead... I buried him" does read as responding to what arrives. Rejected because the
   axis asks about provisioning versus improvising, and this is a standing practice of giving. It
   would put an almsgiving verse under a temperament heading, which is the reach this roster keeps
   getting caught on.
4. **2:7, waiting for sunset before digging the grave, for pace-right.** The delay is what the
   burial required, not deliberation about whether to act: he had already acted at 2:4.
5. **2:10, going to the physicians.** Says nothing on any of the six axes.
6. **4:5-19, the content of his instructions, for pace-right and doubt.** 4:18 — "Ask counsel of
   every man who is wise, and don't despise any counsel that is profitable" — would read straight
   onto weighing first. Rejected: that is advice he gave, not an act of his. The blueprint allows
   a speech as evidence of the act of making it, and 14:3 already carries that act.
7. **4:2 for pace-right.** "He said to himself, I have asked for death; why do I not call my son
   Tobias" is genuinely a decision reasoned out before acting. Cited once, under plan-left, and
   not counted twice; it is part of why pace sits at 25 rather than lower.
8. **5:8 for pace-right.** The same act as the doubt-left item. Cited once. Same reasoning.
9. **5:14-15, the wages fixed in advance, for plan-left.** A fourth plan item, honest and
   supported, left out only for the three-item cap. On the record if one of the three ever falls.
10. **10:6, "Don't worry. He is in good health," for doubt-right.** Tempting: he asserts his son
    is safe with no news at all. Rejected because 10:1-3, five verses earlier, has him counting
    the days, asking whether the boy is detained or Gabael dead, and very grieved. The reassurance
    is spoken to a grieving wife; the chapter does not show him going on without a reason.
11. **11:16-17, going out to the gate to meet his daughter-in-law, for conflict-right.** No
    dispute is in view. Welcome is not reconciliation.
12. **12:1-5 for lead-right.** He raises the guide's wages, his son proposes half of everything,
    and he says it is due to him. But 12:5 has him calling the angel and making the offer himself,
    so the episode reads as him out in front, not standing someone else up.
13. **12:13, "when you didn't delay to rise up, and leave your dinner."** A third party's report,
    in the angel's mouth, of the act already cited at 2:3-4. Rejected on the precedent that
    removed David's assessment of Abigail (1 Samuel 25:33).
14. **Chapter 13, the prayer of rejoicing.** Content only. The act of writing it adds nothing that
    14:3 does not already carry, and using its content would rest a coordinate on piety.
15. **14:10-11, the last instructions.** Content of instruction again; 14:3 carries the act.

    1-3 evidence items per axis, every `did` one sentence, no virtue words
    no verse is cited on more than one axis

**No cell is "the text does not show this."** All six axes are placed from cited acts, so he clears
the four-axis floor with room; if conflict is masked at 45 he still clears it on five.

## Two things the editor should know about the shape of the roster

- **Tobit's nearest neighbour is Moses, 33.2 units away** (Moses 45,25,25,35,20,35). The closest
  existing pair on the roster is David and Martha at 25.0, so Tobit adds no new collision. He sits
  67.6 units from dead centre, well clear of the central-figure problem that put Jesus, Mary of
  Nazareth and Priscilla in the way of every moderate answer sheet.
- **He adds nothing to the scarce poles.** Every one of his coordinates is left of centre, and
  plan-left is the crowded side the notes already warned about. The axes that needed a sixth
  figure — conflict-right, plan-right, pace-right — are no better off for him. That is what the
  text shows; it is a fact about the roster, not a reason to move a coordinate.

## One plain sentence for the owner, if he is asked anything about this

Tobit's book is in Catholic and Orthodox Bibles and not in Protestant ones, so the figure page
should say where the book stands before it says anything about the man.
