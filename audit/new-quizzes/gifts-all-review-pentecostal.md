# Review of the all-gifts edit: classical Pentecostal lens

Reviewer's chair: a classical Pentecostal pastor-scholar (Assemblies of God and kindred churches; the way our
congregations receive tongues with interpretation, weigh prophecy and pray for the sick). Adversarial, and bound by
the same six statement rules as the editor. I accept rule 6 as fair: nothing below asks for a statement about how
much anyone prays.

Read: `gifts-all-edit.md` in full, beside `spiritual-gifts.ts`; `QUIZ-BLUEPRINTS.md` (the six rules, quiz two);
`audit-pentecostal.md` (what this chair objected to last time); `AUDIT-LOG.md` decision 1 and the row that settled
the earlier G6; the Compass's Gifts axis in `site/src/data/compass.json`; the loaded critic's sections on knowledge,
healing and tongues. Nothing under `site/` was touched and nothing was committed. This is the only file written.

Every verse quoted or described below was opened with `node audit/tools/webbe.mjs`. The quotations I propose were
run through `--check` with a deliberately wrong control beside them: 10 of 10 real strings verbatim, the control
(“But earnestly desire the greater gifts”, which is not what this translation says) rejected. Word counts of my
replacement statements were counted by script.

One honesty note about my own chair. I speak as a Pentecostal would, but **no Pentecostal document was opened for
this review** (no position paper, no Fee, no Gee, no Horton). So nothing I say about "how my churches do it" may go
on a page as a cited Pentecostal position. It is a reviewer's objection, to be met in wording, not a source.

---

## Verdict in four sentences

The owner's decision has been carried out fairly: the six gifts are in, in the passage's own words, described as
events and as what other people said, and none of them is drawn as a hunch, a feeling or a personality trait. **I
raise no blocker.** I raise eight majors and five minors, and most of the majors are one fault in several places:
the page cites a verse (1 Corinthians 14:13, 14:29, 14:2, 12:30) and then writes a statement or a sentence that
stops short of what that verse says. Nothing in the edit teaches that every Christian must have these gifts; one
of my fixes (finding 7) has to be weighed against that, and I say so there.

## What PASSES, said explicitly

**The frame.**
- **[S6]**, the sentence on all six pages: "Many Christians believe God still gives this gift today; many believe
  he gave it only for the time of the apostles." Identical on all six, no warm clause for either side. PASS. I do
  not ask for "to be welcomed and tested" back; the editor's reason for dropping it is right.
- Healing's extra sentence, "Both believe that God still heals and answers prayer." PASS.
- **[S3]** on wisdom, discernment and faith. It places that disagreement where it actually is. PASS.
- `result`: "Some of the everyday patterns here are temperament as much as gift". The old wording, said of tongues
  or healing, was exactly what this chair must not find. The fix is right. PASS. The new sentence "this page does
  not say what any event was" is even-handed as written. PASS.
- `intro`, `flat`, `omitted`, tagline, description. PASS. `omitted` keeps "by prophecy with the laying on of the
  hands of the elders" inside the quotation and does not trim it; the last sentence (prophecy scored, whether
  anybody is a prophet not) matches the distinction my own churches draw between the gift and the office.
- The optional line above the scale ("If something has never happened to you, disagree is an honest answer").
  PASS, and I would ship it: it also stops a reader who has never spoken in tongues from feeling cornered.
- The note to the engineer about the share text (loaded critic S20). I endorse it from this chair: a Pentecostal
  pastor does not want "Where my answers pointed: miracles, healing, prophecy" pasted into a group chat as a
  self-announced claim. Gifts are weighed in the body.
- The decision to leave in, on purpose, the preacher who holds that preaching is prophecy (unsolved item 5). PASS.
  The page must not rule on it, and it does not.

**The word of knowledge.** Slot 2, "People have asked me how I knew something about them that nobody had told me."
PASS, and it is the best sentence in the edit from this chair: it is what people really say after a word of
knowledge, it is silent on how, and it puts the claim in other people's mouths. "The New Testament calls no
recorded act a word of knowledge" is true and honest. Keeping Acts 5:1-4 under discernment only, with "Luke does
not say how Peter knew", is accurate; my predecessor wanted "not having been told", and the new sentence says the
same thing without claiming the verse. PASS. I do **not** ask for Acts 10:19-20 or Acts 5 to be listed under
knowledge: the text does not call them that, and the rule that kept James 5 off the healing page applies to me too.

**Healing.** Slot 1 and slot 3. PASS: events, "after" not "because", nothing counted. The summary's "They cannot
tell this gift apart from an answer to prayer" is a distinction my own tradition also draws (we all pray for the
sick; Paul still asks "Do all have gifts of healings?"). PASS. All four act sentences checked against the text:
nothing of the name of Jesus, the prayer, the laying on of hands or the signs is trimmed. PASS as sentences (see
finding 6 for the selection).
- Slot 2, the meal. **PASS with a recorded reservation, no change asked.** It is the nearest thing in the edit to
  rule 6, and it sets prayer against practical care, which my people do not choose between. But a reader who does
  both answers "disagree" truthfully and the row rises, nobody is shamed at either end, and every alternative I
  tried either counted praying, duplicated the miracles reverse item, or became a second "others" statement.

**Miracles.** All three statements. PASS. "Used the word miracle" keeps the word in the witnesses' mouths. The
acts are one verse for each reading (Galatians 3:5 and 2 Corinthians 12:12), which is fair, and Acts 19:11-12
keeps the evil spirits going out. PASS as statements and acts (see finding 8 for the summary).

**Prophecy.** All three statements. PASS. The summary defines prophecy by 1 Corinthians 14:3, which is how my
churches define it and what this chair asked for last time, and "let the others discern" is how we test it. The
gloss ("build them up, urge them on and comfort them") takes no side. The four acts keep "by the Spirit", "the
Holy Spirit said", Philip's four daughters who prophesied, and "the Holy Spirit says". PASS.

**Tongues.** Slots 1 and 2. PASS. "No language I know" grants nothing and denies nothing; a Pentecostal can agree
with it without conceding that it was no language. All four act sentences keep the Spirit, the fire, "as the
Spirit gave them the ability", the laying on of hands and "and prophesied". PASS. Leaving out Mark 16:17 is right.
- **The way the row scores** (private use only reaches "some of this"; the top needs an utterance in church with
  its meaning given). PASS in principle. My churches read "Do all speak with various languages?" (12:30) of the
  gift used in the gathering, which is the row's own listing passage, so crowning that form is not a slight. But
  the page must tell the reader, which it does not yet do: finding 3.
- I note, without raising it, that slots 1 and 2 are one fact and its negation, the fault the editor removed from
  leading and evangelism. For this gift there is little else honest to ask.

**Interpreting tongues.** Slots 1, 2 and 3, the summary and the three act sentences. PASS. "Acts never shows anyone
doing it" is true. Restoring "I desire to have you all speak with other languages" to the 14:5 sentence was the
right catch. "People at church look to me" stands on 14:28 ("if there is no interpreter"), which assumes a church
knows who interprets.

**Job 2, the six replacements** (giving, leading, mercy, faith, evangelism, hospitality). PASS from this chair, all
six. Faith's is a real improvement for us: "I keep going with a project" drew the gift as doggedness, and "people
ask me whether I still think it will happen" does not.

**Discernment.** Removing "I decide something is off about a person" is right, and I say so as the successor of the
reviewer who defended it: what he defended was a Spirit-given perception, and the new statement keeps room for that
by being silent on how. Slot 1 unchanged and slot 2 ("a talk or a book"). PASS. The summary's refusal to define the
gift in the site's voice. PASS. (Slot 3's object: finding 4.)

## Findings

### 1. MAJOR. Tongues slot 3 shuts out the speaker who interprets, which the quiz's own cited verse tells him to do

**Words objected to:** "In church I have spoken in words from no language I know, and **someone else** has said
what they meant."

**Why.** 1 Corinthians 14:13 is one of the three passages this edit prints on the interpretation page: "let him
who speaks in another language pray that he may interpret". 14:5 says "unless he interprets". In my churches the
person who gives the utterance sometimes gives the interpretation too, exactly as 14:13 asks. That reader must
answer "disagree" to the only statement that can carry the tongues row to the top, because of two words. Drafter B
noticed the point ("the same person may do both") and then wrote "someone else" anyway; no critic caught it.

**Replacement I would accept (20 words):**
> In church I have spoken in words from no language I know, and everyone was then told what they meant.

Also acceptable (18): "In church I have spoken in words from no language I know, and the meaning was then given."

**Cost, stated:** the "others" element becomes the church hearing the meaning, not a second person's act. The
editor already calls this O "the weakest kind", so little is lost; interpretation slot 2 ("When someone has
spoken ...") is unaffected.

### 2. MAJOR. The tongues summary quotes the two-word half of 1 Corinthians 14:2 and leaves out who is spoken to

**Words objected to:** "to Corinth Paul writes of a speaker whom “no one understands” (1 Corinthians 14:2)".

**Why.** The verse reads: "For he who speaks in another language speaks not to men, but to God, for no one
understands, but in the Spirit he speaks mysteries." The summary lifts the three words that make the practice
sound pointless and drops "speaks not to men, but to God", which is the reason my people pray this way at all, and
which is what two of the three tongues statements ask about (praying). This is the same fault my predecessor
found five times in the first draft (his G3 and G4): a sentence accurate as far as it goes that stops short of the
clause the passage turns on. The contrast with Acts 2:6 survives the longer quotation.

**Replacement (verbatim substring, checked):**
> to Corinth Paul writes that such a speaker “speaks not to men, but to God, for no one understands” (1 Corinthians 14:2).

### 3. MAJOR. The tongues page does not tell the reader how the row is built, so a reader who prays in tongues daily is shown "some of this" with no reason

**Words objected to:** "These statements ask only whether this has happened to you, on your own or in church."

**Why.** That sentence says private and public count alike. They do not: the editor's own note to the owner says a
reader who prays this way only in private gets 67 and no headline, on purpose. I accept the design (see PASS). What
I do not accept is that the owner is told and the reader is not. A member of my church who has prayed in tongues
for thirty years will read "some of this" as the site doubting her, unless the page says what the top of the row
needs and whose instruction that is.

**Replacement for that sentence:**
> These statements ask whether this has happened to you on your own, and whether it has happened in church with
> the meaning then given, which is what Paul asks of a church (1 Corinthians 14:27-28). If only the first is true
> of you, this row will show part of the way up and no higher.

It says nothing about a "private prayer language" and cites nothing but Paul, so the loaded critic's caution
(no Pentecostal document opened) still holds.

### 4. MAJOR. Discernment quotes 14:29 and 1 John 4:1, and then no statement has their object

**Words objected to:** slot 3, "I have warned people about something **they were being taught about God**, and
they later told me I was right." And in the summary, "when you weighed something said or taught about God".

**Why.** The summary rests the gift on "let the others discern", which is about what prophets have just said, and
on "test the spirits ... because many false prophets have gone out into the world". In my churches this is the
weekly use of the gift: a prophecy, an interpretation or a "God told me" is given, and somebody has to weigh it.
After this edit the three objects are "a new idea", "a talk or a book" and "something they were being taught":
all three are vetting teaching, the Beroean act (Acts 17:11), which needs only an open Bible. The thing Paul and
John actually name, a message given as coming from God, is in the summary ("said or taught") and in none of the
statements. Drafter A's original had it ("said or taught in God’s name"); it was cut for the exclamation reading,
which is a fair reason to reword and not a reason to drop the object.

This is not a plea for the hunch to come back. The replacement is still an act, still confirmed by other people,
still silent on how, and a cessationist has warned friends about "God told me" claims as often as I have.

**Replacement (19 words):**
> I have warned people about something they were told was from God, and they later said I was right.

Also acceptable (20), if the editor wants teaching kept in the same statement: "I have warned people about
something they were told or taught about God, and they later said I was right." ("said" for the second "told", so
the verb is not used twice.)

**Summary, one phrase to match:** "These statements ask what has happened when you weighed something taught about
God, or something said to be from him."

### 5. MAJOR. The word of knowledge: two of the three statements belong to the first reading, and the "even split" is not even

**Words objected to:** "Each reading can reach about two thirds of the row; only a reader with both reaches the
top. That even split is what the summary promises"; and slot 3, "When a question about the Bible comes up, I am
the one asking rather than answering."

**Why, with the arithmetic.** Slot 1 (quoting the part of the Bible people were trying to remember) and slot 3
(the reverse item) both measure being well-read. Only slot 2 sees the gift as my people know it.
- A reader with the first reading only: strongly agree, strongly disagree, strongly disagree = +2, -2, +2 = net +2,
  which prints as 67.
- A reader with the second reading only, who is a new or unread believer: strongly disagree, strongly agree,
  strongly agree = -2, +2, -2 = net -2, which prints as 33, "not found here".

The second reading reaches two thirds only if the reader also happens to be neutral on Bible knowledge. In my
churches the person known for words of knowledge is often not the best-read person in the room, and this row marks
her down for it. The loaded critic saw the choice and named "the other honest choice" (tilt the row to the second
reading). I am not asking for that. I am asking for the split the file claims.

**Replacement for slot 3, a reverse item either reading can disagree with (16 words, -1):**
> When people need to know something, I am as much in the dark as they are.

A newcomer's honest answer is Agree; nobody is shamed; it names neither reading. With it, first reading only = 67,
second reading only = 67, both = 100. It has not been read by the plain critic and should be. Also acceptable (16):
"It is other people, not me, who come out with the thing everyone needed to know."

**If no neutral reverse item survives the plain critic:** keep the statements, delete the words "even split" from
the file, and change the summary's "these statements ask about both" to "two of these statements ask about the
first and one about the second", so the page does not promise what the row does not do.

### 6. MAJOR. Every recorded act on the healing page is an apostle's

**Words objected to:** the selection, not any sentence: Acts 3:1-12 (Peter), Acts 9:32-35 (Peter), Acts 28:7-9
(Paul), Acts 5:12-16 ("By the hands of the apostles").

**Why.** One side of the disagreement holds that these gifts marked the apostles. A healing page on which all four
acts are apostolic illustrates that side's case without saying so. The miracles page was balanced on purpose
(Galatians 3:5 beside 2 Corinthians 12:12); healing was not. The text has a healing by a man Luke calls "a certain
disciple" (Acts 9:10), it is used nowhere else in the quiz, and it needs no connection made by the site: he lays
his hands on a blind man, who receives his sight.

**Replacement.** Put Acts 9:17-18 in place of Acts 9:32-35 (Peter keeps Acts 3 and the shadow in 5:15; which one
goes is the editor's call, and four remains the limit):
> **Acts 9:17-18.** Ananias entered the house, laid his hands on Saul and said that the Lord, who had appeared to
> him on the road, had sent him so that he might receive his sight and be filled with the Holy Spirit; immediately
> something like scales fell from his eyes, he received his sight, and he arose and was baptised.

Every name in that sentence is inside the range, by the editor's own rule. If the editor wants "a disciple at
Damascus" on the page, the range must start at 9:10 and the sentence must then also carry the vision, Ananias's
objection and the Lord's answer, with nothing trimmed.

### 7. MAJOR. `low` quotes 1 Corinthians 12:30 and stops one verse short

**Words objected to:** "And Paul asks, “Do all have gifts of healings? Do all speak with various languages? Do all
interpret?” (1 Corinthians 12:30)." followed by nothing.

**Why.** The first audit declined to add "earnestly desire spiritual gifts" (14:1) to the intro on the ground that
"a cessationist reads it as addressed to Corinth" (AUDIT-LOG, Pentecostal G6). Verse 12:30 is addressed to Corinth
too, and it is now the site's chosen comfort for a low row. Paul's next sentence is "But earnestly desire the best
gifts" (12:31). Quoted together they say what my churches teach: not everyone has each gift, and a low row is not
a ceiling. Quoted alone, 12:30 is the verse most often used against my tradition, placed at the exact point where
a reader of my tradition is looking at a low row. The site adds no gloss, which is right, and I accept 12:30 there
because we read it of the gift used in the gathering. I do not accept the full stop after it.

I was also asked to watch that the page never teaches that every Christian must have these gifts. The pair of
verses is what guards both sides: 12:30 against "must", 12:31 against "cannot".

**Replacement for the last sentence of `low`:**
> And Paul asks, “Do all have gifts of healings? Do all speak with various languages? Do all interpret?”
> (1 Corinthians 12:30), and goes on, “But earnestly desire the best gifts” (1 Corinthians 12:31).

**If the cessationist reviewer will not have 12:31:** then take 12:30 out of `low` as well and leave it where it
already stands as a listing passage on the healing, tongues and interpretation pages. Both or neither.

### 8. MAJOR (one sentence). The miracles summary lacks the limit the file says it has

**Words objected to:** the miracles summary as a whole, against the file's own claims: "which is why both summaries
say what the statements cannot tell apart" (section 7, unsolved item 3) and the loaded critic's condition on
healing slot 1 ("fair only while the summary says the statements cannot tell a gift from an answered prayer").

**Why.** The healing summary has the sentence. The miracles summary does not, although all three miracles
statements are about answers to prayer. In my churches "workings of miracles" is not another name for every
remarkable answer to prayer, which any believer may be part of; and the cessationist reader who agrees truthfully
with slot 1 needs the same sentence for his own reasons. Both chairs want it.

**Replacement: add after "what the people who were there called it."**
> They cannot tell this gift apart from an answer to prayer.

### 9. MINOR. The word of knowledge summary hides who gives, in the one clause that describes my people's reading

**Words objected to:** "others as **being given a fact** that nobody had told you".

**Why.** The first reading is described in the active voice in its holders' own terms. The second is put in a
passive with no giver, and the giver is the whole of what its holders say. The listing verse itself says
"according to the same Spirit". This is small, but trimming the Spirit out of sentences was the theme of the last
review from this chair.

**Replacement:** "others as the Spirit making known to you a fact that nobody had told you".

### 10. MINOR. "Whether any of it was from God"

**Words objected to:** prophecy summary, "Whether **any of it** was from God is for your church to weigh, not this
page."

**Why.** "Any of it" has the ring of doubt ("if any of it was"). The sentence is otherwise exactly what my
churches practise.

**Replacement:** "Whether it was from God is for your church to weigh, not this page."

### 11. MINOR. The `disputed` note guards in one direction and reassures one reader

**Words objected to:** "and calls no event a miracle, a healing or a word from God. A reader who believes these
gifts are not given today can answer every statement truthfully ..."

**Why.** Of three sentences, two exist for the reader who believes the gifts have ceased. That reader needs them
and I would not remove either. But the promise "calls no event a miracle" has a twin the page also keeps and never
states: it calls no event a coincidence either. `result` already says it evenly ("does not say what any event
was"). The note should use the even form.

**Replacement for the second sentence:**
> This page takes no side: it quotes each passage, asks what has happened and what people have said, and does not
> say what any event was, whether a miracle, a healing, a word from God or none of these.

### 12. MINOR. The six are named as "the" disputed gifts three times, while three more pages carry a dispute sentence

**Words objected to:** `draftNote` and `disputed`, which list six; against [S3] on wisdom, discernment and faith.

**Why.** The first audit's blocker from both ends was that 1 Corinthians 12:8-10 is one sentence and the draft
split it. The split is gone from the scoring. It survives in the copy: a reader meets a list of six "disputed"
gifts on the intro, the result and the draft note, and only on three gift pages learns that the same sentence's
other three are disputed by some as well. One clause mends it, and it stops the six reading as the odd ones.

**Replacement: add to `disputed`, after the first sentence:**
> All six stand in one sentence of Paul’s (1 Corinthians 12:8-10), beside wisdom, faith and discernment, and some
> Christians say the same of those three.

This is as uncited as [S3] and belongs on the same list for the citation pass.

### 13. MINOR, for the engineer. No badge, no grouping, no second-class placement for the six

Nothing in the edit does this, and section 8 rightly says to interleave the six among the old rows. I ask that it
be written down as a rule and tested like the adjacency rule: in the running order, the ranking, the gift index
and the share card, the six rows look and sort like every other row; the `disputed` note prints once with the
intro and once with the result, never as a tag on a row; and [S6] is the only place a gift page speaks of the
disagreement. A "disputed" chip beside "prophecy" would undo in the interface what the wording got right.

## What I considered and did not raise

So that the list above can be trusted, these are objections a less careful member of my tradition might make, and
why I do not make them.

- **No statement or sentence about seeking the gifts, Spirit baptism or tongues as evidence of it.** Those are my
  tradition's doctrines, not the text's bare words, and the Compass is the place for them.
- **No "private prayer language" wording.** Finding 3 cites Paul only.
- **James 5:14-15 left off healing, Mark 16:17 left off tongues, 2 Timothy 4:20 left off healing.** All three
  calls are right, for the editor's reasons.
- **Acts 16:16-18 not restored to discernment, and no narrative added under the word of knowledge.** The text does
  not name either act as the gift.
- **"Pray" appearing in eight statements.** None counts it. Rule 6 is kept.
- **The prophecy row rewarding the person who speaks often in church.** Slot 2 puts the confirmation in other
  people's mouths and the summary sends the weighing to the church, which is where we send it.
- **"A habit of staying sure" in the faith summary.** It says "look only for", quotes 1 Corinthians 13:2, and was
  settled last time.
- **"Many ... many" in [S6]** as a head count. It is a fair plain sentence and I would not trade its symmetry for
  a statistic.
