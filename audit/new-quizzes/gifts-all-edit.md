# What are your spiritual gifts? All gifts in: the editor's proposal

Status: **a proposal, nothing wired.** Nothing under `site/` was touched, nothing was committed, and this is the only file written. Read for it: `QUIZ-BLUEPRINTS.md`, `spiritual-gifts.ts`, `gifts-final.md`, `AUDIT-LOG.md` (decisions 1, 4, 6), `audit-pentecostal.md`, `audit-cessationist.md`, the Compass's Gifts axis in `site/src/data/compass.json`, both drafts (`gifts-all-statements-A.md`, `-B.md`) and both critiques (`gifts-all-critique-loaded.md`, `-plain.md`). Every verse quoted or described below was opened with `node audit/tools/webbe.mjs` (World English Bible British Edition). Every word count was counted by script.

The result in numbers: **nineteen gifts, fifty-seven statements** (38 keyed +1, 19 keyed -1), every gift with exactly one reverse item and at least one "others" statement. Longest statement: 20 words. 26 statements are new or replaced: 18 for the six added gifts, 6 "others" replacements, 2 in discernment. Thirty-one of the present thirty-nine are untouched.

Direction: **+1** agreeing raises the gift, **-1** agreeing lowers it. **O** marks the "others" statement: what other people bring to the reader, or have said or done in answer to them.

## For the owner, in plain words (the two questions that were asked in jargon)

- **The discernment statement.** "I decide something is off about a person before I could say what it is" is replaced by **"I have warned people about something they were being taught about God, and they later told me I was right."** The Bible passage is about weighing what is said and taught, not about sizing people up, and the new statement says nothing about how you knew, so a Pentecostal and a cessationist can both answer it.
- **Measuring every gift the same way.** Six gifts had no statement about what other people bring you or say of you, so a newcomer was steered towards those six. Each now has one. For example, giving gains **"People tell me when someone they know is going without, and I usually find something to give."**

Three more things he should be told plainly, none of them a question:

1. With healing, miracles, prophecy, tongues and interpreting tongues, a reader can see which gift a statement is about. There is no honest way round it, and the intro now says so.
2. Healing, miracles and tongues cannot be asked about without the word "pray". Eight of the eighteen new statements use it. None counts how much, how long or how hard anybody prays; each asks what happened or what people said.
3. Someone who prays in tongues only on their own will see "some of this" on that row and will not have it as their headline; the top of the row needs it to have happened in church with the meaning given, which is what 1 Corinthians 14:27-28 asks of the church. Without that, tongues would be the headline for every reader who has ever done it, whatever else is true of them.

One conflict to know about: `HANDOFF.md` says "keep the quiz under about fifty statements". Nineteen gifts at three each is fifty-seven, about eight minutes. The scope for this pass fixed nineteen and three, so I have not cut; if play shows people tiring, the first cut is a decision about which gifts, not about items per gift.

# 1. The six new gifts

The sentence every one of the six summaries carries, identical on all six pages so no gift gets a warmer or cooler version (called **[S6]** below; the decision between the critics is in section 7):

> Many Christians believe God still gives this gift today; many believe he gave it only for the time of the apostles.

Each gift page then prints the existing link line once ("The Theology Compass sets that disagreement out in both sides’ own words.", through `groupHref()`; slug `gifts`, key `spirit`).

Slot numbers are the statement's position within its gift (the running order takes each gift's first statement in round one, and so on). They are chosen so that a newcomer's first round meets the gentlest statement of each new gift and the bare events come last, while the reverse items stay spread across the three rounds (7, 7 and 5).

## 1.1 The word of knowledge

- **key** `knowledge` · **slug** `knowledge` · **display name** the word of knowledge
- **The passage it is named from (verbatim):** 1 Corinthians 12:8: “and to another the word of knowledge according to the same Spirit”

**Summary for the gift's page:**

> The passage names a word of knowledge, something said, and nowhere explains it. Some Christians take it as having what the Bible teaches ready to say when it is needed, others as being given a fact that nobody had told you, and these statements ask about both; they cannot see how you came to know anything. The New Testament calls no recorded act a word of knowledge, so the passages below are the places where Paul writes of knowledge among a church’s gifts. Many Christians believe God still gives this gift today; many believe he gave it only for the time of the apostles.

**Recorded in the early church:**

- **1 Corinthians 1:4-7.** Paul thanks God for the grace given to the Corinthians in Christ Jesus: that in everything they were enriched in him, in all speech and all knowledge, even as the testimony of Christ was confirmed in them, so that they come behind in no gift, waiting for the revelation of the Lord Jesus Christ.
- **1 Corinthians 14:6.** Paul asks what he would profit them if he came speaking with other languages, unless he spoke to them by way of revelation, or of knowledge, or of prophesying, or of teaching.
- **Romans 15:14.** Paul writes that he is persuaded the Christians at Rome are full of goodness, filled with all knowledge, and able also to admonish others.

Not cited, on purpose: Acts 5:1-4 (it stays under discernment only, see section 3); 1 Corinthians 14:24-25 (Paul writes it of prophecy); 1 Corinthians 13:2 and 13:8 (not acts, and 13:8-12 is on the Compass's list of passages the two sides argue from).

## 1.2 Healing

- **key** `healing` · **slug** `healing` · **display name** healing
- **The passages it is named from (verbatim):** 1 Corinthians 12:9: “and to another gifts of healings by the same Spirit” · 1 Corinthians 12:28: “then gifts of healings” · 1 Corinthians 12:30: “Do all have gifts of healings?”

**Summary for the gift's page:**

> The translation quoted here says gifts of healings, with both words in the plural, and Paul asks, “Do all have gifts of healings?” (1 Corinthians 12:30). These statements ask what has happened when you prayed for people who were ill and what they said afterwards. They cannot tell this gift apart from an answer to prayer, and nothing here counts how much anyone prays. Many Christians believe God still gives this gift today; many believe he gave it only for the time of the apostles. Both believe that God still heals and answers prayer.

The last sentence reuses the Compass's audited words ("They affirm that God still heals and answers prayer") without welding them into one causal claim, as the loaded critic asked, and gives it to both sides, as the plain critic asked.

**Recorded in the early church:**

- **Acts 3:1-12.** At the temple gate Peter told a man lame from birth that he had no silver or gold but would give what he had, and said, in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, get up and walk; he took him by the right hand and raised him up, immediately his feet and ankle bones received strength, and he went into the temple walking, leaping and praising God; when the people ran together in wonder, Peter asked why they looked at him and John as though they had made the man walk by their own power or godliness.
- **Acts 9:32-35.** At Lydda Peter found Aeneas, who had been bedridden for eight years because he was paralysed, told him that Jesus Christ heals him, and told him to get up and make his bed; immediately he arose, and all who lived at Lydda and in Sharon saw him and turned to the Lord.
- **Acts 28:7-9.** The father of Publius, the chief man of the island, lay sick with fever and dysentery; Paul went in to him, prayed, and laying his hands on him healed him, and then the rest on the island who had diseases came and were cured.
- **Acts 5:12-16.** By the hands of the apostles many signs and wonders were done among the people, and more believers were added to the Lord; the sick were carried into the streets and laid on cots and mattresses so that Peter’s shadow might fall on some of them as he came by, and crowds from the cities around Jerusalem brought the sick and those tormented by unclean spirits, and they were all healed.

Left off: James 5:14-15 (it names elders, oil and prayer and no gift, so placing it here is a connection the site would be making); 2 Timothy 4:20 (true and in the text, but as an "act" it reads as the site taking a turn in the argument).

## 1.4 Prophecy

- **key** `prophecy` · **slug** `prophecy` · **display name** prophecy
- **The passages it is named from (verbatim):** 1 Corinthians 12:10: “and to another prophecy” · Romans 12:6: “if prophecy, let’s prophesy according to the proportion of our faith”

**Summary for the gift's page:**

> Paul says what it is for: “he who prophesies speaks to men for their edification, exhortation, and consolation” (1 Corinthians 14:3), that is, to build them up, urge them on and comfort them. He also tells the church to weigh it: “let the others discern” (1 Corinthians 14:29). These statements ask whether you have said what you believed God gave you to say, and what people said afterwards. Whether any of it was from God is for your church to weigh, not this page. 1 Corinthians 12:28 and Ephesians 4:11 also name prophets among the people given to the church; a result here names a pattern and says nothing about whether anybody is a prophet. Many Christians believe God still gives this gift today; many believe he gave it only for the time of the apostles.

The plain gloss after the first quotation is the editor's decision: the plain critic showed that a newcomer knows none of the three words, the quiz already glosses the translation elsewhere ("says exhorting where many others say encouraging"), and the three plain verbs are the ordinary meanings and take no side. The sentence about prophets follows shepherding's ("says nothing about who should hold an office"); both drafters and the loaded critic asked for it, because "Your answers pointed most to: prophecy" will otherwise be read as "you are a prophet".

**Recorded in the early church:**

- **Acts 11:27-30.** Prophets came down from Jerusalem to Antioch, and one of them, Agabus, stood up and indicated by the Spirit that there would be a great famine all over the world, which Luke says happened in the days of Claudius; the disciples, each as he had plenty, determined to send relief to the brothers in Judea, and sent it to the elders by Barnabas and Saul.
- **Acts 13:1-3.** In the church at Antioch there were prophets and teachers; as they served the Lord and fasted, the Holy Spirit said to separate Barnabas and Saul for the work to which he had called them, and when they had fasted and prayed and laid their hands on them, they sent them away.
- **Acts 21:9-14.** Philip had four virgin daughters who prophesied; a prophet named Agabus came down from Judea, bound his own feet and hands with Paul’s belt, and said that the Holy Spirit says the Jews at Jerusalem will bind the man who owns the belt in this way and deliver him into the hands of the Gentiles; the company begged Paul not to go up, he would not be persuaded, and they ceased, saying that the Lord’s will be done.
- **1 Timothy 1:18.** Paul commits his instruction to Timothy according to the prophecies which were given to him before, so that by them he may wage the good warfare.

Acts 21:8 is evangelism's, so this range starts at verse 9. Acts 15:30-32 (Judas and Silas, "being prophets themselves") stays on the encouraging page, where the audit restored the phrase. Drafter B's "given about him" is corrected to the text's "given to you".

## 1.5 Tongues

- **key** `tongues` · **slug** `tongues` · **display name** tongues
- **The passages it is named from (verbatim):** 1 Corinthians 12:10: “to another different kinds of languages” · 1 Corinthians 12:28: “and various kinds of languages” · 1 Corinthians 12:30: “Do all speak with various languages?”

**Summary for the gift's page:**

> The translation quoted here says different kinds of languages where many others say tongues: speaking or praying in a language the speaker has never learnt. At Pentecost “everyone heard them speaking in his own language” (Acts 2:6); to Corinth Paul writes of a speaker whom “no one understands” (1 Corinthians 14:2). These statements ask only whether this has happened to you, on your own or in church. Many Christians believe God still gives this gift today; many believe he gave it only for the time of the apostles.

The plain clause after the colon is the plain critic's: neither draft ever said what the gift is, and the headline can print the bare word "tongues". It follows the translation's own word, languages, and is silent on what kind of language, which is where the two sides part.

**Recorded in the early church:**

- **Acts 2:1-13.** When the day of Pentecost had come there was a sound from the sky like the rushing of a mighty wind, tongues like fire appeared and one sat on each of them, and they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak with other languages as the Spirit gave them the ability; Jews from every nation, dwelling in Jerusalem, came together bewildered because everyone heard them speaking in his own language the mighty works of God, and others, mocking, said they were filled with new wine.
- **Acts 10:44-46.** While Peter was still speaking, the Holy Spirit fell on all who heard the word, and the circumcised believers who had come with Peter were amazed that the gift of the Holy Spirit was poured out on the Gentiles also, for they heard them speaking in other languages and magnifying God.
- **Acts 19:1-7.** At Ephesus Paul found certain disciples who said they had not even heard that there is a Holy Spirit and had been baptised into John’s baptism; they were baptised in the name of the Lord Jesus, and when Paul had laid his hands on them the Holy Spirit came on them and they spoke with other languages and prophesied; they were about twelve men in all.
- **1 Corinthians 14:18-19.** Paul thanks God that he speaks with other languages more than all of them, and says that in the church he would rather speak five words with his understanding, to instruct others, than ten thousand words in another language.

The loaded critic found that Drafter B's Acts 2:1-13 sentence trimmed the wind and the fire out of its own range and recommended A's 2:4-13; but A's sentence opens "At Pentecost", which is verse 1. The fix here is the full range with nothing trimmed. Mark 16:17 is not used (Drafter B's reason: the place of the longer ending of Mark is itself disputed).

## 1.6 Interpreting tongues

- **The passages it is named from (verbatim):** 1 Corinthians 12:10: “and to another the interpretation of languages” · 1 Corinthians 12:30: “Do all interpret?”

**Summary for the gift's page:**

> The translation quoted here says the interpretation of languages: giving a church the meaning of what was said in a language it does not know, so that, in Paul’s words, “the assembly may be built up” (1 Corinthians 14:5). Acts never shows anyone doing it; what the New Testament has is Paul’s instructions to Corinth, below. "Acts never shows anyone doing it" was checked: `--grep "interpret" --book Acts` returns two verses, both Luke translating a name (Acts 4:36, 13:8).

**Recorded in the early church** (instruction, not acts, and the summary says so):

# 2. The "others" statement for the six existing gifts that lacked one

My own count from `spiritual-gifts.ts`: seven of the thirteen have one (serving, teaching, encouraging, administration, wisdom, discernment, shepherding) and **six** have none: giving, leading, mercy, faith, evangelism, hospitality. The audit log's "five" was counted before its own fix G8 took leading's away. Both drafters and both critics count the same.

Both drafters chose the same statement to remove in all six cases, and both critics agreed with all six. So do I. In every case the reverse item stays and the gift keeps three. The new statement takes the slot of the one removed, so the spread of reverse items across the rounds does not move.

| giving | 3 | When somebody needs money and I have it, I decide quickly and rarely think about it again. | People tell me when someone they know is going without, and I usually find something to give. | 17 |
| leading | 1 | When nobody will decide, I make the decision for the group. | People have put me in charge of things I did not ask to run. | 14 |

**Why that one went, and where the new one came from:**

- **Giving.** Goes: the only statement in the quiz with two claims (deciding quickly; not thinking about it again), and the second is an inner state. "I give away things I am still using" stays because a teenager and a pensioner can both answer it. Comes in: Drafter B G1 in the loaded critic's form ("going without" for "short of money", which keeps the capacity guard the first edit put on every money item), extended by the editor. The plain critic killed G1 because "people tell a gossip the same thing"; the second half answers that and says what the statement is about, in the shape serving's accepted statement already has ("People ask me ..., and I usually say yes"). The plain critic's pick, "People close to me have said I give away more than I should", is refused: it is about how much the reader gives, by other people's measure, rule 6 names giving, and the gift's own summary says "nothing here counts how much". One limit no wording removes: the reader who gives in secret (Matthew 6:3-4) is told of fewer needs.
- **Leading.** Goes: it is the reverse item ("I would rather be told where we are going than be the one to decide it") turned round, so the gift was asked one question twice, and it is the leading statement nearest the figure quiz's Lead axis ("I start handing out jobs in a group before anyone has asked me to"). "I end up answering for how the whole thing went" stays because it carries the summary's "answering for it". Comes in: Drafter A La, verbatim, both critics' pick. It reports what others did, with a cost in it, and it does not bring back "being consulted before a decision", the act audit finding G8 found three gifts sharing.
- **Showing mercy.** Goes: it reports a perception nobody can check (the "I can tell" family), and the first editor kept it only while no discernment statement read a room. Comes in: Drafter B Me1 with both critics' one change ("turn to me" for "ring me"). "Even people I do not know well" is a second clause, and it earns its place: it is what keeps this off shepherding, which is the same people over years.
- **The gift of faith.** Goes: it measures doggedness at a task, which is temperament and the figure quiz's ground; "project" and "written it off" are office words; and a Pentecostal reader finds the gift drawn as a personality trait. Comes in: Drafter B F2, verbatim, the loaded critic's pick. The critics split (section 7, decision 11).
- **Evangelism.** Goes: it is the reverse item turned round, and "without much trouble" reports a feeling, which audit finding G9 removed from giving. Comes in: Drafter B E1, verbatim, both critics' pick. A housebound reader can answer it. Beside the reverse item it treats the reader who speaks only when asked correctly: they agree with both and net nothing.

**Old statement (slot 3, +1):** I decide something is off about a person before I could say what it is.

**New statement (slot 3, +1, 20 words):** I have warned people about something they were being taught about God, and they later told me I was right.
