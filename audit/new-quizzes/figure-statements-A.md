# Draft A — "Who in the Bible are you most like?" statements

**Angle: everyday life.** Every statement is something a person of any age meets at work, at home
or among friends. Nothing is borrowed from a Bible story, nothing uses church vocabulary, and no
statement names or implies a figure. Thirty-six candidates: six per axis, three keyed each way, so
the editor can pick the best three with both directions present.

Status: draft for the editor. Not wired, not audited, no data file written.

---

## Sign convention (confirmed against the Compass before drafting)

`bipolar.ts` scores a sheet as `raw += item.direction * answer`, then
`score = (raw + span) / (2 * span) * 100`, and `lean()` reads a score **below 50 as toward
`group.left`** and **above 50 as toward `group.right`**. Band index 0 is the far left pole,
index 4 the far right, index 2 the middle band where the instrument names no position.

So, in the Compass data and here:

- **`direction: -1`** — agreeing pulls the score **down, toward the LEFT pole**.
- **`direction: +1`** — agreeing pulls the score **up, toward the RIGHT pole**.

Checked against live data rather than assumed. On axis `grace` (left `Monergist`, right
`Synergist`), "God alone works saving faith in a person; the sinner contributes nothing" is keyed
`-1`, and "A person whom God is drawing to salvation can still finally refuse him" is keyed `+1`.
Agreement with the monergist item moves the reader left. The convention holds.

Axis poles below are the blueprint's, unchanged:

| key | left pole (−1) | right pole (+1) |
|---|---|---|
| pace | Acts first | Weighs first |
| voice | Says it | Holds it |
| lead | Out in front | Alongside |
| conflict | Confronts | Reconciles |
| doubt | Asks why | Takes it on trust |
| plan | Plans ahead | Meets the day |

## Note on the roster, since the statements have to survive it

The roster includes **Jesus**, by the owner's decision, and these statements are written so that
he can be on it safely. That is a drafting constraint, not a footnote: the moment any statement has
an obviously right answer, a quiz with Jesus on the roster stops measuring temperament and starts
scoring holiness, which the blueprint forbids and which would be the "loaded" failure all over
again in a worse place.

So blueprint rule 3 was applied hardest here. Every item below is a real trade-off between two
ordinary strengths: speed against care, candour against restraint, taking charge against making
someone else stronger, confronting against reconciling, verifying against trusting, providing
against staying loose. None of the thirty-six has an end that a decent person must pick, and
because the Gospels record Jesus at **both** ends of most of these axes, no single answer can be
gamed toward him. Nothing here excludes him from the roster; the exclusion risk sits entirely on
the other side of the measurement, in the coordinate work.

## pace — Acts first (−1) ⟷ Weighs first (+1)

| # | dir | words | statement |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | −1 | 16 | When something needs doing, I have usually started before the others have finished talking about it. |
| P2 | −1 | 17 | I buy the thing, book the trip, or send the message while I still feel like it. |
| P3 | −1 | 14 | At work I would rather try something this week than spend the week deciding. |
| P4 | +1 | 11 | I sleep on decisions that other people make on the spot. |
| P5 | +1 | 15 | Before I reply to a difficult message, I leave it sitting for a few hours. |
| P6 | +1 | 15 | I want the price compared and the reviews read before I buy anything that matters. |

Trade-offs (what each end costs):

- **P1** — Starting early gets the thing moving and wastes work when the plan changes; waiting keeps your options and can lose the moment.
- **P2** — Acting on the feeling gets you the trip and sometimes the regret; holding off saves the money and sometimes the chance.
- **P3** — Trying this week buys real information cheaply and risks a public false start; deciding first avoids the false start and spends the week.
- **P4** — Sleeping on it catches the bad decision and can mean the choice is made without you; deciding on the spot keeps you in the room and locks you in.
- **P5** — The pause keeps a sentence you would regret out of the thread; the delay leaves someone waiting who needed an answer today.
- **P6** — Comparing gets the better price and costs the evening; buying straight away costs a little more and gets the thing home.

## voice — Says it (−1) ⟷ Holds it (+1)

| # | dir | words | statement |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | −1 | 15 | If I think the plan is wrong, I say so in the room, not afterwards. |
| V2 | −1 | 13 | My family knows what I think about most things without having to ask. |
| V3 | −1 | 15 | In a group I am usually the one who names the thing nobody is saying. |
| V4 | +1 | 13 | I often leave a conversation with an opinion I never said out loud. |
| V5 | +1 | 13 | People are sometimes surprised to learn what I thought about it all along. |
| V6 | +1 | 15 | I would rather write my thoughts down, or keep them, than say them straight away. |

Trade-offs:

- **V1** — Saying it in the room stops a bad plan early and can cost someone their footing in front of others; saying it later protects them and lets the plan run.
- **V2** — Being known saves everyone guessing and leaves you nowhere to stand back from; being harder to read keeps your own counsel and makes people guess.
- **V3** — Naming it clears the air and can make you the problem; leaving it lets the meeting end and lets the thing go unhandled.
- **V4** — Holding the opinion keeps the room steady and means the room decides without it; voicing it puts it on the table and puts you on the table too.
- **V5** — Thinking it through privately can arrive at something better late; saying it as you go gets it tested early and half-formed.
- **V6** — Writing it makes it clearer and slower; saying it straight away makes it faster and rougher.

## lead — Out in front (−1) ⟷ Alongside (+1)

| # | dir | words | statement |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | −1 | 16 | When a group has no plan, I end up making one and saying who does what. |
| L2 | −1 | 13 | I am comfortable being the one everybody looks at when something goes wrong. |
| L3 | −1 | 10 | I would rather run the meeting than sit in it. |
| L4 | +1 | 16 | I get more out of helping one person do well than out of running the show. |
| L5 | +1 | 13 | At work I am usually the one somebody comes to quietly for help. |
| L6 | +1 | 14 | In a group I wait to see who is leading before I offer anything. |

Trade-offs:

- **L1** — Making the plan ends the drift and takes the choice off everyone else; waiting leaves room for someone else's plan and leaves the group standing about.
- **L2** — Carrying the blame frees the others to work and puts it all on one person; sharing it spreads the weight and blurs who answers for it.
- **L3** — Running it keeps the meeting moving and means you hear less of the room; sitting in it means you hear everything and steer nothing.
- **L4** — One person going further is a slower, deeper return; running the show reaches everyone at once and nobody very far.
- **L5** — Being the one people come to gets the help where it is needed and eats the day; not being that person protects your work and leaves them to find someone else.
- **L6** — Waiting avoids trampling whoever was about to lead and can leave a gap nobody fills; stepping in fills the gap and sometimes takes someone's turn.

## conflict — Confronts (−1) ⟷ Reconciles (+1)

| # | dir | words | statement |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | −1 | 13 | I will raise a problem with someone even when the timing is bad. |
| C2 | −1 | 14 | I would rather have the argument now than keep the peace for another week. |
| C3 | −1 | 16 | When two friends fall out, I tell each of them where I think they are wrong. |
| C4 | +1 | 15 | When two people I know are at odds, I end up carrying messages between them. |
| C5 | +1 | 12 | I will let a small unfairness go to keep a room calm. |
| C6 | +1 | 17 | I look for the sentence both sides can agree to rather than the one that settles it. |

Trade-offs:

- **C1** — Raising it badly timed deals with it while it is small and lands on someone having a hard week; holding it is kinder now and lets it grow.
- **C2** — The argument now costs a bad evening and buys a clear month; the quiet week costs the month and buys the evening.
- **C3** — Telling both sides can end the thing and can lose you both; staying out keeps you both and leaves them apart.
- **C4** — Carrying messages keeps two people talking and can keep them from talking to each other; refusing forces the meeting, or the silence.
- **C5** — Letting it go keeps the room working and lets a pattern set; naming it fixes the pattern and stops the room.
- **C6** — The agreed sentence keeps everyone in and can leave the real question open; the settling sentence answers it and can end the conversation.

## doubt — Asks why (−1) ⟷ Takes it on trust (+1)

| # | dir | words | statement |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | −1 | 13 | I want to know the reason for a rule before I follow it. |
| D2 | −1 | 13 | I check things myself even when someone I trust has already checked them. |
| D3 | −1 | 10 | People have told me I ask one question too many. |
| D4 | +1 | 11 | If someone I trust says it is handled, I stop checking. |
| D5 | +1 | 14 | I can start on a job before I understand the whole reason for it. |
| D6 | +1 | 12 | I would rather get going on someone's word than wait for proof. |

Trade-offs:

- **D1** — Knowing the reason catches the rule that made no sense and holds everything up while you ask; following first gets moving and follows bad rules too.
- **D2** — Checking again catches the miss and tells the other person you did not take their word; not checking honours them and inherits their mistake.
- **D3** — The extra question finds the hole in the plan and can stall a room that had decided; stopping short keeps the room moving with the hole in it.
- **D4** — Stopping frees your attention and makes their error yours; carrying on checking finds it and costs the trust twice.
- **D5** — Starting early gets the work done and can do the wrong work well; waiting to understand does the right work late.
- **D6** — Moving on a word is faster and sometimes wrong; waiting for proof is right and sometimes too late to matter.

## plan — Plans ahead (−1) ⟷ Meets the day (+1)

| # | dir | words | statement |
|---|---|---|---|
| N1 | −1 | 13 | I keep more food, money, or fuel in reserve than this week needs. |
| N2 | −1 | 13 | I pack, book and arrange things weeks before anyone asks me about them. |
| N3 | −1 | 15 | I write the list the night before rather than work it out in the morning. |
| N4 | +1 | 10 | I deal with today's problem today and let tomorrow's arrive. |
| N5 | +1 | 13 | I book things late and sort out the details as they come up. |
| N6 | +1 | 10 | People can drop in on me without wrecking my day. |

Trade-offs:

- **N1** — A reserve absorbs the bad month and ties up what you could have used; running lean keeps it all working and leaves nothing spare.
- **N2** — Arranging early gets the better dates and commits you before you know; arranging late keeps you free and takes what is left.
- **N3** — The night-before list starts the day already moving and fixes it before you know how the day feels; working it out in the morning fits the day and spends the first hour.
- **N4** — Today only keeps you present and lets tomorrow's problem arrive full size; working ahead shrinks tomorrow and spends today on it.
- **N5** — Booking late keeps the plan open and pays the late price; booking early locks it and pays less.
- **N6** — An open day means you are there when someone needs you and the work waits; a closed day finishes the work and misses the person.

## Band adjectives, in the engine's format

Five per axis, index 0 = far left pole, index 4 = far right, index 2 = the middle band where the
instrument names no position. Written to survive `headline()`, which joins up to three of them with
commas and capitalises the first — so each reads as a clause about a person, none flatters, none
belittles, and the middle band says "both" rather than "neither", because on a temperament axis
sitting between is a real answer and not an empty one.

```ts
pace:     ['acts on the moment', 'leans to acting', 'between acting and weighing', 'leans to weighing', 'weighs it through']
voice:    ['says it plainly', 'leans to saying it', 'between saying and holding', 'leans to holding it', 'holds it close']
lead:     ['out in front', 'leans to the front', 'between front and alongside', 'leans to alongside', 'works beside people']
conflict: ['meets it head on', 'leans to confronting', 'between confronting and reconciling', 'leans to reconciling', 'brings the two together']
doubt:    ['needs the reason', 'leans to asking why', 'between asking and trusting', 'leans to trusting', 'takes it on trust']
plan:     ['plans it out', 'leans to planning', 'between planning and improvising', 'leans to meeting the day', 'meets the day as it comes']
```

Reads, as a headline: "Meets it head on, leans to asking why, plans it out."

## Self-check against the blueprint's six rules

1. **Under 22 words, one claim, first person, present tense, about what I DO or what others SAY of me.** All 36 counted; longest is 17 (P2, C6). Every item is an action or a reported observation — D3 and V5 are the "others have said" form. No item asks what the reader believes about themselves in the abstract.
2. **No church or Bible vocabulary.** None anywhere: no church, pray, Bible, serve, calling, ministry. Words checked include "peace" (C2, used of a household week, not as a virtue term) and "trust" (D2, D4, D6, used of a colleague or friend).
3. **No statement answerable only one way by a decent person.** Each has a trade-off line above naming what both ends cost. The ones I would still put to the loaded-hunting critic first: **C5** (letting unfairness go can read as the weak answer), **L2** (taking the blame can read as the admirable one) and **D2** (checking a friend's work can read as the distrustful one). Draft B and the critics should attack those three hardest.
4. **Both directions in every group.** Three and three, all six axes.
5. **No statement points at its own result.** No figure, era, occupation or situation from any Bible narrative appears. The everyday-life angle is the main defence here — nothing about shepherding, sailing, building, kings, prison or wells.
6. **Nothing about devotion.** No item counts praying, reading, giving or attending. N1's "money in reserve" is about provisioning, not giving, and should stay worded that way so it cannot drift into generosity.

## Open flags for the editor

- **C6 and L4 are the two longest "rather than" constructions.** They work, but if a plainer draft B version exists, prefer it; the house voice is short.
- **V2 ("my family knows what I think")** assumes a household. It is fine for most readers, but a reader living alone has to translate. Draft B may have a version that does not.
- **P2 lists three examples** ("buy the thing, book the trip, send the message"). It is one claim with three instances, not three claims, but a strict critic may call it a double. A single-instance version is easy if wanted.
- **N1 lists three nouns** for the same reason, same answer.
- If the editor keeps three per axis, keep at least one "others have said of me" item in the final eighteen if it survives critique — it breaks up eighteen sentences starting with "I".
