# Judith: adversarial verification of the proposed figure entry

Verifies `figure-judith-research.md`. The whole book was read from disk with
`node audit/tools/webbe.mjs` (World English Bible British Edition): chapters 1-7 for the setting,
8-16 closely, and then every cited reference was opened again one at a time. Nothing below was
judged from memory. The book was also searched for timing words, questions, waiting and secrecy to
look for acts the researcher missed.

The standard applied is the one the roster has already been held to: the first verification
(`figures-verification.md`, failure mode REACH) and the five audit reviews closed in `AUDIT-LOG.md`.
Four rulings from that audit decide most of what follows, so they are named here once:

- **Deborah, M5 (applied):** "she sent and called Barak ... shows nothing about instinct versus
  deliberation." Her Pace became "not shown".
- **Jesus, F6 (applied):** John 11:6, staying two days, was removed from Pace as "a deliberate delay,
  not time taken to decide".
- **Deborah, M5 again (applied):** ordering a campaign against an enemy is not "walks toward the
  argument"; the Conflict axis is about a disagreement between people.
- **Reasons, B1 (applied):** right-pole evidence "must be an act of going on without asking, toward
  a person or an instruction, not a statement of faith in God".

## The coordinates, one by one

### Pace: 45 "both ends" becomes 40, shown, on one act

The researcher called this the coordinate they were least sure of, and it is the one that does not
survive. The right-hand item (12:7) falls under the John 11:6 ruling: an interval that is not time
taken to decide. The first left-hand item (8:9-10) falls under the Deborah ruling: a summons with
no timing in it. The research also leans, in its argument though not in the entry, on 9:1 (she put
ashes on her head and prayed before going) as a "fourth act" toward weighing. That is the piety of
a prayer doing the work of temperament evidence, the prayer is that same evening, and the decision
had already been announced. It should not count, and it is not cited.

What is left is real: 8:33. She chose the night. Ozias had given five days; she went out on the
first. Together with "this day" in 8:11 the text puts the oath, her summons and her departure in
one day. One clear act one way sits, on this roster, at 35 (Elijah, Martha) or 65 (Gideon, Hannah).
I have put her at 40, the mildest value that still names a pole, because the single act is an
announcement of timing, because she had "heard all the words" before moving, and because she
prayed, dressed and packed before she left. The band word at 40 is "quick to move", which is what
the verse shows.

**The editor should know this changes what the engine does.** At 45 with acts both ways the axis
was masked; at 40 with one act it counts. If the editor thinks one announcement is too little to
match readers on, the honest fallback is "The text does not show this" at 50, which leaves four
placed axes. What I do not think survives is the both-ends reading, because its right end rests on
waiting, and the audit has already said waiting is not weighing.

### Voice: 35 becomes 40

Two acts of speaking out (8:11 to the rulers; 13:14-15, loud and unprompted, to the people) against
one act of withholding that the text sustains from 8:34 until she comes back through the gate in
chapter 13. The roster's own two-against-one cells sit at 40 or 60, not 35 or 65: Jesus 40,
Esther 60, Mary of Nazareth 60, Nehemiah 55. Her left record is fuller than two items (14:8, 16:1),
which is why this is 40 and not 45.

One thing the editor should see, because the research rejected it and the roster contains the
counter-precedent. Judith's speeches to the watch and to Holofernes (10:12-13, 11:5-19) were
rejected as voice evidence either way, on the ground that calling a speech concealment interprets
its content. But Rahab's Voice-right item, which survived every audit, is exactly that: "She told
the king's messengers the men had gone out at dark" (Joshua 2:4-5). Joseph's Genesis 42:7 is the
same. I have **not** added 10:12-13, for two reasons: 8:34 already says the same thing in her own
words, so it would count one fact twice; and a sentence on her page that turns on her having
deceived someone is the line a Catholic or Orthodox reader is most likely to object to, even though
the book uses the word itself (9:10, 9:13, 13:16). If the editor wants strict consistency with
Rahab, adding it would bring Voice to about 45, both ends, masked, and she would still be placed on
four axes.

### Lead: 10 becomes 15

All three items stand and nothing in the book points the other way. I checked the two candidates:
16:23 (she let her maid go free) and 8:10 (the maid "was over all things that she had"). Neither is
an act of working beside someone who leads; the research is right to leave them out. The pattern
runs through the book: 8:10, 8:33, 10:9, 13:11, 14:1-5, 15:12-13, 16:1.

The value is the reach. 10 would make her the furthest out in front of anyone on the roster, past
David, Nehemiah and Deborah, who all sit at 15: a king, a governor and a judge. Judith holds no
office, and twice the text shows her directing the rulers to give the order rather than giving it
herself: "Command that they open the gate" (10:9) and "You shall set a captain over them" (14:2).
Afterwards "Judith went away to Bethulia, and remained in her own possession" (16:21). "5 should
stay free" is not a reason for 10. 15, level with Deborah, whose record is the nearest in kind.

### Conflict: 10 becomes 25

10 is where Elijah and Nehemiah sit, each on repeated face-to-face confrontations (Carmel and Ahab;
the nobles, the men of Judah, Sanballat's invitations). Judith has one clear one: she had her own
rulers come to her and told them they were wrong (8:11-12). The second item is an act of war done
by stealth, after four days in which she agreed with everything Holofernes said. Under the David
precedent it points left; under the Deborah ruling it points nowhere. I have kept it, because
within this axis's two ends it is certainly not the other one and the book sets her against the
party that wanted terms. But two acts one way, one of them arguable, is 25 on this roster's
convention, not 10. Nothing in the book points right: her deference in the camp is not bringing
two sides together, and the text calls it deceit.

### Reasons: not shown. Agreed, no change

### Plans: 10 stands

Three supported acts from three separate episodes (leaving the city, the night in the tent, the end
of her life), with more on the record uncited (12:6-7 with 13:10 "according to their custom";
14:1-4; 15:11), and nothing the other way. The one candidate for the other pole, that she used
Holofernes's own sword (13:6), needs "she brought no weapon", which the text does not say; the
research is right to reject it as an argument from silence. Nehemiah sits at 10 on two acts and
Joseph at 5 on two; three acts and a pattern across the book carry 10. The rejection of 8:6 (the
fast) matches the audit's ruling on Daniel 6:10: a devotional habit is not provisioning.

## Did the researcher miss an act pointing the other way?

I searched chapters 8-16 for each pole she is not placed on.

- **Pace, right.** Nothing that is time taken to decide. 9:1 and 12:8 are prayers; 12:7 is waiting.
- **Voice, right.** 13:4, "said in her heart", is the same shape as Hannah's 1 Samuel 1:13. Not
  used: she was standing beside a sleeping man, so the silence shows the situation and not her.
  10:12-13 and 11:5-19 are discussed under Voice above.
- **Lead, right.** 16:23 and 8:10, discussed above. Neither holds.
- **Conflict, right.** 10:23 (she bowed to Holofernes) and 12:14 ("Who am I, that I should
  contradict my lord?"). Neither is reconciling. They are the reason Conflict is 25 and not 10.
- **Plans, right.** 13:6 only, rejected above. Also true and not citable: the opening on the fourth
  day was Holofernes's doing (12:10-11), not hers.
- **Reasons, either way.** Nothing.

So nothing was missed that should be cited. Two things were under-weighed: the deference in the
camp (Conflict) and the Rahab precedent (Voice).

## Small things in the research file that are not in the entry

Left here so nobody copies them onto a page later.

- The table row for 12:15 says she "had her servant lay fleeces". The verse says "her servant went
  and laid fleeces"; no instruction from Judith is recorded.
- The row for 13:9-10 says she "put it in the food bag". In 13:10 it is the maid who puts the head
  in the bag ("gave Holofernes' head to her maid; and she put it in her bag of food").
- The row for 12:7 and the entry both say "spring"; the text says "the fountain of water in the camp".

## What the editor needs to know

1. **The Nehemiah collision is gone, and that is a side effect, not the reason.** Measured the way
   the engine measures, the entry as proposed sat 7.1 units from Nehemiah. The corrected entry sits
   46.6 from him; her nearest are David 29.2, Deborah 38.1, Moses and Gideon 39.7. Almost all of
   that comes from Pace becoming a counted axis (she 40, he 75). I ruled on Pace from the two audit
   precedents before measuring anything, and the research is right that a figure may not be placed
   for a distributional reason, so the editor should check the Pace reasoning on its own terms. If
   the editor takes the fallback (Pace not shown), Nehemiah is her nearest again at 21.2, further
   than Paul and Deborah were (17.3), with Gideon at 29.2.
2. **Pace is now her thinnest cell**: one verse, one announcement. It is honest, and it is thin.
3. **8:11 and 8:12 are one speech** serving Voice and Conflict. Two verses, two different claims,
   tolerated elsewhere on the roster (Nehemiah 5:7, Esther 4:16), but the page will show them side
   by side.
4. **Her page must say which Bibles have this book.** Judith is in Catholic and Orthodox Bibles and
   not in Protestant ones. That belongs in the page copy and the draft note, not in this entry. The
   research's points 3 and 4 (the draft note in `bible-figure.ts` that says she is not in the quiz;
   the hardcoded list of women in `engine-test.mjs`) still stand.
5. **The text spells the governor "Ozias".** The entry follows the text.
6. Her position is distinct from every figure on the roster, every "not shown" condition holds
   (one item, null ref, null toward, value 50), and no one-way cell sits inside the 41-59 band, so
   `maskFor()` will not throw.

## Axes honestly placed

**Five: Pace 40, Voice 40, Lead 15, Conflict 25, Plans 10.** Reasons is not shown. The floor is
three. If the editor takes the fallback on Pace she has four; if the editor also applies the Rahab
precedent to Voice she has three (Lead, Conflict, Plans), which still clears the floor, and all
three of those rest on supported acts.
