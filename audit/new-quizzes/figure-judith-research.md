# Judith: the research behind one figure entry

Companion to `figures.verified.json`, in the shape `figures-notes.md` set. The whole book —
sixteen chapters — was read from disk with `node audit/tools/webbe.mjs` (World English Bible
British Edition, the deuterocanon read from `demos/sounds-like-scripture/raw/eng-webbe_vpl.txt`).
Nothing below was written from memory. Every reference in the entry resolves and every quotation
is a verbatim substring of the verses its reference names; both were checked by
`node audit/tools/webbe.mjs --check` on the finished entry (10 of 10 quotations verbatim,
14 of 14 references resolving).

Judith does not appear before 8:1. Chapters 1–7 are Nebuchadnezzar, Holofernes, the campaign, the
siege of Bethulia and the failure of its water — read for the setting, but they contain no act of
hers. The book names the governor **Ozias** (elsewhere spelled Uzziah); this file uses the text's
spelling.

## Every recorded act of Judith's that bears on an axis

Acts of Uzziah/Ozias, the elders, Achior, the maid, Bagoas and Holofernes are excluded, and so are
things said *about* her by other people (13:18–20, 15:9–10, 16:6–10) — those are other men's
assessments, which the verification pass removed elsewhere in this roster (Abigail, 1 Samuel 25:33).

## The argument, axis by axis

### pace 45 — both ends shown. The coordinate I am least sure of

**Left.** 8:9–10 is the first thing she does in the book and there is no interval in it: she hears
what Ozias has sworn and sends her maid to summon the city's three rulers. 8:33 sets the same night
for going out — "You shall all stand at the gate tonight. I will go out with my maid." Hearing,
deciding and departing are one movement in the narrative.

**Right.** 12:7 is a recorded interval of three days inside the enemy camp, with a nightly walk out
into the valley, before the night she acts. One item, but it covers more elapsed time than both
left items together. 9:1 is a fourth act on this axis, also right — she stopped, put ashes on her
head and prayed before going — and it did not fit the three-item cap. The roster's own precedent
counts this shape as pace-right: Esther 4:16, "She called a three-day fast before going in to the
king", carries her 85.

**Why 45 and not 40 or 55.** Two decisions taken with no recorded interval, against one recorded
interval of three days and one recorded pause to pray. Neither end dominates. 45 sits inside the
41–59 band, so the engine will read this axis as **both** and leave it out of matching — which is
the structurally correct statement here, not a dodge: the text really does show her at both ends.
The tilt is left rather than dead centre because the decision *to act at all* is taken at once,
while the waiting is waiting for an opening she did not arrange.

I expect an auditor to push on this one. See "the Nehemiah collision" below before moving it: the
coordinate is load-bearing, which is a reason to state the argument clearly, not a reason to move it.

### voice 35 — says it, with one firm act of holding it

**Left.** 8:11: she summons the rulers to her house and tells them to their faces that what they
said before the people was not right — unasked, unbidden, and to the men who govern the city.
14:8: after the act, she declares to Achior, in the midst of the people, everything she had done
from the day she went out. Also on the record and left, cut for the three-item cap: 13:14–15 (she
said with a loud voice what had been done and showed the head) and 16:1–2 (she began the song in
all Israel).

**Right.** 8:34, and it is not a small item: "I will not tell you until the things are finished that
I will do." The whole operation runs on that refusal, and it holds from chapter 8 to chapter 14.

Three recorded acts of speaking out against one sustained refusal to disclose. 35: clearly left,
with the withholding cited beside it so the page shows the tension rather than hiding it.

### lead 10 — out in front, no act pointing the other way

10:9 has her telling the elders at the gate to command that it be opened. 14:1–2 has her telling
the whole assembly to hang the head on the wall, arm every man, set a captain over them and go out
at daybreak — the city's order of battle, given by a private widow who holds no office, and done.
15:13 has her going before all the people in the dance, leading the women, with the men following
in armour. 8:10 (summoning the city's three rulers to her house) is a fourth, and is cited under
pace rather than here so that no verse carries two axes.

I looked for an act at the other end and found none that survives. 10 rather than 5 only because
5 should stay free for a figure whose whole record is command.

### conflict 10 — confronts, no act pointing the other way

8:12: she puts it to the rulers that they have tested God and stand in the place of God among men.
13:6–8: she takes Holofernes' sword down from the bedpost at his head and strikes twice upon his
neck. Those are the two ends of the same disposition — she walks at the disagreement, whether it
is her own governors or the general besieging her city.

**Disclosure:** 8:11 and 8:12 are two verses of one speech, used for two different things — that
she spoke up unbidden (voice) and what she charged them with (conflict). The verification pass
flagged exactly this pattern for Abraham at Genesis 18:23–25. It is two distinct verses and two
distinct claims, but an editor should know it is one speech.

### doubt (`doubt`, published as "Reasons") — the text does not show this. 50, one null item

This is the honest answer and I looked hard for a way around it. Judith asks no one for a reason,
asks for no sign, and is never recorded requiring anything before going on. The tempting item is
her speech in 8:12–17, where she argues against the elders' five-day test of God — but that is the
*content* of a speech, which the blueprint rules out, and reading it as "she needed no reason" is
an argument from silence, the pattern the verification pass removed five times elsewhere. 12:8
("she implored the Lord God of Israel to direct her way") is asking for direction, not asking why.

The cell takes the structural form the notes require: exactly one item, `ref: null`,
`toward: null`, `did: "The text does not show this."`, value 50.

### plan 10 — plans ahead, on three separate episodes

10:5: she gives her maid wine and oil, fills a bag with roasted grain, figs and bread, and packs
all her vessels — she leaves the city provisioned, and 12:2 shows her refusing the enemy's food
because of it. 13:3: she had told her maid beforehand to stand outside the bedchamber and wait for
her, as she did daily, and told Bagoas the same — the exit was arranged before the act. 16:24: she
distributed her goods before she died, which shows the same habit outside the crisis and away from
the plot.

Three further items on this axis were left out for the cap: 12:7 (the nightly routine established
over three days, which later carries the escape — cited under pace instead), 14:1–2 (the morning's
order of battle, cited under lead), and 10:3–4 (the dressing). Nothing in the book points the other
way; see the rejected items for the one candidate.

## Considered and rejected, with the reason

- **Her speeches to the Assyrian watch (10:12–13) and to Holofernes (11:5–19), as voice-left.**
  She speaks readily and at length to strangers and to the enemy commander. But what she says is
  designed to conceal her purpose, so counting it as "says the thought aloud" would be reading the
  act backwards. Rejected: the axis would be measuring the opposite of what happened.
- **The same speeches as voice-right ("acts of concealment").** Equally rejected, and for the
  sharper reason: calling a speech an act of concealment is an interpretation of its content. Voice
  stands on 8:34, which says it in her own recorded words.
- **8:6 (fasting all the days of her widowhood except the feasts) as plan-left.** A fixed regimen
  stated in the verse, and there is a precedent for keeping one: Daniel 6:10 was kept for his plan
  coordinate. Rejected here because plan already rests on three stronger acts of actual
  provisioning, and because taking a devotional practice as temperament evidence when it is not
  needed invites an objection for nothing.
- **13:6 (she took his own sword from the bedpost) as plan-right, "she improvised".** The act is
  real and it is cited — under conflict, for what it plainly is. Reading it as improvisation
  requires "she brought no weapon", which the text never says. Argument from silence; rejected.
  This is the only candidate anywhere in the book for a plan-right item, and it does not hold.
- **8:12–17 as doubt-right.** Content of a speech, plus an argument from silence. Rejected; see
  the doubt section.
- **13:18–20, 15:9–10 and 16:6–10 (Ozias, the high priest and the elders, and the song) as evidence
  for anything.** Other people's assessments of her, and largely virtue judgements. The
  verification pass removed exactly this kind of item from Abigail (1 Samuel 25:33). Rejected.
- **16:23 (she let her maid go free) as lead-right.** One act at the end of a life, and reading a
  manumission as "makes someone else stronger" is a stretch. Rejected.
- **8:33 ("I will go out with my maid") as lead-right.** The maid acts throughout under her
  direction (13:3: "Judith had said to her servant that she should stand outside her bedchamber").
  Directing a servant is not working alongside. Rejected.
- **12:2 (refusing Holofernes' food) as conflict-left.** A real refusal delivered in his own tent,
  but a mild one, and conflict already stands on two firm items. Left out rather than rejected;
  an editor who wants a third could use it.
- **The `who` line.** Written as a plain identifier in the style of the others ("cupbearer to
  Artaxerxes, then governor…", "wife of Nabal of Carmel, later wife of David"): *widow of Manasses
  of Bethulia, who went with her maid into the camp of Holofernes.* 8:2–3 gives the husband and the
  city, 10:17 and 12:7 the camp. No virtue word, and nothing about what the act accomplished.

## What the editor needs to know before this ships

1. **The Nehemiah collision, and it is the important one.** On the three axes where both are placed
   — lead, conflict, plan — Judith (10, 10, 10) and Nehemiah (15, 10, 10) are nearly the same
   person. Measured the way the engine measures, they sit **7.1 units apart**, against a roster
   whose closest pair before this was Paul/Deborah at 17.3 and a tie rule of 1 unit. They will swap
   constantly. The two axes that would separate them — pace (Judith 45, Nehemiah 75) and voice
   (Judith 35, Nehemiah 55) — are exactly the two that get masked out, hers by both-ends and his by
   the no-pole band. I checked what moving her pace does: at 60 or 65 Nehemiah is still nearest; only
   placing her at 30–40 separates them, and then David becomes nearest at ~23. **I did not move the
   coordinate to fix this**, because placing a figure for a distributional reason is the thing this
   quiz is not allowed to do. It is the editor's call, and it should be made in the open.
2. **She reinforces the crowded side of every axis she is placed on** — voice-left, lead-left,
   conflict-left, plan-left. She adds nothing to the thin right poles the notes worried about
   (conflict-right has 4 figures; plan-right has 3 at the 65 threshold; doubt-right is down to 2
   after the calibration pass). If Tobit can honestly carry a right pole, that is where the roster
   needs him.
3. **The draft note in `site/src/lib/quizzes/bible-figure.ts` currently says she is not in the
   quiz**: "Judith, Tobit, the Maccabees and others from the books Catholic and Orthodox Bibles
   also contain are not in it yet; that is a limit of the draft, not a judgement about those
   books." That sentence has to be rewritten when this lands, and it should say plainly which books
   the roster now draws on and that they are not in Protestant Bibles.
4. **`site/scripts/engine-test.mjs` counts the women by a hardcoded list of names** (line ~643).
   "Judith" must be added to it or the nine-women floor silently loses her.
5. Her position tuple is distinct from every existing figure's, so the duplicate-position assertion
   in the same test still passes. The roster becomes 24 with her, 25 with Tobit.
6. The engine's `minShownAxes` is 3. Judith is placed on four (voice, lead, conflict, plan), with
   pace masked as both and doubt as none, so she clears the floor without needing the borderline
   axis.
