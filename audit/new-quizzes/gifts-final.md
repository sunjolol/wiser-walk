# What are your spiritual gifts? The whole instrument on one page

Status: **draft, unaudited, not registered.** Data file: `site/src/lib/quizzes/spiritual-gifts.ts`. Everything from "The copy around it" down to the running order was generated from that file, so the wording here is the wording a reader will meet.

Thirteen gifts, three statements each, 39 statements, about 5 minutes. Every gift has statements keyed in both directions. Longest statement: 19 words. Every quotation is verbatim from the World English Bible British Edition on disk and was machine-checked as a substring of it.

Direction: **+1** means agreeing raises that gift; **-1** means agreeing lowers it. Signs were re-derived from the final wording.

## The copy around it

- **Title:** What are your spiritual gifts?
- **Tagline** (10 words): Thirty-nine statements, thirteen gifts, and a conversation to have afterwards.
- **Description** (39 words): Thirteen gifts the New Testament lists, each in its passage’s own words. Thirty-nine plain statements about what you do and what people bring you. Scripture gives no test for gifts, so check the result with people who know you.
- **Share title:** Where my answers pointed: spiritual gifts
- **Minutes:** 5 · **Code prefix:** SG · **Radix:** 13 · **Naming floor:** 17 · **Tie steps:** 1

### Draft note (top of the quiz)

> This one is an unaudited draft. The thirteen gifts and the passages that list them are quoted from the World English Bible British Edition, but these statements have not been through the adversarial fairness audit the Theology Compass went through, and nobody has played them yet. Still open, and the owner’s to decide: whether and how to include prophecy, healing, miracles, tongues and their interpretation. They are left out of this draft for now, which says nothing about whether they are lesser or have ceased. Treat the result as a conversation starter, not a verdict.

### The honest frame: intro (94 words)

> Scripture gives lists of gifts. It gives no test for them. The lists say each is given “for the profit of all” (1 Corinthians 12:7), and gifts are recognised in service, by other people, over time. So this is a conversation starter, not a verdict: thirty-nine statements cannot see your last ten years. If you know the lists you will sometimes see which gift a statement is about, which is one more reason to take the result to two or three people who have watched you serve and ask them whether it is true.

One line for above the start button on a phone, if the full intro sits below it:

> Scripture lists gifts and gives no test for them; other people usually name yours before you do.

### The honest frame: result (82 words)

> This is where your answers pointed. It is not a measurement of what God has given you. Nobody is only one of these, the list here is not everything Scripture names, and a self-report can be wrong in both directions, about what you avoid as much as what you are good at. The useful step is not to believe this page. It is to send it to somebody who has served alongside you and ask whether it matches what they have seen.

### What a low row means (prints wherever a gift shows below the line)

> “As each has received a gift, employ it in serving one another” (1 Peter 4:10). A gift low on this page is a pattern these statements did not find, not a verdict on you. And some of these are asked of everyone: “Be hospitable to one another without grumbling” (1 Peter 4:9) comes one verse earlier.

### When nothing stands out (replaces the strategy’s flat-state line)

> “As each has received a gift, employ it in serving one another” (1 Peter 4:10). Thirty-nine statements found no pattern standing out. That is a fact about the statements, not about you; ask the people who have watched you serve.

### Headline frame

> Your answers pointed most to: *showing mercy*

So the largest type on the page is not a bare one-word verdict.

### What is not in this draft

> Prophecy, healing, miracles, tongues and their interpretation are not in this draft. (The translation quoted here says “prophecy”, “gifts of healings”, “workings of miracles”, “different kinds of languages” and “the interpretation of languages”: 1 Corinthians 12:9-10.) That is not because they are lesser or have stopped. Christians reading the same passages disagree about whether and how they are given today, and a set of statements about yourself could not measure them fairly either way.

> The Theology Compass sets that disagreement out in both sides’ own words. *(links to the Compass’s Gifts axis)*

**Owner: this is your open decision.** Whether and how to include prophecy, healing, miracles, tongues and their interpretation has not been decided. The copy above deliberately does not say they belong to another era, does not say they belong to another kind of Christian, and does not promise they will be added.

**Recorded in the early church:**

- **Romans 16:1-2.** Paul commends Phoebe, a servant of the assembly at Cenchreae, and asks the Romans to assist her because she has been a helper of many, himself included.

## Editor's decisions

### Critic suggestions applied

- **Draft B is the base, Draft A is mined** (loaded critic). 28 of 39 lines are B’s or a critic’s trim of B’s; 8 are A’s; 3 are new.
- **"I can tell" asserts being right; "I decide" reports the act** (loaded critic). Applied to the discernment item.
- **A capacity guard on every money item** (loaded critic). All three giving items can be answered by someone with no income.
- **No pronoun may point outside its own sentence** (plain critic). B evangelism-3, B evangelism-5, B faith-2 and A G-EVG-5 were dropped; A G-FTH-3 was reworded.
- **No double claims** (plain critic). A G-ADM-4 trimmed; B discernment-1, B leading-3, B giving-5 and A G-GIV-5 not used.
- **At least one serving item that is not manual labour, and one hospitality item that needs no front door** (plain critic). Serving's third line is new for this reason; "I add a seat" is kept for the second.
- **Drain reports ("wears me out", "leaves me drained") never as a gift's only reverse item** (loaded critic). In the final set no reverse item is a drain report at all, so a tired or ill reader is not pushed flat across the board.
- **The mercy / shepherding collision** (both critics). "Keeps in touch with the person everyone stopped calling" is gone from mercy; mercy is carried by presence in acute distress, shepherding by the same people over years.
- **Citation corrections** (loaded critic): leading now cites Acts 15:13-29 so the letter is inside the range; shepherding carries "with tears"; Acts 16:16-18 is cut from discernment (the text says Paul was "greatly annoyed", not that he perceived anything).
- **"Whether and how"** in the sentence about the gifts left out (loaded critic). "How they are given today" alone presupposed that they are, which is one side's position. The translation's own words ("different kinds of languages", "gifts of healings") are printed beside the familiar ones.
- **A sentence for a low result, flat-state copy from 1 Peter 4:10, and a headline frame** (loaded critic). All three are in `giftsFrame`.
- **Rule 5 conceded on the page** (both drafters, loaded critic): the intro says a reader who knows the lists will sometimes see which gift a statement is about.

### Critic suggestions rejected, with the reason

- **Loaded critic: keep B serving-3 ("rather be handed a job than asked what I think").** Rejected; the plain critic is right that it measures deference, not serving, and it drags leading and wisdom down in the reader's head.
- **Loaded critic: B giving-5 ("I decide in advance what I can give, and I hold to that") as giving's reverse.** Rejected. 2 Corinthians 9:7 on disk reads "Let each man give according as he has determined in his heart", so keying decided-in-advance giving as the absence of the gift would set the quiz against a text it sits beside.
- **Loaded critic: B leading-3 ("answerable for the whole thing than do one part well").** Rejected; the plain critic is right that these are not opposites. The new line keeps its content without the false choice.
- **Loaded critic: B wisdom-3 ("I hold back my view until I have heard the whole story") and A leading-5 ("I wait to hear where most people are heading").** Rejected on the critic's own finding 3.4: both measure what the figure quiz measures as temperament (weighs first, holds it; its draft has "In a group I wait to see who is leading before I offer anything"). The same behaviour cannot be temperament in one quiz and a gift in the other.
- **Loaded critic: B wisdom-4 ("I tell them I am the wrong person to ask").** Rejected; the plain critic is right that someone who is simply never asked will disagree, and disagreeing with a reverse item RAISES wisdom.
- **Loaded critic: B faith-3 ("without a back-up plan more often than most people I know").** Rejected as the figure quiz's plan axis, and it asks the reader to rank themselves against other people.
- **Plain critic: kill A G-WIS-2.** Rejected in its rewritten form; the objection was that it duplicated A G-DSC-3, so G-DSC-3 was dropped instead and the act belongs to one gift only.
- **Plain critic: B mercy-5 and B serving-5 (drain reports) as the reverse items.** Rejected under the loaded critic's rule above; behaviours were available for both.
- **Plain critic: B giving-4 as written ("easier to give my time than money").** Kept only with a guard; unguarded, a reader with no money agrees and is marked down for being poor, against the quiz's own proof text (the Macedonians gave out of "deep poverty").
- **Plain critic: A G-GIV-1 ("I move money before I think it through").** Rejected; no capacity guard, and B giving-1 covers the same act with one.
- **Plain critic: ship 26 statements, or cut to ten gifts.** Not applied. The blueprint fixes three per gift and the list of gifts, and at two per gift the scale has nine rungs across thirteen categories, so most results would be ties. It is a fair worry and it is the owner's call: see below.

### Decisions that are the owner's

1. **The gifts left out.** Whether and how prophecy, healing, miracles, tongues and their interpretation come in is open and is yours. The draft note says so to every reader.
2. **Discernment and faith do not fully measure what their passages name.** 1 Corinthians 12:10 says "discerning of spirits"; the statements can see only everyday shrewdness about people and offers. 1 Corinthians 12:9's faith is not saving faith and not mere risk-taking. Each summary says this in one sentence. The alternatives are to rename them or to drop them; both critics raised it and neither could settle it.
3. **Thirty-nine statements on a phone.** The plain critic expects people to tire around statement 25. If play shows that, the first cut is the list of gifts, not the items per gift.
4. **A naming clash.** The Compass already has an axis at `/axis/theology-compass/gifts/`. A quiz called "spiritual gifts" will read as the same thing to a reader and to a search engine.
5. **Overlap with the figure quiz.** "I commit to things before I can see how they will be paid for" is close to that quiz's plan axis. It is the best single line for this gift, so it stays for now; compare the two instruments side by side before either goes live.

### For the engineer

- The quiz is **not registered**. `giftSources`, `giftActs` and `giftsFrame` are exported beside it because the existing group slots render under Compass headings ("How the argument unfolded", "Passages both sides argue from", BSB text).
- `category.ts` words are vice words: "leaned away", "a strong pull", "Strongest pull: ...". This quiz needs its own, and a share text that shows the top three only and never the bottom of thirteen.
- `namingFloor` is 17, not the sins quiz's 10: with two forward items and one reverse, a reader who agrees with everything scores 67 on all thirteen, and at 10 would be handed two gifts chosen by the alphabet.
- `tieMargin()` in `category.ts` computes `Math.max(1, tieSteps)`, so the sins quiz's `tieSteps: 0` ("only an exact equality is a tie") is silently treated as 1. This quiz sets 1 on purpose and is unaffected; the sins quiz is not behaving as its comment says.
- With thirteen categories, three-way ties at the top will happen; the tie state names only two.
- The Compass cross-link must go through `groupHref()`: the axis slug is `gifts`, its key is `spirit`.
- The blueprint says gifts are given "for the common good"; the translation on disk says "for the profit of all" (1 Corinthians 12:7). The page quotes the disk.
