# Audit: the cessationist lens (confessional Reformed / conservative Baptist)

Reviewer's position: the sign and revelatory gifts served the apostolic age (WCF 1.1, "those former
ways of God's revealing his will unto his people being now ceased"). Every verse quoted below was
read in the World English Bible British Edition on disk (src "webbe"). Nothing edited. Findings
only; what is fine is not listed.

## Spiritual gifts quiz

### G1. BLOCKER-ADJACENT MAJOR: the line between "in" and "left out" runs through the middle of 1 Corinthians 12:8-10, and that line is itself a side
Target: gifts `wisdom`, `discernment`, `faith`; `giftsFrame.notIncluded`.
1 Corinthians 12:8-10 is one list of nine. The draft takes three (word of wisdom, faith, discerning
of spirits) as patterns of ordinary life and leaves five out as disputed. Many cessationists
(Gaffin, O. Palmer Robertson) count the word of wisdom, the faith of 12:9 (read with 13:2, "all
faith, so as to remove mountains") and the discerning of spirits among the extraordinary gifts;
classical Pentecostals count the same three as supernatural manifestations, not shrewdness, advice
or risk appetite. So the split offends both ends at once, and a reader is told "Your answers
pointed most to: discernment" over 1 Corinthians 12:10. The summaries half-concede this; the
headline and the share text do not.
Fix: treat 1 Corinthians 12:8-10 as one block and move wisdom, discernment and faith into the
"not in this draft" sentence (ten gifts remain: Romans 12, "helps" and "governments", Ephesians
4:11, 1 Peter 4). If the owner keeps them, the names must change on every surface to what is
measured ("being asked for advice", "weighing people and offers", "going ahead early") and the
1 Corinthians verses must leave the headline position.

### G2. MAJOR: "That is not because they are lesser or have stopped."
Target: `giftsFrame.notIncluded`, third sentence.
Read quickly, or screenshotted, this says the site holds that they have not stopped. It also rules
on rank, where Paul himself ranks: "first apostles, second prophets, third teachers" (1 Corinthians
12:28), "he is greater who prophesies than he who speaks with other languages" (14:5). The draft
note already has the neutral construction; the page sentence does not.
Fix: "Leaving them out says nothing about their rank, and nothing about whether they are given
today. Christians reading the same passages disagree about whether and how they are, and a set of
statements about yourself could not measure them fairly either way." This is equally safe for a
Pentecostal reader.

### G3. MAJOR: the left-out list is incomplete, and the quotations are cut from verses that name the rest
Target: `giftsFrame.notIncluded`; `giftSources.evangelism`, `giftSources.shepherding`, `giftSources.wisdom`.
The page names five omissions. It silently drops "the word of knowledge" (the other half of
1 Corinthians 12:8, the verse quoted for wisdom) and "apostles" and "prophets" (Ephesians 4:11:
"He gave some to be apostles; and some, prophets; and some, evangelists; and some, shepherds and
teachers", from which the draft quotes only the last two clauses; also 1 Corinthians 12:28). A
reader who opens the verse finds gifts the page never mentions. For my side apostleship is the
plainest case, since nearly every tradition agrees it is not given today in the sense of the Twelve.
Fix: "Apostles, prophets and prophecy, the word of knowledge, healing, miracles, tongues and their
interpretation are not in this draft", with Ephesians 4:11 and 1 Corinthians 12:8, 28 added to the
references.

### G4. MAJOR: the gift of faith is scored as financial and practical risk-taking, against Luke 14:28
Target: statements 10 and 15; `PLAIN.faith`.
"I commit to things before I can see how they will be paid for or finished" is "stepping out in
faith" in plain clothes, which is faith-mission and charismatic usage, not 1 Corinthians 12:9.
Luke 14:28 on disk: "which of you, desiring to build a tower, doesn't first sit down and count the
cost, to see if he has enough to complete it?" The editor refused a giving item because it was
keyed against 2 Corinthians 9:7; by that rule statement 10 cannot stand, and statement 15 scores
naming risk as the absence of a gift. The definition "a habit of going ahead before the outcome is
secured" is the site's own and is not citable. The cited act cuts the other way too: in Acts
27:23-25 Paul believes a specific word an angel had spoken to him ("it will be just as it has been
spoken to me"), which is not going ahead without knowing.
Fix: best, G1. Otherwise: 10 -> "I stay sure a thing will come right long after the people around
me have stopped expecting it." (+1, 18 words); 15 -> "When a plan looks shaky, I am among the first
to say we should drop it." (-1, 16 words); summary -> "these statements look only for a habit of
staying sure when others have stopped", and the Acts 27 sentence must say that Paul had been told
by an angel.

### G5. MAJOR: discernment is modelled as a hunch about a person, and its reverse item is keyed against 1 Corinthians 13:7
Target: statements 9 and 35; `giftActs.discernment` (Acts 5:1-4).
(a) Statement 35, "I decide something is off about a person before I could say what it is", scores
a pre-rational impression as the discerning of spirits. That is the intuitive model of the gift.
The texts that use the idea are about testing: "test the spirits, whether they are of God"
(1 John 4:1); "examining the Scriptures daily to see whether these things were so" (Acts 17:11).
(b) Statement 9 keys "assume the best of somebody" as the gift's absence, one chapter before love
"believes all things" (1 Corinthians 13:7). Same fault the editor caught with 2 Corinthians 9:7.
(c) Acts 5:1-4 never says how Peter knew; Pentecostal teaching files that passage under the word of
knowledge. Filing it under discernment is a connection the site made.
Fix: 35 -> "When something sounds right to everyone else, I am the one who goes and checks it."
(+1, 16 words). 9 -> "I take a new idea as it sounds rather than look into where it came from."
(-1, 16 words). Drop Acts 5:1-4; keep Acts 8:18-23 ("I see that you are in the poison of
bitterness") and add Acts 17:11 as a recorded act of testing, or say that the New Testament names
no act as this gift.

### G6. MAJOR: shepherding quotes an office and cites two charges to elders, then hands the result to anyone
Target: gift `shepherding`, summary and acts.
Ephesians 4:11 names people Christ gave to the church ("some to be ... shepherds and teachers");
the confessional reading is one office, pastor-teacher. Both cited acts (Acts 20:28-31, 1 Peter
5:1-3) are addressed to elders. "Your answers pointed most to: shepherding. Many translations say
pastors" reads as a pointer toward an office that my churches fill by qualification and call
(1 Timothy 3), not by self-report; churches that ordain differently have their own objection to a
quiz doing it.
Fix: add to the summary: "Ephesians 4:11 names people given to the church, and both passages below
are addressed to elders. This page describes a pattern of care and says nothing about who should
hold an office."

### G7. MINOR: "gifts are recognised in service, by other people, over time" is printed as if the lists said it
Target: `giftsFrame.intro`, third sentence; `introShort` ("other people usually name yours before you do").
The sentence runs "The lists say each is given 'for the profit of all' ..., and gifts are
recognised ...", so the second clause borrows the first clause's authority. It is a sound
inference, but it is uncited, and `introShort` is an empirical claim with no source at all.
Fix: "In Acts the church recognises people for work it has watched them do (Acts 6:3; 16:2)."
Replace `introShort` with "Scripture lists gifts and gives no test for them; ask the people who
have watched you serve."

### G8. MINOR: the frame never says who distributes the gifts
Target: `giftsFrame.intro` / `result`.
The chapter being quoted says "the one and the same Spirit produces all of these, distributing to
each one separately as he desires" (1 Corinthians 12:11). A page headed "What are your spiritual
gifts?" that never says this lets gifts read as personality. One verbatim clause fixes it and no
tradition objects to it.
Fix: after "It gives no test for them." add: "It says the Spirit gives them, 'distributing to each
one separately as he desires' (1 Corinthians 12:11)."

### G9. MINOR: "Nobody is only one of these"
Target: `giftsFrame.result`, third sentence. An uncited doctrinal claim (1 Peter 4:10 says "a gift").
Fix: "No one is summed up by one row of this page".

### G10. MINOR: exhorting has been turned into motivational coaching
Target: statement 31, "I tell people what I think they are capable of, even when they have not asked."
Romans 12:8 on disk says "exhorts", and the cited act is Barnabas exhorting them "to remain near to
the Lord". "What you are capable of" is self-esteem vocabulary.
Fix: "I urge people to stick with what they know is right, even when they have not asked."
(+1, 17 words; the unasked-for push is still the cost.)

## Figure quiz

### F1. BLOCKER: moderate answers name Jesus about one time in six
Target: `bibleFigure.config` (tie 10, hedge 45, centre 10) with Jesus at 55,40,50,50,70,40; editor's note 2.
By the editor's own simulation a reader who commits to nothing is told he is nearest Jesus 16% of
the time, and nearest the Lord's mother 17%. "I am most like Jesus" as the reward for lukewarm
answers is the one screenshot this quiz cannot survive, and the draft note admitting it does not
cure it. This does not reopen the owner's decision to include him.
Fix: before any figure is named, require the reader to be outside the middle band on at least
three axes; otherwise name nobody ("Your answers were too even to place"). Apply it to every
figure, so Jesus is handled by the same rule as the rest. Re-run the simulation and publish only
if Jesus is named no more often than the median figure.

### F2. BLOCKER: Mark 6:38-41 is cited as Jesus "meeting the day"; John 6:6 says the opposite
Target: Jesus, plan axis, third evidence item (toward right).
The parallel account on disk: "He said this to test him, for he himself knew what he would do"
(John 6:6). The Gospels expressly deny that the feeding was improvised, so the item makes Scripture
say what Scripture rules out.
Fix: delete the item. With only Mark 14:13-16 and Luke 22:8 left, the evidence points one way; let
the verifier re-argue the coordinate, or mark the right side as not shown. Do not substitute a
saying (Matthew 8:20) for an act.

### F3. BLOCKER: Gethsemane and the arrest filed under "Takes it on trust: go on and let the reason come later"
Target: Jesus, axis `doubt`/Reasons (70), items Matthew 26:42 and John 18:11; also Luke 2:46.
The text says he knew the reason: "knowing all the things that were happening to him" (John 18:4);
"I came to this time for this cause" (John 12:27); "How then would the Scriptures be fulfilled?"
(Matthew 26:54). The axis summary for that pole ends "now and then it is the wrong work", and his
rail carries the band "slow to question". That is obedience with full knowledge described as
proceeding without a reason. Luke 2:46 is half a sentence: verse 47 adds that all were "amazed at
his understanding and his answers".
Fix: Jesus is "not shown" on Reasons, with one sentence on his page: "The Gospels record him as
knowing what was coming (John 18:4), so this axis is not applied to him."

### F4. BLOCKER: the Reasons axis treats faith in God as a temperament and prints "Neither is the better way to be" beside John 20:25
Target: axis `doubt`/Reasons summary; evidence Genesis 15:6 (Abraham), John 20:25 (Thomas), John 11:27 (Martha), Joshua 2:9-11 (Rahab), Luke 1:38 (Mary), Judges 4:14 (Deborah), Daniel 6:10.
Readers reach the right pole by agreeing "I go along with things without checking them first";
figures reach it by believing God. Genesis 15:6, "He believed in the LORD, who credited it to him
for righteousness", is the justification text of Romans 4, not a disposition to skip checking;
Scripture commends checking (Acts 17:11). On the other side, Thomas's refusal sits under "Neither
is the better way to be", where the Lord says "Don't be unbelieving, but believing" and "Blessed
are those who have not seen and have believed" (John 20:27, 29). The site is contradicting the
passage it cites.
Fix: (1) restrict the evidence on this axis to how a person handled instructions, reports and
plans from other people (Ruth 3:5, Nehemiah 1:2, Esther 4:11, Matthew 18:21 survive); remove
Genesis 15:6, John 11:27, Joshua 2:9-11, Daniel 6:10 and Judges 4:14 (a prophetess delivering a
word, not a temperament), and re-argue those coordinates. (2) Add to the axis summary and to every
figure page: "This axis is about how someone handles instructions and reports. It is not about
faith in God, which Scripture never treats as a matter of temperament." (3) On Thomas's page print
John 20:27-29 beside 20:25.

### F5. MAJOR: Luke 6:12 has had the prayer taken out
Target: Jesus, pace axis, second item.
Evidence sentence: "He was on the mountain all night before he called his disciples". Text: "he
went out to the mountain to pray, and he continued all night in prayer to God." The blueprint's
own example says he "prays all night". Removing the prayer from a sentence about the Lord in order
to fit a temperament scale is treating Scripture loosely.
Fix: "He went out to the mountain to pray and continued all night in prayer to God; when it was
day he called his disciples and chose twelve."

### F6. MAJOR: John 11:6 and Mark 1:41 are read as temperament where the text gives the reason
Target: Jesus, pace axis, items one and three; the pole summaries printed on his page.
John 11:4 and 11:15 say why he stayed ("for the glory of God"; "so that you may believe"); it was
not time taken to think it through. Mark 1:41 begins "Being moved with compassion", which the
evidence sentence omits while the pole is defined as moving "on instinct". Both pole summaries name
a failing ("sometimes means starting again", "sometimes misses the moment"), and those sentences
are drawn on his rails.
Fix: drop John 11:6 or quote 11:4 with it; restore "moved with compassion" to Mark 1:41. On Jesus's
page print the acts and the both-ends sentence only: no band adjective and no pole-cost summary
against his name.

### F7. MAJOR: Luke 23:34 as a conflict style
Target: Jesus, conflict axis, second item (toward "Reconciles").
The prayer from the cross is filed as "works to bring the two sides together". That reduces his
intercession for his executioners to a manner of handling arguments.
Fix: replace with Luke 22:51 ("he touched his ear and healed him", in the middle of the arrest), an
act in a conflict. Keep John 21:15-17.

### F8. MAJOR: the share line turns any result into a boast, and Jesus's into an irreverent one
Target: `shareTitle` "Who in the Bible I am most like", and the title's promise.
Pasted into a chat it reads "Who in the Bible I am most like: Jesus"; the accompanying note cannot
travel with a text message. The gifts quiz already solved this with a headline frame.
Fix: shareTitle "Whose recorded acts my answers sat nearest"; result lead "Your answers sat nearest
the recorded acts of". If a plain-text share exists, it must carry `JESUS_NOTE` when the figure is
Jesus, or not be offered for him.

### F9. MINOR: "temperament as the Gospels record it"
Target: `JESUS_NOTE`.
The Gospels record acts; the temperament is the site's construction. As written, the sentence
credits the Evangelists with the site's axes.
Fix: "Landing nearest Jesus here is about where a few of his recorded acts fall on these six axes,
not a measure of holiness; every Christian is called to be like him in character, whatever their
temperament."

### F10. MINOR: recorded sins and rebuked acts sit under "Neither is the better way to be" with no sign of what the text says next
Target: Moses, Exodus 4:13 (pace, "Weighs first"); Peter, John 18:10 and Matthew 16:22; Abraham, Genesis 17:17.
Exodus 4:13 is a refusal, not a weighing, and the next verse reads "The LORD's anger burnt against
Moses". Peter's sword and his rebuke of Jesus are both answered by a rebuke from the Lord. The
figure pages print the act and stop.
Fix: remove Exodus 4:13 from pace (Exodus 3:11 and 4:1 already carry his objections on other
axes), and add one line to every figure page: "An act listed here is evidence of how this person
moved, not approval of it; read the passage for how it was received."

### F11. MINOR: loose fits between act and axis
- David, voice, 2 Samuel 6:14: dancing "with all his might" is not saying a thought aloud. Remove;
  1 Samuel 17:32 and 2 Samuel 1:17 carry the coordinate.
- Daniel, plan, Daniel 6:10: set times of prayer used as evidence of planning. It counts a
  devotional practice, which blueprint rule 6 forbids on the reader's side. Remove; Daniel 1:8 stays.
- John the Baptist, plan 80: rests on his diet and clothing, and Matthew 3:4 and Mark 1:6 are the
  same fact counted twice. The text does not show how he planned. Mark as not shown (50).
- Paul, Reasons, 2 Corinthians 12:8: "I begged the Lord three times that it might depart from me"
  is a petition, not a request for the reason. Remove.
