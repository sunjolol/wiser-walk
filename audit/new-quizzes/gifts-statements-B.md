# Draft B — "What are your spiritual gifts?" statements

**Angle: what others come to me for, and what I find draining.** People recognise a gift in each
other before they see it in themselves, and the absence of one shows up as the thing somebody
quietly avoids. So every forward-keyed statement here is either a thing the reader *does* without
being asked or a thing *others have said or done* about them ("people text me", "friends check with
me", "people say I am hard to sell to"), and every reverse-keyed statement names something that
drains them or that they would gladly hand to somebody else. Nothing asks what the reader believes
about themselves in the abstract. (QUIZ-BLUEPRINTS.md, statement rules 1–6.)

Thirteen gift categories, **five candidates each — three keyed `+1`, two keyed `-1`** — so the
editor can cut to the blueprint's three per gift (one reverse-keyed) with both directions present
and a spare. **65 candidates.** Every word count below was computed, not estimated; the longest
statement is 19 words.

> Note on the owner's instruction of 2026-09-21 ("Don't leave Jesus off the list. I want to include
> our Savior"): that concerns the figure quiz's roster, quiz one in the blueprint. It does not
> touch this draft — a gifts quiz has no roster of people — and nothing here should be read as
> narrowing it.

## The sign convention, checked against the engine before drafting

Checked in `site/src/lib/strategies/category.ts`: `score()` accumulates `direction * answer` per
group, so **agreement with a `+1` item raises that gift's score and agreement with a `-1` item
lowers it.** A unipolar row has no poles and no centre — `pull()` turns the score into words
("a slight pull" … "the strongest here"), and `nothingNamed()` means a reader who denies everything
is told nothing stands out rather than being handed a gift by default. Both matter for the copy in
§3: the result must be able to say "nothing here stood out" without that reading as a verdict either.

## 1. How the thirteen were kept apart

The real risk in a gifts instrument is not loaded wording, it is **cross-loading**: serving and
helping and hospitality all look like "being useful", leading and administration both look like
"running things", wisdom and discernment both look like "seeing clearly". Each category below
therefore got one behavioural definition, and every statement was written to fit exactly one of
them. The definitions are printed with the tables so a critic can test each item against the one
it is supposed to feed.

Serving is *doing the job*; administration is *the order the job runs on*; hospitality is
*the house*. Leading is *the direction*; administration is *the detail*. Wisdom is *being asked
what to do*; discernment is *noticing what is off before being asked*. Encouraging is *pushing
somebody toward action*; mercy is *staying with somebody in pain without pushing at all*.
Teaching is *making it understood*; shepherding is *staying with the same few people for years*.

## 2. The candidates

### `serving` — serving / helping

*Romans 12:7; 1 Corinthians 12:28. Measured as: doing the practical job itself, usually unasked and often unseen.*

| dir | words | statement | why a decent person could answer either way |
|---|---|---|---|
| +1 | 16 | At a gathering I end up clearing plates or moving chairs without anybody asking me to. | Agreeing says the practical job gets done; it also says I left the conversation to do it. Disagreeing is not laziness — somebody has to stay at the table with the guests. |
| +1 | 15 | People text me when something needs carrying, fixing or collecting, and I usually say yes. | Being the first call is useful and it is also a queue that never empties. Someone who says no has time for the things they chose. |
| +1 | 17 | I would rather be handed a job to do than be asked what I think should happen. | A real trade-off between two kinds of person, not a virtue: the doer keeps the evening running, the one with an opinion stops a bad plan early. |
| -1 | 17 | I would rather sit and talk with people than get up and deal with the practical jobs. | Staying present with someone is a genuine good, and so is the washing-up. Neither end reads as the admirable one. |
| -1 | 15 | Fetching and tidying while everybody else is talking leaves me feeling I missed the evening. | An honest cost, not a confession. Plenty of people feel the opposite and find the job the easiest way to be there. |

### `teaching` — teaching

*Romans 12:7. Measured as: making a thing understood — explaining it, and checking it landed.*

| dir | words | statement | why a decent person could answer either way |
|---|---|---|---|
| +1 | 16 | People come to me to have something explained, even when they only wanted the short answer. | It names its own cost in the same breath: the explanation is wanted and it is longer than was asked for. |
| +1 | 19 | When a friend is lost in something I know well, I end up drawing it out step by step. | Patience with the slow route helps one person and bores another; sending the summary is a kindness too. |
| +1 | 13 | I check whether the other person actually followed me before I move on. | Checking respects them and can also feel like being tested. Not checking trusts them to speak up. |
| -1 | 16 | Being asked to explain something slowly, from the very beginning, is the part I like least. | Impatience with first principles is a real preference, not a failing — it usually goes with getting on and doing the thing. |
| -1 | 16 | I would rather send somebody a good link than sit down and walk them through it. | The link scales and respects their time; the sitting down does not scale and is sometimes the only thing that works. |

### `encouraging` — encouraging

*Romans 12:8. Measured as: putting courage into somebody — the push to keep going or to do the hard thing.*

| dir | words | statement | why a decent person could answer either way |
|---|---|---|---|
| +1 | 16 | People come to me when they need a push to do the thing they are avoiding. | Being the push is wanted by some and resented by others; whether it helps depends on the person, not on the pusher. |
| +1 | 18 | When somebody is about to give up on something good, I am the one who talks them round. | Talking someone round can be exactly right and can also override a decision they had good reasons for. |
| +1 | 16 | I tell people what I think they are capable of, even when they have not asked. | Unasked belief lands as fuel or as pressure. Keeping it to yourself is respect, not indifference. |
| -1 | 18 | When a friend is hesitating, I leave them to decide in their own time rather than press them. | Restraint is somebody else's strength and a real gift to a hesitant person; pressing is not automatically better. |
| -1 | 15 | A pep talk sounds false coming from me, so I let somebody else give it. | Knowing you are not the one for it is self-knowledge, not cowardice; the quiet friend is often believed more. |

### `giving` — giving

*Romans 12:8. Measured as: releasing money and possessions, quickly and without a fuss.*

| dir | words | statement | why a decent person could answer either way |
|---|---|---|---|
| +1 | 17 | When somebody needs money and I have it, I decide quickly and rarely think about it again. | Fast release helps now and skips the question of whether the money was the right help. Slow is not meanness. |
| +1 | 16 | I give away things I am still using when somebody needs them more than I do. | A genuine tension: the thing goes where it is needed, and I end up replacing it or doing without. |
| +1 | 13 | People know they can ask me for money before they ask anyone else. | Being that person is useful and exhausting, and being asked last can simply mean people know your situation. |
| -1 | 12 | I find it easier to give my time than to give money. | Two kinds of generosity, with no ranking between them. An hour costs somebody more than a note does. |
| -1 | 19 | I decide in advance what I can give, and I hold to that when I am asked for more. | Planned giving is a discipline and a wall. Agreeing is prudence; disagreeing is responsiveness. Both cost something. |

### `leading` — leading

*Romans 12:8. Measured as: setting the direction and carrying responsibility for how the whole thing turns out.*

| dir | words | statement | why a decent person could answer either way |
|---|---|---|---|
| +1 | 15 | When a group stalls, I end up saying what we are going to do next. | Somebody has to move it and the mover can be wrong. Waiting is sometimes how a better plan appears. |
| +1 | 17 | People look at me when a decision has to be made and nobody wants to make it. | Carrying the decision protects everyone else and takes the choice away from them. |
| +1 | 17 | I would rather be answerable for how the whole thing turns out than do one part well. | A plain trade-off: the person answerable for everything does no single part as carefully as the specialist. |
| -1 | 13 | Being the one everybody looks to for the next move wears me out. | Naming the cost, not a fault; many capable people do their best work without the room watching them. |
| -1 | 14 | I do my best work when somebody else has decided where we are going. | Working well inside a decision is a strength in itself, and it depends on the decision being good. |

### `mercy` — showing mercy

*Romans 12:8. Measured as: staying with people in pain without needing to fix it.*

| dir | words | statement | why a decent person could answer either way |
|---|---|---|---|
| +1 | 16 | I stay with people having a terrible time even when there is nothing to be done. | Staying costs the evening and fixes nothing; leaving and sending help is not colder, only different. |
| +1 | 15 | Somebody crying in front of me does not make me want to leave the room. | Comfort with distress is not courage and discomfort is not failure; both are ordinary and both change who you can help. |
| +1 | 16 | I notice the person who is not coping before I notice anything else in the room. | The same attention that finds them misses the rest of the room, which somebody else is holding together. |
| -1 | 16 | When somebody is in pain I want to fix the cause rather than sit with it. | Fixing is what half of all suffering actually needs. Sitting is what the other half needs. Neither answer is the good one. |
| -1 | 15 | A long conversation about somebody's grief leaves me drained for the rest of the day. | True of many people who still show up for it. Not being drained is not a higher love, only a different constitution. |

### `administration` — administration

*1 Corinthians 12:28. Measured as: the order underneath — lists, dates, details, so the thing actually runs.*

| dir | words | statement | why a decent person could answer either way |
|---|---|---|---|
| +1 | 15 | I am usually the one keeping the list and chasing people for the missing details. | The chaser makes it happen and is the reason everyone's phone buzzes on a Saturday. |
| +1 | 15 | When plans are loose, I write them down and send them round without being asked. | Writing it down removes confusion and also pins people to a plan they were still thinking about. |
| +1 | 16 | I notice when a date or a number does not add up before anyone else does. | Catching it early saves the day and can stall a thing that would have been fine. |
| -1 | 15 | Timetables, forms and chasing people for dates are the parts I hope somebody else takes. | An honest preference. The people who hate the forms are frequently the ones doing the work the forms are about. |
| -1 | 14 | I would rather start the work than spend the morning organising who does what. | Starting wins time and risks duplication; organising spends the morning and saves the week. Genuinely two-sided. |

### `wisdom` — wisdom

*1 Corinthians 12:8. Measured as: being brought the tangled situation and asked what to do.*

| dir | words | statement | why a decent person could answer either way |
|---|---|---|---|
| +1 | 12 | People bring me tangled situations and ask me what they should do. | Being asked is a compliment and a weight; not being asked can simply mean your friends decide for themselves. |
| +1 | 15 | When two good options are on the table, people ask me which one to take. | Somebody has to say it, and saying it means owning an outcome that was not yours to live. |
| +1 | 19 | I hold back my view until I have heard the whole story, even when people want an answer now. | Waiting buys accuracy and spends the other person's patience, which is a cost they are paying, not you. |
| -1 | 17 | When friends ask what they should do, I tell them I am the wrong person to ask. | Declining can be honesty about your limits rather than avoidance, and it hands the question back to the one who owns it. |
| -1 | 17 | I would rather say plainly what I would do than weigh up somebody else's situation for them. | Straight talk is quick and clear; weighing is slow and fits the person. Both are somebody's favourite friend. |

### `discernment` — discernment

*1 Corinthians 12:10. Measured as: noticing what is off — in a claim, an offer, a room — before others do.*

| dir | words | statement | why a decent person could answer either way |
|---|---|---|---|
| +1 | 17 | People say I am hard to sell to: I hear the catch before I hear the offer. | The same ear that catches the catch misses the offer that was genuinely good. |
| +1 | 14 | Friends check a person or an offer with me before they commit to it. | Being the check is useful and makes you the one who has to say the unwelcome thing. |
| +1 | 15 | I can tell a room has gone wrong before anything has been said about it. | Reading the room early is often right and sometimes reads a mood into a room that was only tired. |
| -1 | 17 | I take people at their word, and I am sometimes the last to see a problem coming. | Trust is a way of treating people well that costs you occasionally. Suspicion catches more and costs the relationship. |
| -1 | 15 | I would rather assume the best of somebody than test whether they are being straight. | Agreeing is generosity; disagreeing is care. Whichever way you answer, somebody has been protected and somebody has been wronged. |

### `faith` — faith

*1 Corinthians 12:9. Measured as: committing before the outcome is secured, and still expecting it.*

| dir | words | statement | why a decent person could answer either way |
|---|---|---|---|
| +1 | 16 | I commit to things before I can see how they will be paid for or finished. | Committing early makes things exist that otherwise would not, and occasionally leaves other people holding the gap. |
| +1 | 16 | People say I am still expecting it to work out after others have written it off. | Hope keeps a thing alive past the point some would cut it loose, which is either loyalty or sunk cost, and you cannot always tell which. |
| +1 | 14 | I start things without a back-up plan more often than most people I know. | The back-up plan is prudence and it is also an exit that makes the first plan easier to abandon. |
| -1 | 15 | I want the money, the dates and the people confirmed before I will say yes. | Wanting it confirmed is responsibility to whoever comes after you, and it means some things never start. |
| -1 | 17 | Being asked to press on with no sign it will work is where I want to stop. | Knowing when to stop saves years. Not knowing builds the thing nobody thought possible. Both are true of real people. |

### `evangelism` — evangelism

*Ephesians 4:11. Measured as: starting the conversation about God with people outside it.*

| dir | words | statement | why a decent person could answer either way |
|---|---|---|---|
| +1 | 16 | I bring up what I believe with people who do not share it, without much trouble. | Openness starts conversations some people want and puts others on the spot. |
| +1 | 13 | I end up talking about God with people I have only just met. | Some strangers are glad it came up and some wanted a quiet queue; both reactions are reasonable. |
| +1 | 14 | People who are not religious tell me I am easy to ask about it. | Being easy to ask is a real thing others notice; not being asked often means your friends already know where you stand. |
| -1 | 12 | I keep what I believe to myself unless somebody asks me directly. | Restraint respects the other person and leaves the question unasked. Neither end is the honourable one. |
| -1 | 17 | Starting that conversation with a stranger is the thing I would most gladly hand to somebody else. | Naming what drains you is not a confession of cowardice; most people have something on this list they would hand over. |

### `shepherding` — shepherding

*Ephesians 4:11. Measured as: staying with the same few people over a long time.*

| dir | words | statement | why a decent person could answer either way |
|---|---|---|---|
| +1 | 16 | I keep following up with the same few people long after everybody else has moved on. | Staying is what some people need and can also keep them leaning on you rather than standing up. |
| +1 | 13 | I notice when somebody has stopped turning up, and I get in touch. | Getting in touch can be the message that brings them back, or the one that makes them feel watched. |
| +1 | 14 | People I helped years ago still come back to me when something goes wrong. | A long tail of people is a sign of trust and a claim on your week that never expires. |
| -1 | 14 | I give people what they need at the time and let it end there. | Clean endings are honest and leave nobody half-held; the long follow-up is a different gift, not a better person. |
| -1 | 16 | Keeping up with a long list of people over months is more than I can manage. | A limit most people have. Someone who can hold the list often holds less of something else. |

### `hospitality` — hospitality

*1 Peter 4:9-10. Measured as: opening the home and the table, and making room for one more.*

| dir | words | statement | why a decent person could answer either way |
|---|---|---|---|
| +1 | 17 | I invite people to my home without waiting until it is tidy or the food is planned. | The low bar gets people through the door and gives them a kitchen full of washing-up; the high bar gives them an evening someone prepared. |
| +1 | 14 | People end up staying at my place when they have nowhere else to go. | Having the spare room used is a good thing to be known for and it is your house, all the time. |
| +1 | 17 | I add a seat for whoever turns up rather than keep to the numbers I planned for. | One more seat welcomes the extra guest and changes the evening the first guests were invited to. |
| -1 | 13 | I would rather meet people somewhere out than have them in my home. | Meeting out is easier on everybody and keeps a door shut. Both are ways of protecting something worth protecting. |
| -1 | 15 | Having people in the house all evening leaves me needing the next day to myself. | A cost, honestly stated. The people who need the day after are often the ones who host anyway. |

## 3. The honest frame

### For the intro (86 words)

> Scripture gives lists of gifts. It gives no test for them. The lists say gifts are given for
> everyone else as much as for the person who has them, and they show up in service and get named
> by other people long before you would name them yourself. So this is not a verdict, and a few
> dozen statements cannot see your last ten years. Take what comes out to two or three people who
> have watched you work, and ask them whether it is true.

### For the result (82 words)

> This is where your answers pointed. It is not a measurement of what God has given you. Nobody is
> only one of these, the list here is not everything Scripture names, and a self-report can be
> wrong in both directions, about what you avoid as much as what you are good at. The useful step
> is not to believe this page. It is to send it to somebody who has served alongside you and ask
> whether it matches what they have seen.

Both avoid a number, in line with `pull()`: the engine deliberately prints no percentage for a
unipolar result, and the frame copy must not smuggle one back in ("your top gift is 78% …").

### The sentence about what is not in this draft (48 words)

> Prophecy, healing, miracles, tongues and their interpretation are not in this draft, not because
> they are lesser or have stopped, but because Christians reading the same passages disagree about
> how they are given today, and a set of statements about yourself could not measure them fairly
> either way.

Three things it deliberately does **not** say: that they are for another era, that they are for
another kind of Christian, and that the site will add them later (the blueprint records this as the
owner's open decision, so the page should not promise an outcome he has not chosen). If a second
line is wanted, the honest one points sideways rather than down: *the Compass's Gifts axis is where
that disagreement is set out in both sides' own words* — a cross-link the platform already supports.

## 4. What I could not fully solve, for the critics

1. **Rule 5 is only partly achievable in a gifts quiz.** A reader who knows the traditional lists
   will guess that "people end up staying at my place" feeds hospitality. What rule 5 can still buy
   is that nothing is *labelled*: no statement uses a category's own name or its stock phrase
   ("I have a heart for …", "I love to serve"), so the reader is agreeing with a behaviour, not with
   a title. The items most likely to be read off are `hospitality` and `giving`; the least are
   `faith`, `wisdom` and `serving`'s third item. I would rather the editor accept this openly on the
   page than pretend the instrument is blind.
2. **Suspected cross-loads**, worth a critic's attention before wiring: `serving` +3 ("rather be
   handed a job") also depresses `wisdom`/`leading` in a reader's mind; `mercy` −1 ("want to fix the
   cause") overlaps `serving`; `administration` +3 ("a date or a number does not add up") brushes
   `discernment`; `shepherding` +2 ("notice when somebody has stopped turning up") brushes `mercy`.
   Each still fits exactly one definition in §1, but a reader does not have §1.
3. **Two items lean admirable and would be my first cuts if a critic agrees:** `discernment` +1
   ("hard to sell to") flirts with flattery, and `faith` +2 ("still expecting it to work out")
   could read as praise rather than as description — its "sunk cost" note is doing a lot of work.
4. **`faith` is the category most likely to be misread.** In 1 Corinthians 12:9 it is not saving
   faith, and a reader who scores low may hear "you do not believe". Whatever three items are
   chosen, the gift page needs a first line that says plainly it is not about whether you believe.
5. **Word "religious"** appears once (`evangelism` +3). It is ordinary rather than church jargon,
   but a newcomer-voice critic should confirm it does not read as a slight.
6. **Verbatim passages are not in this draft on purpose.** The blueprint requires each gift to be
   defined in the words of its passage, quoted from the World English Bible British Edition on disk.
   I have given references and a behavioural definition only; the editor should take the quotations
   from the file rather than from memory, and check that the plain-English gloss in §1 matches the
   wording that ends up quoted.
