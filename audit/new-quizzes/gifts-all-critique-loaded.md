# Adversarial critique: LOADED WORDING. "What are your spiritual gifts?", all gifts in

Targets: `gifts-all-statements-A.md` (Drafter A, "from the reader") and `gifts-all-statements-B.md`
(Drafter B, "from the text outward"). Every candidate gets **KEEP / FIX / KILL** with the reason.
Rewrites are under 22 words and were counted by script, not by eye.

Read first, as briefed: `QUIZ-BLUEPRINTS.md` (the six rules), `spiritual-gifts.ts`, `gifts-final.md`,
`AUDIT-LOG.md` decisions 1, 4 and 6, `audit-pentecostal.md`, `audit-cessationist.md`, and the Compass's
Gifts axis in `site/src/data/compass.json`. Every reference that either draft puts into a summary or a
recorded act was opened with `node audit/tools/webbe.mjs` (list at the end). Nothing under `site/` was
touched, nothing was committed, and this is the only file written.

What I hunted, candidate by candidate: statements a decent person can answer only one way; flattery;
reverse items that shame; anything that says in the site's voice that an event was from God; anything a
cessationist cannot answer truthfully without granting the other side's doctrine; anything that draws a
Pentecostal's gift as a hunch, a temperament or a skill; devotion counted (rule 6); statements a
prejudiced, credulous or self-important reader agrees with strongly for the wrong reason; neighbouring
gifts sharing one act; summaries and acts that say more or less than their verses.

---

## 1. Verdict

**Neither draft is the base this time. Take the scoring design from A and most of the wording from B.**

- A's best contribution is not a statement. It is two tests: (1) a gift that is a plain fact (tongues)
  will beat every gift that is a pattern unless the top of its row needs more than the fact, and (2) for
  every reverse item, ask what the reader with no experience of the gift answers, because disagreeing
  with a reverse item RAISES the gift. Both tests are right and I applied them to B's candidates too.
  Several of B's reverse items fail the second test less often than A's, because B preferred plain
  reports with no "when" in them.
- B's best contribution is the scenes. Its recorded acts are fuller and keep the Spirit, the signs and
  the laying on of hands. Its one sentence for the disagreement is the better one (section 3, S1).
- Counted over the thirty candidates each for the six added gifts:
  **A: 7 keep, 11 fix, 12 kill. B: 14 keep, 5 fix, 11 kill.**
- Nothing in either draft says in the site's own voice that an event was a miracle, a healing or a
  word from God. Both drafters held that line. The faults I found are of other kinds: devotion let in
  by the side door (three candidates), reverse items that raise the row for nearly every praying
  Christian (one bad case), a gift's absence scored with a neighbouring gift's act (two cases), one
  pair of neighbouring gifts sharing an act outright (A-K1 and A-P1), and ten places where a summary
  or an act says more or less than its verses (section 3, S5 to S12).

The honest limit, said once: for healing, miracles, prophecy, tongues and interpretation **rule 5 is
lost and cannot be got back**, and both drafters say so. I did not mark any statement down for
pointing at its own result where the alternative was to turn the gift into a feeling.

## 2. The six added gifts, candidate by candidate

### 2.1 The word of knowledge

The hard fact here is that three statements must serve two readings of a phrase the Bible uses once and
never explains. Both drafters saw it; they split the three differently.

| id | statement | verdict | reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-K1 | People have told me that a fact I gave them was exactly what they needed to know. | **KILL** | It is the same act as A-P1 ("something I said to them was what God wanted them to hear") with one noun changed, so knowledge and prophecy would share an act, which the brief forbids. "Exactly" is a flattery word. Any helpful person agrees. |
| A-K2 | People have asked me how I knew something about them that they had never told me. | **FIX** | "They had never told me" leaves the door open to "somebody else told me", so the gossip agrees truthfully. B-K2 is the fixed form: "that I had no way of knowing". |
| A-K3 | I have known something about a person's situation that nobody had told me, and it proved true. | **KILL** | "Known ... and it proved true" is the reader marking their own homework: the "I can tell" family the first edit ruled out ("I can tell asserts being right"). Memory keeps the hits. It is about knowing, and the passage names a WORD of knowledge, something said. |
| A-K4 | When a question about the Bible comes up, I would rather look it up than answer from memory. | **KILL** | It scores checking as the absence of a gift, while discernment's own page cites checking as a recorded act (Acts 17:11, "examining the Scriptures daily"). It also rewards the reader who answers confidently from memory, who is the reader of 1 Corinthians 8:1 ("Knowledge puffs up"). |
| A-K5 | What I know about a person's situation is what they or someone else has told me. | **KEEP (spare)** | A plain fact; agreeing is everybody's ordinary answer and shames nobody; a newcomer agrees, which lowers a row they do not have. | A spare / B-K1 | People ask me where the Bible says something, and I can usually tell them. | **KEEP (spare)** | Counts knowledge, not reading hours, so rule 6 holds. "I can usually tell them" is a small self-rating of competence, the same shape as serving's accepted "and I usually say yes". It cannot ship beside B-K2: two "others" statements. |
| B-K2 | People have told me I said something about their life that I had no way of knowing. | **KEEP (pick)** | The best shape in either file for the contested reading: what people said, silent on how, asserting nothing. A cessationist answers it truthfully either way. Putting the contested claim in OTHER people's mouths is safer than putting it in the reader's (B-K3, A-K3). |
| B-K3 | I have told somebody a thing about their situation that they had not told anyone. | **KEEP (spare)** | Honest, but the reader can only know "they had not told anyone" because the person said so, which makes it B-K2's event told twice. A confident guesser agrees. |
| B-K4 | When somebody gets a fact about the faith wrong, I let it pass rather than correct it. | **KILL** | It is the figure quiz's ground: "I often leave a conversation with an opinion I never said out loud" (voice) and "I would rather have the argument now than keep things calm" (conflict) are already temperament there, and the first edit's rule is that one behaviour cannot be temperament in one quiz and a gift in the other. The reader who corrects everybody disagrees strongly and gains, for the wrong reason. "The faith" is church shorthand. |
| B-K5 | When a question about the Bible comes up, I am the one asking rather than answering. | **KEEP (pick)** | Agreeing shames nobody; asking is how people learn. A newcomer agrees or picks the middle, both harmless. |
| B spare | In a discussion I end up supplying the verse or the fact people were reaching for. | **KEEP (pick)** | First person, an act, first reading. "End up" is the house's own turn of phrase (wisdom has it). Near teaching, but the act differs: having it to hand against making it understood. |

**My three:** B-K2 (others, second reading), B's spare (first reading), B-K5 (reverse, first reading).
The contested claim is carried by what other people said, and each reading can reach about two thirds of
the row; only a reader with both reaches the top. **The editor should know the other honest choice:**
swap B's spare for B-K3 and the second reading can reach the top while the first is left with the
reverse item and with teaching's row. The brief's own asymmetry points that way (a Pentecostal must
not find the gift drawn as a skill; a cessationist's low row is "a fact about the statements"). I
chose the even split because the summary promises that the statements "ask about both".

### 2.2 Healing

| A-H5 | When someone wants prayer for an illness, I point them to somebody else. | **KILL** | A's chair-two test, turned on A. | B-H3 | When somebody tells me they are ill, I pray with them there and then. | **KILL** | A prayer habit. | B-H4 | When somebody is ill, I would sooner bring them a meal than pray with them. | **FIX (use A-H4)** | It scores mercy's act as the absence of healing, so a reader with both is marked down on one for having the other; in Acts 9:36-41 both are in one room. | B-H5 | When I pray for someone who is ill, I ask for comfort rather than for the illness to go. | **KILL** | It looks inside a prayer, which is as near rule 6 as anything offered, and it scores as a lack what 2 Corinthians 12:8-9 records of Paul. Conditional as well. |

**My three:** A-H1 (others), B's spare, A-H4 (reverse). A reader for whom one recovery followed one
prayer lands mid-row; the top needs "more than once", people saying so, and praying with the person.

### 2.6 Interpreting tongues

One wording point first. For tongues I preferred "words" to "a language" so that nobody has to grant
the disputed point. For interpretation that fix backfires: "words nobody there knew" can be read as
hard vocabulary, and a reader who explains long words at a Bible study would agree. Here the reader
is describing somebody else's speech as the room took it, so "a language nobody there knew" concedes
little and closes the loophole. "Nobody there" includes the reader, which keeps the person who
translates Spanish for a visitor from agreeing.

| id | statement | verdict | reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-I1 | When someone has spoken aloud in a language nobody there had learnt, I have given its meaning. | **FIX (pick)** | Sound. Plainer: **"When someone has spoken in church in a language nobody there knew, I have said what it meant."** (18) |
| A-I2 / B-I1 | People (in my church) look to me for the meaning when someone speaks in a language nobody knows. | **KILL** | A standing reputation, which caps the newcomer (the audit's G7 over again), and it is the self-appointed interpreter's favourite sentence. |

| A-I4 / B-I4 | When someone speaks in a language nobody there knows, I wait for another person to say what it means. | **KEEP (spare)** | Honest and shame-free; conditional, so A-I5 is safer. |
| A-I5 | When people speak in languages nobody there has learnt, I have no idea what is meant. | **FIX, lightly (pick)** | Every chair can answer it. Add the place so "there" has one: **"When people at church speak in a language nobody there knows, I have no idea what is meant."** (18) |

| B-I3 | People have told me that my explaining what was said in an unknown language helped them. | **FIX (pick)** | The right "others" statement: it reports the effect Paul names ("that the assembly may be built up") and claims nothing about being right. ## 3. Summaries and recorded acts, checked against the verses

Every reference was opened. Findings only; what is not listed says what its verses say.

**S1. The one sentence: use B's, on all six pages.** B: "Many Christians hold that this gift is given
today, to be welcomed and tested; many hold that it was given for the time of the apostles." A's longer
form ("authenticated the apostles and their message and passed away once that foundation was laid")
lifts a sentence the Compass writes about "those sign gifts" together and pins it on each gift singly.
Said of prophecy, the word of knowledge or interpretation, "authenticated the apostles" is not
obviously how cessationists themselves put it (the Compass's own next clause is about "the completed
Scriptures"), and "that foundation" has nothing to point back to. B's plain form is true of every one
of the six and names nobody.

**S2. Healing's extra clause: reuse the audited words exactly.** B writes "both hold that God heals in
answer to prayer", which welds two claims into one causal one. The Compass says cessationists "affirm
that God still heals and answers prayer". Write: "both affirm that God still heals and answers
prayer." A's version hangs the clause on one side only ("while affirming"), which reads as though the
other side might not.

**S3. B's discernment summary defines the gift in the site's voice.** "The passage says discerning of
spirits: telling what is behind a message, a teaching or a claim to speak for God." The passage gives no
definition, and that one is narrower than some churches teach. A's form is safer: name the passage,
quote 1 Corinthians 14:29 and 1 John 4:1, then say what the statements ask. A's closing words "they
are not about first impressions of people" should stay; they are the plain answer to AUDIT-LOG
decision 4.

**S4. B's miracles summary has a false "so".** "Luke writes that God worked special miracles by the
hands of Paul (Acts 19:11), so these statements ask what has happened ..." The second half does not
follow from the first. Drop "so" and make two sentences. A's "this page does not say what any event
was" is the better closing sentence for that page.

**S5. 1 Corinthians 1:4-7 is trimmed at both ends, in both drafts.** The range includes "even as the
testimony of Christ was confirmed in you" (verse 6) and "waiting for the revelation of our Lord Jesus
Christ" (verse 7). One side leans on the first phrase and the other on the second, and both are cut.
This is the fault the Pentecostal reviewer found four times last round. Say what the verses say:
"Paul thanks God that the Corinthians were enriched in Christ in all speech and all knowledge, even as
the testimony of Christ was confirmed in them, so that they come behind in no gift as they wait for the
revelation of the Lord Jesus Christ."

**S6. Drop A's 1 Corinthians 13:2 from knowledge's acts.** It is not an act; A's sentence cuts "all
faith, so as to remove mountains" out of the middle of the verse; and it stands six verses from
13:8-12, which the Compass lists among the passages both sides argue from. B's three are enough.

**S7. Romans 15:14.** A leaves out "full of goodness". Use B's sentence.

**S8. 1 Timothy 1:18, B's prophecy act.** B writes "the prophecies which had been given about him".
The text on disk says "the prophecies which were given to you before". Write "given to him".

**S9. Acts 2.** B cites 2:1-13 and its sentence leaves out the sound like wind and the tongues like
fire (verses 2-3): signs trimmed from inside the cited range. Use A's range, 2:4-13, whose sentence
matches it.

**S10. Sentences that reach outside their range.** B's Acts 9:40-42 says "where the disciple Tabitha
had died" (that is 9:36-37); A's sentence for the same range stays inside it ("turning to the body").
B's Acts 10:44-48 says "in the house of Cornelius" (earlier in the chapter). A's Acts 19:6-7 says "at
Ephesus" and "disciples" (19:1); use B's 19:1-7. Small, but the verifier will fail them.

**S11. Acts 11:27-30, B.** Leaves out "all over the world". A has it.

**S12. Acts 9:32-35, B.** "Told him that Jesus Christ healed him": the text is present tense, "Jesus
Christ heals you". Write "heals him", as A does.

**S13. James 5:14-15 (A's optional fourth act for healing).** It names elders, oil and prayer, and
names no gift, so placing it on this page is a connection the site makes: the same objection the
cessationist reviewer raised against Acts 5:1-4 under discernment. Leave it off. B's four acts stand.

**S14. Galatians 3:5 with Hebrews 2:3-4 (B) or 2 Corinthians 12:12 (A).** Fair as a pair, one verse
for each reading, as discernment already has. Note B's inconsistency so the editor decides on purpose:
B refuses 1 Corinthians 13:8 for knowledge because it is on the Compass's argued list, then uses
Hebrews 2:3-4, which is on the same list. I would keep the pair. Of the two, 2 Corinthians 12:12 is
the closer fit for this page, because its words are about "mighty works".

**S15. Acts 5:1-4 stays under discernment only** (both drafters agree), with A's added sentence: "Luke
does not say how Peter knew." It is true, and it is what stops either reading from claiming the verse.
The knowledge page must not cite it, nor 1 Corinthians 14:24-25, which Paul writes of prophecy.

**S16. B's knowledge summary.** "Supplying what God has made known at the moment it is needed" is
opaque. Plainer, and still naming nobody: "Some Christians take it as having what the Bible teaches
ready to say when it is needed, others as being given a fact that nobody had told you, and these
statements ask about both." One caution for the editor, which I cannot settle because I opened no
book: some who hold that gifts ceased take the first reading and hold that THAT gift continues. "Many
hold that it was given for the time of the apostles" stays true because "many" is not "all", but this
page may be better served by the wisdom sentence (S17).

**S17. Wisdom, discernment and faith.** Use B's wording: "Christians who hold that some gifts were
given only for the time of the apostles differ on whether this is one of them." It is uncited, as both
drafters say. Keep it this general and name nobody unless somebody opens the books.

**S18. The result note.** "Some of these patterns are temperament as much as gift" is, said of tongues
or healing, precisely the sentence a Pentecostal reader must not find. A caught it. "Some of the

**S19. Prophecy is scored; prophets are not.** Both drafters ask for the sentence. It is needed for a

**S20. The headline and the share text.** "Where my answers pointed: miracles, healing, prophecy"

and it is the owner's decision that they are scored, so I raise one thing only: the share text for
these six rows should carry the words "what people said", or the top-three share should be looked at
again by the engineer.

## 4. Job 2: the "others" statement for the six gifts that lack one

My own count agrees with both drafters: seven of the thirteen have one (serving, teaching,
encouraging, administration, wisdom, discernment, shepherding) and **six** lack one (giving, leading,
mercy, faith, evangelism, hospitality). The audit's "five" was counted before its own fix took
leading's away.

Both drafters chose the same statement to remove in all six cases, for compatible reasons. I agree
with all six removals.

| gift | candidate | verdict | reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| giving | A-Ga: People close to me have said I give away more than I should. | **KILL** | It is about how much the reader gives, by other people's measure, and the gift's own summary says "nothing here counts how much". Rule 6 names giving. It is also a boast dressed as a complaint. |
| giving | A-Gb: When someone cannot afford something they need, people tend to mention it to me. | **KILL** | A's own reason: rewards being known to have money. |
| giving | B-G1: People tell me when somebody they know is short of money. | **FIX (pick)** | The right act (others bring the need). "Short of money" makes it a statement about who is known to have money. **"People tell me when somebody they know is going without."** (10) keeps the capacity guard the first edit insisted on. One limit no wording removes: the reader who gives in secret (Matthew 6:3-4) is told of fewer needs. |
| giving | B-G2: Friends have told me off for giving away something I still needed. | **KEEP (spare)** | The cost is in the sentence, but it repeats the statement that stays. |
| leading | A-La: People have put me in charge of things I did not ask to run. | **KEEP (pick)** | What others did, with a cost in it. It does not bring back "being consulted before a decision", so the audit's G8 is not undone. |
| leading | B-L1: I get put in charge of things without having asked to be. | **KEEP** | The same statement; A's names the people, which is what an "others" statement is for. |
| leading | A-Lb | **KILL** | Being asked what happens next is G8's shared act again. |
| leading | B-L2: Once I have decided, people go along with it, even those who wanted something else. | **KILL** | The domineering reader agrees strongly, for the wrong reason. |
| mercy | A-MEa: People come to me when they are upset, often before they have told anyone else. | **FIX (use B-Me1)** | "Before they have told anyone else" flatters (the chosen confidant), and it sits on shepherding's and encouraging's "people come to me". |
| mercy | B-Me1: People ring me when they are at their lowest, even people I do not know well. | **KEEP (pick)** | "Even people I do not know well" is what keeps it off shepherding. Optional: "turn to me" for "ring me", so it does not depend on a telephone. |

## 5. Job 3: discernment

**The ruling both drafters reach is right, and they reach it the same way.** In 1 Corinthians 12:10 and
14:29, 1 John 4:1 and 1 Thessalonians 5:20-21 the thing weighed is a message, a teaching or a claim to
speak for God, never a first impression of someone's character. "I decide something is off about a
person before I could say what it is" goes. The Pentecostal reviewer keeps what he asked for because
the replacement is silent on HOW the reader knew.

| id | statement | verdict | reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-D1 | I have warned people off something said or taught in God's name, and they later told me I was right. | **FIX (pick)** | The right shape and the one the brief asked for: what happened, then what others later said, silent on how. "Said or taught in God's name" will stop a newcomer. **"I have warned people off a teaching or a claim about God, and they later told me I was right."** (20) |
| A-D2 / B-Dc | I have said that a popular teaching was wrong while the people around me still welcomed it. | **KILL** | No outcome in it. The contrarian agrees strongly, and so does the prejudiced reader the audit worried about, now about ideas instead of people. |
| A-D3 | I am slower than the people around me to accept that a message or a claim is from God. | **KILL** | Ranks the reader against other people (refused for faith in the first edit), sits on the figure quiz's Asks why axis, and draws the gift as mere slowness. 1 Thessalonians 5:21 also says "hold firmly that which is good". |
| B-Da | I have warned people about a teaching or a claim that was later shown to be false. | **FIX (spare)** | "Shown to be false" is the reader's own verdict, and "a claim" with nothing after it takes in scam emails. |
| B-Db | People have told me later that I was right to warn them about a teaching or a claim. | **KEEP (spare)** | Clean, but with the reworded statement 20 it would give discernment two "others" statements and cap the newcomer twice. The fixed A-D1 begins with the reader's own act, so it caps less. |

One risk no wording removes: the reader who warns against everything has followers who tell him he was
right. Having the confirmation come from other people is the most that can be done.

**Statement 20, "Friends ask me whether someone can be trusted before they rely on them."** Both
drafters find the same fault in it (its object is a person's character) and both are right. Take B's
rewording, which is concrete and plain: **"Friends send me a talk or a book and ask whether it can be
trusted."** (15, others.) A's ("a teaching or a claim holds up before they go along with it") is sound
but abstract.

**Statement 9, "I take a new idea as it sounds rather than look into where it came from."** Stands.

**The summary.** Use A's (see S3), with B's closing sentence (S17). "These statements can see only that
everyday pattern" is no longer true and should go.

**A caution for the editor.** Teaching, wisdom, discernment and (if B-K1 is ever used) knowledge would
each have a "people ask me" statement. The acts differ (explain, choose, vet, locate), so this is not
the audit's G8 again, but the well-regarded reader will rise on all of them together.

### What I would say to the owner, in plain words

1. For five of the new gifts a reader can tell which gift each statement is about. There is no honest
   way round that, and the intro should say so (B's proposed sentence is the better one).
2. Healing, miracles and tongues cannot be asked about without mentioning prayer. None of the
   recommended statements counts how much anybody prays.
3. A reader who holds that these gifts ceased can answer every statement truthfully. Two of them
   ("People have told me they got better after I prayed for them" and "I have prayed for something
   everyone present thought impossible, and it happened") can be true of that reader as well, which is
   why the healing and miracles pages must say that the statements cannot tell a gift from an answered
   prayer and call nothing a miracle themselves.
