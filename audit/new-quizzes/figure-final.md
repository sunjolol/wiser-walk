# Who in the Bible are you most like? — the final instrument, on one page

Editor's cut from draft A (everyday life), draft B (what other people notice), the loadedness
critique and the plain-language critique. Data file: `site/src/lib/quizzes/bible-figure.ts`
(status `draft`, not registered). Figures: `site/src/data/bible-figures.json`, a flattened copy of
`figures.verified.json`, all 22 people plus Jesus.

**Sign convention (the Compass's, read from `strategies/bipolar.ts`).** A score of 0 is the left
pole, 100 the right. `-1` means agreeing moves the reader toward the LEFT pole; `+1` toward the
RIGHT pole. Every sign below was re-derived from the final wording.

## The two statements the editor touched or wrote, against the six rules

**6. "I work out the details when I get there, not before."** Neither draft had a clean second
item for Meets the day: N4 echoes Matthew 6:34 and was killed by both critics; N5 ("I book things
late") says the same thing as 18; N6 carries a hospitality halo; the loaded critic's N6 fix ("I do
not mind…") reports a feeling; B34 and B36 would have been a second borrowed voice on the axis.
Rule 1: eleven words, one claim, first person, present tense, a thing done. Rule 2: no church or
Bible word. Rule 3: agreeing means arriving unprepared, disagreeing means committing before you
know; both cost something. Rule 4: the axis has 12 on the other side. Rule 5: points at no figure.
Rule 6: nothing about devotion.

**11. "I go along with things without checking them first."** B28 with "People have told me"
removed, because it would have been the only statement on its pole and the axis already has a
borrowed voice (5). The loaded critic called B28 the load-bearing guard that stops "takes it on
trust" being heard as the admirable answer; the bluntness that does that work is unchanged.
Nine words, one claim, first person, present tense, a thing done; both ends cost something.

## Critic suggestions rejected, with the reason

- **Loaded critic's recommended eighteen (fourteen borrowed-voice items).** A reader with nobody
  commenting on them must disagree with both poles and lands central, and the centre of this map
  is where Jesus and Mary of Nazareth sit. The plain critic's ceiling was applied instead.
- **Plain critic: keep A P1.** "Before the others have finished talking" belittles the other pole.
- **Plain critic: keep A P5.** Waiting before replying to a hard message is standard advice; agreeing is the wise answer.
- **Plain critic's V1 rewrite ("at the time, not afterwards").** Still accuses the other pole of talking behind backs.
- **Plain critic's V3 rewrite ("what everyone else is avoiding").** The truth-teller frame, made stronger.
- **Plain critic's L2 rewrite ("stands up and answers for it").** Heroic; the admirable answer.
- **Plain critic: keep A C1.** "Even when the timing is bad" is a concessive that signals courage.
- **Plain critic: keep A D2.** "Someone I trust has already checked" makes agreeing a small betrayal.
- **Plain critic's B17 rewrite.** Without the tease frame, asking everyone's view reads as humility.
- **Plain critic: keep B33 as drafted, and the B36 rewrite ("the calmest one").** Both are boasts.
- **Plain critic: keep A N6.** Hospitality halo on a Christian site.
- **Loaded critic's B24 fix ("agree… and then resent having agreed").** Two claims; a reader who does only the first cannot answer.
- **Loaded critic's C5 and C6 fixes.** C5 still reads as the weak answer; C6 is still abstract.
- **Loaded critic: keep B1 with its tease frame.** Booking overlaps the Plans axis; A P2 measures the impulse without touching planning.
- **Both critics' D5 fix.** A good sentence, passed over only because it mirrors 17 ("before I follow it" / "before I understand") and B28 guards the pole better.
- **A N4.** Killed by both critics (Matthew 6:34). Not replaced in kind.

## Copy

- **Title:** Who in the Bible are you most like?
- **Tagline (11 words):** Eighteen plain statements, then the figure whose recorded acts sit nearest.
- **Description (40 words):** Eighteen statements about how you act, speak, lead, argue, question
  and plan. None mentions the Bible. The result names the figure whose recorded acts sit nearest,
  and shows the cited moments that place them there. It measures temperament, not virtue.
- **Share title:** Who in the Bible I am most like
- **Draft note:** This one is an unaudited draft. Every figure is placed by cited, recorded acts,
  and each reference has been checked against the text, but where a figure sits is still a
  judgement, and the statements have not been through the adversarial fairness audit the Theology
  Compass went through. Figures near the middle of the map, Jesus among them, come up more often
  than the rest for a reader with moderate answers; that is how the map is built, not a finding
  about you. No percentage is ever shown against a person. Neither end of any axis is the better
  one. Treat the result as a conversation starter, not a verdict.
- **The sentence that accompanies Jesus wherever he appears as a result** (the `note` field on his
  outcome, exported as `JESUS_NOTE`): Landing nearest Jesus here is about temperament as the
  Gospels record it, not a measure of holiness; every Christian is called to be like him in
  character, whatever their temperament.

## What the owner should know before playing it

1. **Jesus is on the list**, placed by the same rule as everyone else: eighteen cited acts from
   the four Gospels, nothing removed by the verifier, no coordinate moved. Three of his six values
   sit near the middle because the Gospels record him at both ends, and his evidence says so.
2. **The middle of the map is crowded, and that decides a lot of results.** A quick simulation of
   the final instrument with the Compass's thresholds: on moderate answer sheets Mary of Nazareth
   is named about 17% of the time, Jesus about 16%, Priscilla 10%, Mary Magdalene 9%, while Peter,
   Elijah, Nehemiah, Abigail, Paul and Deborah are each under 1%. About two named results in three
   are within the 10-unit tie rule. This is the verifier's first structural finding, measured. It
   needs an engineering or roster answer before launch; the editor changed no coordinate to hide it.
3. **Priscilla** has four "the text does not show this" cells of six and is the third most often
   named. The verifier suggests more of her record, or Barnabas or Gideon in her place. Neither has
   verified evidence on file, so the editor left the roster as verified.
4. **Thresholds** (tie 10, poor fit 45, central 10) are the Compass's, carried over untested.
   `maxDistance` is derived the Compass's way (largest distance between two figures, 132.9) and
   only sets bar length here, since `hideOutcomeScore: true` forbids printing a number.
5. The strategy's result sentences still say "tradition" ("No listed tradition is a close fit").
   That is the engineer's parameterisation job, named in the blueprint.
