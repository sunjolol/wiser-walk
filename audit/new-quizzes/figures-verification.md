# Figure roster: adversarial verification

Every reference in `figures.json` was opened in
`demos/sounds-like-scripture/work/verses-all.json` at `src: "webbe"` (World English Bible British
Edition) and read before it was judged. Nothing here was checked from memory. The corrected data is
`figures.verified.json`.

## Headline

| check | result |
|---|---|
| refs that resolve in the WEBBE file | 255 of 255 |
| quotations verbatim (exact substring of the cited verse range) | 45 of 45 |
| evidence items **supported** | 170 |
| evidence items **overstated** (reworded to what the text shows) | 76 |
| evidence items **not in the text** (removed) | 9 |
| **wrong reference** | 0 |
| "the text does not show this" cells, before | 14 |
| "the text does not show this" cells, after | 15 |
| coordinates changed | 14, across 10 figures |
| roster | 22 people plus Jesus; 10 women; 14 Old Testament, 9 New |

The citation work is honest: nothing points at a verse that does not exist, nothing points at the
wrong verse, and no quotation has been touched up. The failure mode in this file is not fabrication,
it is **reach** — 76 of 255 `did` sentences say slightly more than the verse they cite, almost
always by carrying in a detail from an adjacent verse that was not cited (the household in Genesis
12:5, the descent from fifty in Genesis 18:24-31, the arrow signal agreed at 1 Samuel 20:20-22) or
by adding an unstated intensifier ("straight into", "walked in on", "with nothing in hand", "in
front of everyone"). Each of those is small. On a page whose whole promise is that a reader can
click the reference and see the act, each is also exactly the thing a hostile reader screenshots.
They are reworded in the verified file, keeping the reference the verifier actually read.

Arguments from silence are the pattern worth naming: five items ("told no one", "without asking
anything about it", "made no charge back", "rather than raising it privately", "rather than
directing them herself") treated what the text does not say as evidence of temperament. Two were
load-bearing enough to remove; the rest are reworded to the act the verse records.

## Jesus, held to the strictest standard

Eighteen items, all from the four Gospels, all resolving, all three quotations verbatim. **Nothing
was removed and no coordinate moved.** Eight items are supported as written; ten are overstated and
reworded. The placement is honest about the both-ends cells, the `did` sentences import no virtue
words, and Mark 15:34 was rightly kept out.

Four things the editor must carry forward:

1. **Mark 8:32 says nothing about Jerusalem.** "He told the disciples what was coming in Jerusalem"
   is Matthew 16:21's wording; Mark 8:31 has the elders, chief priests and scribes, the killing and
   the rising, and no city. Reworded to the verse: "He spoke to them openly, and Peter took him and
   began to rebuke him."
2. **Mark 6:7 does not say "his own authority".** It says he gave them authority over the unclean
   spirits. "Without him" is inferred. Both fixed.
3. **John 21:15-17 only reads as restoration if the denial is in view**, and the denial is John
   18:25-27, which is not cited. Reworded to the three questions and the three commissions; if the
   editor wants the restoration reading on the page, the denial has to be cited too.
4. **Luke 23:34** stands in the WEBBE text on disk, and it is absent from a number of early
   manuscripts. Not a reason to drop it; a reason for the editor to know before a reader says so.

One naming point, not a data point: the axis key is `doubt`. The pole names ("Asks why" / "Takes it
on trust") are fine and the evidence never says he doubted, but the word `doubt` must not surface as
a label on his figure page or his card.

## Two structural findings the data cannot fix

**Jesus is the closest figure on the roster to dead centre** — 25.0 units from (50,50,50,50,50,50),
with Mary of Nazareth next at 27.4 and Priscilla at 31.6. Under a Euclidean nearest-neighbour
strategy the most central outcome wins for every moderate answer sheet, so this roster will tell a
large share of readers that they are most like Jesus. That is an artefact of the both-ends rule
(three of his coordinates sit near 50 because the Gospels record both ends) and of the honest 50s
elsewhere; it is not a claim the data is making. It needs an engineering answer before launch — the
Compass's own poor-fit hedge is the precedent: name nobody when nothing is close, and add the
equivalent of the 10-unit tie rule.

**Paul and Deborah sit 17.3 units apart**, the closest pair on the roster; Abraham/Mary Magdalene
and David/Martha are both at 27.8. Without a tie rule these pairs will swap on a single answer.

No axis has every figure on one side, so the blueprint's requirement holds. But the notes' own
"five on both sides at the strict threshold" no longer holds on **conflict-right**, which is down to
four after Priscilla's removal (Hannah 60 and Daniel 55 are the nearest reserves). The notes warned
that plan-right had no sixth in reserve; conflict-right is the axis that actually lost one.

**Priscilla should be reconsidered.** She now carries four "the text does not show this" cells out of
six, both remaining coordinates rest on one verse plus one greeting, and she is the third most
central figure on the roster, which under nearest-neighbour means she will be named often on almost
no evidence. Either more of her record goes in, or Barnabas and Gideon — cut with reasons in the
notes, and both firmer — take the slot.

## Smaller things the editor should know

- **One verse doing several jobs.** Acts 18:26 carried three of Priscilla's six axes (now two).
  John 11:16 carries Thomas's pace, voice and lead. Luke 10:40 carries four of Martha's six. Genesis
  18:23-25 is Abraham's voice-left and his conflict-right at once. It is not dishonest, but a figure
  page that prints the same verse four times under four headings will read as thin.
- **Two items, one fact.** John the Baptist's plan-right cites Matthew 3:4 and Mark 1:6, which are
  parallel accounts of the same clothing and diet. The coordinate (80) rests on one datum.
- **Acts of others counted as acts of the figure.** Genesis 39:22 is the prison keeper's act, Romans
  16:3-4 is Paul's greeting, 1 Samuel 25:33 was David's assessment (removed), Joshua 2:18 was the
  spies' condition (removed). The first two are kept, reworded so the page says whose words they are.
- **The notes and the data disagree once**: the notes discuss Mark 6:35-41 for Jesus's plan-right;
  the data cites Mark 6:38-41. The crowd, the lack of food and the late hour are in 6:35-36, so the
  reworded sentence stays inside 6:38-41.
- Every "the text does not show this" cell still satisfies all four conditions (single item, null
  ref, null toward, value 50), including the new Priscilla conflict cell.

## What the engine now does

An axis counts toward a match only where BOTH sides name a position: the figure, because the text
places them there, and the reader, because their score is outside the 41-59 band in which this
instrument says no pole is named. The mask is derived in `bible-figure.ts` from the `toward` fields
and the coordinate, never stored, so it cannot drift from the evidence:

- **none** — no cited act. The 50 beside it is the middle of a scale, not a place the person stood.
- **both** — cited acts point both ways and the coordinate stays inside the no-pole band.
- **shown** — the acts place the figure, and the coordinate says where. Acts pointing both ways
  still count as shown when their weight carried the coordinate out of the band.

A figure needs four of six shown axes to be a possible result (`minShownAxes`), and a reader must
have at least two axes in common with a figure before it is named rather than hedged
(`minMatchAxes`).
