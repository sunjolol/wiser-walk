# Review of `gifts-all-edit.md`: a Catholic theologian and an Orthodox priest, reading together

Scope: only what is new or changed in the editor's proposal (the six new gifts, the six replaced "others" statements, discernment, and all rewritten copy), judged as part of the whole nineteen. Read beside `site/src/lib/quizzes/spiritual-gifts.ts`, the first Catholic and Orthodox audit (`audit-catholic-orthodox.md`), `QUIZ-BLUEPRINTS.md` and the Compass's Gifts axis in `site/src/data/compass.json`. Nothing under `site/` was edited, nothing committed; this is the only file written.

The owner's decision (all gifts in) and the scope (nineteen, three each) are taken as settled and are not argued with anywhere below.

**What was opened for this review, so a finding can be checked:**

- Bible text with `node audit/tools/webbe.mjs`: Isaiah 11:2-3; 1 Corinthians 12:7-11, 12:31, 14:1, 14:12, 14:26-29; 1 Thessalonians 5:12-13 and 5:19-21; 1 John 4:1; 1 Timothy 4:14; 2 Timothy 1:6; James 5:14-16; Acts 19:6.
- Catechism of the Catholic Church 799, 800, 801 and 2003, on vatican.va. 801 reads: "No charism is exempt from being referred and submitted to the Church's shepherds." 800: charisms "are to be accepted with gratitude". 2003 names "the gift of miracles or of tongues" as charisms.
- Lumen Gentium 12, on vatican.va: "Extraordinary gifts are not to be sought after, nor are the fruits of apostolic labor to be presumptuously expected from their use; but judgment as to their genuinity and proper use belongs to those who are appointed leaders in the Church". Note for whoever cites it: the Vatican's English has no "rashly"; the Latin has *temere* (not opened here), and the Compass's audited sentence says "rashly". Reuse the Compass's sentence; do not quote the constitution on the page without opening it.
- NOT opened, given only as pointers for the citation pass: Ignatius of Loyola, *Spiritual Exercises*, the rules for the discernment of spirits; John Climacus, *Ladder*, Step 26; John Cassian, *Conferences* 2; Aquinas, *Summa* I-II q.111 a.4. Their existence and subject were checked against an encyclopedia article only.

**Verdict in one paragraph.** No blocker. Four majors and seven minors. The statements are better from this chair than we expected: asking what happened and what other people said is nearer to how our churches recognise a charism (by others, over time, under a pastor) than any self-rating would be, and the removal of the old discernment statement is exactly right. The majors are all in the copy, and they are one fault seen four times: the disagreement is drawn with two sides, and the third way of holding these gifts, which is the teaching of the Catholic and Orthodox churches and which the Compass's own audited summary already words, has no sentence; and the Church's shepherds appear in the intro and the result but are missing from the six pages where they matter most.

## Findings

### 1. MAJOR. The `disputed` note draws two sides where the Compass draws three

**Words objected to:** "Christians reading the same passages disagree about whether prophecy, healing, miracles, tongues, their interpretation and the word of knowledge are given today. This page takes no side ... A reader who believes these gifts are not given today can answer every statement truthfully and will most likely see "not found here" on those rows".

**Why.** The note gives one whole sentence to the reader who holds these gifts have ceased and none to the reader whose Church teaches that they are given, are to be received with gratitude, and are not to be sought. That is not a footnote to the Pentecostal position; it is a different way of holding the same "yes", and for an Orthodox priest it is the pastorally urgent half: a young convert who goes looking in himself for prophecy or miracles is the textbook road to what our tradition calls prelest, spiritual delusion. A quiz that asks fifteen statements about these gifts is, in practice, an invitation to look. We do not ask for the quiz to be changed (the owner has decided), and we do not ask the page to warn anyone off, which would take a side against 1 Corinthians 14:1 ("earnestly desire spiritual gifts", which the Pentecostal reviewer rightly wanted respected). We ask that the page DESCRIBE the difference, as it describes the other one. The Compass's Gifts axis already does, in a sentence that passed the twenty-reviewer audit: continuationists "differ on how eagerly to seek them: Pentecostals and charismatics seek them openly, while Catholic and Orthodox teaching receives them with gratitude and warns against seeking them rashly." The brief says to reuse that vocabulary. The editor reused the cessationist half and the healing clause and dropped this one.

**Replacement we would accept** (the second sentence is new; the rest is the editor's; 121 words):

> Christians reading the same passages disagree about whether prophecy, healing, miracles, tongues, their interpretation and the word of knowledge are given today. Those who believe they are given differ on how eagerly to seek them: Pentecostal and charismatic Christians seek them openly, while Catholic and Orthodox teaching receives them with gratitude and warns against seeking them rashly. This page takes no side: it quotes each passage, asks what has happened and what people have said, and calls no event a miracle, a healing or a word from God. A reader who believes these gifts are not given today can answer every statement truthfully and will most likely see “not found here” on those rows, which is a fact about the statements.

"Charismatic" here names people, as the Compass does; it is not the forbidden label "charismatic gifts". **[S6] itself PASSES unchanged** on all six pages: Catholics and Orthodox are truthfully inside its first half, and the editor's rule that all six pages carry the identical sentence is worth more than a third clause repeated six times. The `draftNote` can stay as it is provided this note is shown with the intro, as the editor specifies.

### 2. MAJOR. Only the prophecy page says anyone weighs anything; the other five say nothing

**Words objected to:** prophecy's summary has "Whether any of it was from God is for your church to weigh, not this page." Healing, miracles, tongues, interpreting tongues and the word of knowledge have no such sentence. Miracles ends "This page calls nothing a miracle itself", which says who does NOT judge and is silent on who does.

**Why.** Catechism 801: "No charism is exempt from being referred and submitted to the Church's shepherds." Lumen Gentium 12 says the same of "their genuinity and proper use". In Catholic practice the word "miracle" in particular is a judgement the Church makes, slowly, through her bishops; in Orthodox practice a person who believes he has healed or been given a word takes it to his priest or spiritual father and says nothing more about it. The intro and the result frame do send the reader to "your pastor or priest" (that PASSES, see below), but the gift pages are separate, indexable pages, and the five that most need the sentence are the five without it. Second point, on the prophecy sentence itself: "your church" alone reads as the congregation deciding among themselves, which is one polity's answer. 1 Thessalonians 5:12, in the chapter Paul's "test all things" comes from, speaks of "those who labour amongst you, and are over you in the Lord and admonish you" (opened on disk). "Those who lead it" is true of elders, pastors, priests and bishops alike and costs a Brethren or Baptist reader nothing.

**Replacement we would accept.** One sentence, identical on healing, miracles, tongues, interpreting tongues and the word of knowledge, placed immediately before [S6] (the editor's own evenness rule, applied once more):

> Whether anything that happened was this gift is for your church and those who lead it to weigh, not this page.

and prophecy's sentence becomes:

> Whether any of it was from God is for your church and those who lead it to weigh, not this page.

### 3. MAJOR. The new discernment statement scores the self-appointed watchman highest

**Words objected to:** "I have warned people about something they were being taught about God, and they later told me I was right."

**Why.** The object is now right (a teaching, not a person), and the removal of "I decide something is off about a person" is the best single change in the proposal. But the act that is scored is going round warning other people about their teachers on one's own judgement, and the confirmation comes from whoever agreed. Both of us know this parishioner. In a Catholic parish he warns people about the homily and the bishop; in an Orthodox one he is the online-formed zealot who warns everybody about everybody, and his friends tell him he is right. The editor half sees it ("the reader who warns against everything") and leaves it as the cost. From our chair it is more than a cost: confidence in one's own judgement against one's teachers, confirmed by one's own circle, is the very thing the gift exists to catch. The text itself has the weighing done by "the others", together and in order (1 Corinthians 14:29), not by one person broadcasting. A layperson noticing that something taught is wrong is real and good; the form it takes among us is to raise it, with the person concerned or with someone responsible, and to be found right by them.

**Replacement we would accept** (20 words, +1, sign re-derived: agreeing describes weighing what is taught and being confirmed by others):

> I have raised a worry about something being taught about God, and the people I took it to later agreed.

It keeps everything the brief asked for: an event, what others later said, silence on how the reader knew. "The people I took it to" can be a pastor, an elder, or the friend who was being taught, so a mother who warned her daughter off a cult still agrees truthfully. What changes is the verb: "warned" presumes the reader was right and had the standing to say so; "raised a worry ... took it to" reports the same event as something submitted to others.

### 4. MAJOR. The discernment summary closes with the cessationist's view and leaves out what the name chiefly means in our two traditions

**Words objected to:** the proposed summary, whose last two sentences are "They say nothing about how you knew, and they are not about first impressions of people. Christians who believe some gifts were only for the time of the apostles disagree about whether this is one of them."

**Why.** The editor declined to define the gift because a definition "is narrower than some churches teach", and then let the statements do the narrowing: all three are about vetting ideas, talks, books and teaching. For Catholics "discernment of spirits" is first of all the Ignatian sense, telling what is moving a soul, one's own or a directee's; for the Orthodox, *diakrisis* is the chief gift of the spiritual father, the discernment of thoughts. It is the one word of the nineteen that a Catholic hears weekly. A page headed "discernment" that knows only the book-vetting sense, and names one tradition's view of it and not ours, will read to a Catholic or Orthodox reader as not knowing the word. We agree the statements should NOT try to measure that second sense (it belongs to confessors and elders, and a self-report of it would be the fault of finding 3 squared). The page should simply say so.

**Replacement we would accept** (one sentence added before [S3]; everything else the editor's):

> The passage names the discerning of spirits and does not explain it. Paul tells the church to weigh what prophets say, “let the others discern” (1 Corinthians 14:29), and John writes, “test the spirits, whether they are of God” (1 John 4:1). These statements ask what has happened when you weighed something said or taught about God. They say nothing about how you knew, and they are not about first impressions of people. Catholic and Orthodox teaching also uses this name for telling what is moving a person’s own thoughts and desires, which is the work of a confessor or spiritual father; these statements do not ask about that. Christians who believe some gifts were only for the time of the apostles disagree about whether this is one of them.

This sentence names two traditions, so by the site's rule it needs its sources opened before the quiz leaves draft; the pointers are listed at the top of this file and none was opened here. If the citation pass cannot be done in time, the fallback that names nobody is acceptable: "Many Christians also use this name for telling what is moving a person’s own thoughts and desires, and take that to a confessor or spiritual father; these statements do not ask about that."

### 5. MINOR. "In church" shuts out every Catholic and Orthodox reader, and Catholic charismatics most of all

**Words objected to:** prophecy slot 3, "I have said something **in church** that I believed God had given me to say"; tongues slot 3, "**In church** I have spoken in words from no language I know, and someone else has said what they meant."

**Why.** In British English "in church" means during the service. In our services no layperson speaks of his own accord, so the statement presumes an open floor, which is one family of churches' order of worship. The Catholic charismatic renewal (in the Compass's own history line, Duquesne 1967) does all of this at a prayer meeting in the parish hall, and its members would truthfully answer "never in church". The editor's own interpretation slot 3 already says "People **at church**", which is the wider phrase: the church's life, not the liturgy.

**Replacement we would accept** (one word each; 15 and 20 words; signs unchanged, +1):

> I have said something at church that I believed God had given me to say.

> At church I have spoken in words from no language I know, and someone else has said what they meant.

### 6. MINOR. The tongues reverse item has the Latin hole the editor closed one slot further down

**Words objected to:** tongues slot 1, "Every word I have ever prayed has been in a language I know."

**Why.** The editor wrote "words nobody taught me" into slot 2 precisely so that "a memorised Latin or Church Slavonic prayer" could not score. Slot 1 still has the hole, from the other side. A Catholic who sings the Salve Regina or answers the Latin Mass, an Orthodox who prays in Church Slavonic or Greek, a Copt who prays in Coptic, none of them knowing the language, must honestly DISAGREE, and disagreeing with a reverse item adds two points to tongues. It names no headline on its own, but it moves a whole class of our readers from 0 to 33 on a row they have nothing on, and it is the kind of thing that gets screenshotted.

**Replacement we would accept** (14 words, -1, sign re-derived: agreeing says every prayer was in known or learnt words, which lowers the gift):

> Every word I have ever prayed was one I knew or had been taught.

A reader who has never prayed still agrees; a teenager knows his own words; the Latin-praying Catholic was taught his; only the reader slot 2 is looking for disagrees.

### 7. MINOR. Healing slot 3: "prayed with" builds the top of the row on the bedside

**Words objected to:** "More than once, someone I prayed **with** has recovered when nobody expected them to", and the editor's note that "the top needs ... praying with the person".

**Why.** The letter's own preposition is "for": "pray for one another, that you may be healed" (James 5:16, opened on disk). In both our churches the ordinary way of praying for the sick is at a distance: a name given for the Liturgy or a Mass intention, a rosary or an akathist at home; the person one sends to the bedside is the priest (James 5:14). A reader whose prayers for the sick have been answered more than once, and who has never stood at a bedside praying aloud, is capped by one preposition. The reverse item may keep "pray with them": there the bedside act is the point of the trade-off, and it PASSES (see below).

**Replacement we would accept** (14 words, +1): 

> More than once, someone I prayed for has recovered when nobody expected them to.

It stays distinct from slot 1, which is what people TOLD the reader; this one is repetition and the unexpected.

### 8. MINOR. The word of knowledge is measured as Bible recall only

**Words objected to:** summary, "Some Christians take it as having **what the Bible teaches** ready to say when it is needed"; slot 3, "When a question **about the Bible** comes up, I am the one asking rather than answering."

**Why.** For a Catholic or Orthodox reader, knowing the faith is knowing Scripture, the creeds, the liturgy, the Fathers and the catechism together, and the classical Catholic reading of "the word of knowledge" (Aquinas; pointer only, not opened) is the ability to explain the faith, not to find the verse. A catechist who always has the Church's answer ready but does not quote chapter and verse is, on this row, a newcomer. Slot 1 ("quoting the part of the Bible people were trying to remember") can stay: it is one real act and our readers can do it too. Widening the other two costs a Protestant nothing, since for him "what Christians believe" is what the Bible teaches.

**Replacements we would accept:** summary clause, "Some Christians take it as having Christian teaching ready to say when it is needed, others as ..."; slot 3 (17 words, -1, sign unchanged):

> When a question about what Christians believe comes up, I am the one asking rather than answering.

### 9. MINOR. Isaiah 11: the collision is now double, and the two pages that collide do not carry the sentence

**Words objected to:** the display pair "wisdom" and "the word of knowledge", and both summaries.

**Why.** Isaiah 11:2 on disk: "the spirit of **wisdom** and understanding ... the spirit of **knowledge** and of the fear of the LORD". With knowledge added, two of the nineteen rows now share a name with two of the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit that a Catholic was taught he received at confirmation. The intro's sentence (from the first audit, and it PASSES) is only in the quiz flow; the gift pages stand alone. The display name "the word of knowledge" is well chosen and PASSES. But it now sits beside a bare "wisdom" from the same verse, and the file's own precedent applies: "the gift of faith" was chosen because a reader low on a bare "faith" hears "you do not believe". A Catholic low on a bare "wisdom" hears the same kind of thing.

**Replacements we would accept.** Display name `wisdom` becomes "the word of wisdom" (key and slug unchanged). One sentence in each summary, before [S3] or [S6]:

> This is not the wisdom Catholic teaching counts among the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit (Isaiah 11:2); this page is about Paul’s list.

> This is not the knowledge Catholic teaching counts among the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit (Isaiah 11:2); this page is about Paul’s list.

### 10. MINOR. The two shortest strings still send the reader to peers only

**Words objected to:** `description`, "Scripture gives no test for gifts, so check the result with **people who know you**"; `introShort`, "ask **the people who have watched you serve**" (kept "unchanged ... still true").

**Why.** The first audit's finding G2 was fixed in the long intro and the result, and that fix PASSES. These two strings are the ones that travel furthest (the search result, the card, the line above the start button on a phone), and the description now names "prophecy, healing and tongues" in the same breath. With those in, the peer-only form is the Protestant default the long copy has already corrected.

**Replacements we would accept:** description's last sentence, "Scripture gives no test for gifts, so take the result to your pastor or priest and to people who know you."; introShort, "Scripture lists gifts and gives no test for finding which are yours; ask your pastor or priest and the people who have watched you serve." `flat` may stay as it is: it reports that nothing stood out.

### 11. MINOR. The share line (the editor's item S20) should be a condition of wiring, not an option

**Words objected to:** `shareTitle` unchanged, "Where my answers pointed: spiritual gifts", over a top three that can now read "miracles, healing, prophecy".

**Why.** The editor saw it and passed it to the engineer as one option. From our chair it is the place where everything careful on the page falls away: the pastor-or-priest sentence cannot travel with a card, and a card that says "miracles" in a group chat is the opposite of how both our traditions teach a person to carry such a thing (quietly, and to one's priest). We do not ask for the six to be hidden from the card; that would treat them as lesser, which the owner has ruled out.

**Replacement we would accept:** the editor's own sense, in one line that fits all nineteen: `shareTitle` "What my answers pointed to, still to be checked with people who know me". If the card has room for one more line it should be the first sentence of the result note's last step: "Take it to your pastor or priest."
