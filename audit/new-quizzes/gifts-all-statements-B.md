# Gifts quiz, all gifts in: Drafter B (from the text outward)

Status: **a draft for the critics and the editor. Nothing here is wired, nothing under `site/` was touched, nothing was committed.** Drafter A's file was not read.

Method, as briefed. For each gift I opened every place Acts and the letters record it (`node audit/tools/webbe.mjs`, the World English Bible British Edition on disk), wrote down what a bystander in that scene could have seen or heard (what was said, what changed, what people did next, who tested it and how), and only then looked for a present-day pattern in plain words that a reader could report about themselves. Every quotation in this file was machine-checked as a verbatim substring of the verse its reference names (107 of 107; see the appendix). Every word count was machine-counted. The longest statement offered is 20 words.

Direction: **+1** agreeing raises the gift, **-1** agreeing lowers it. **O** marks an "others" statement: what other people bring to the reader, or have said or done in answer to them.

## Four things the editor should know before reading the candidates

1. **The count in job 2 is seven and six, not eight and five.** Of the thirteen gifts now in `spiritual-gifts.ts`, seven have an "others" statement (serving, teaching, encouraging, administration, wisdom, discernment, shepherding) and six have none (giving, leading, mercy, faith, evangelism, hospitality). The audit said eight and five because it counted before its own fix G8 replaced leading's "People look at me when a decision has to be made" with "When nobody will decide, I make the decision for the group." That fix was right (three gifts shared the act of being consulted), and it cost leading its only "others" statement.
2. **A reverse item built on a condition has a hole in it.** "When somebody is ill, I say I will pray and do it later" is disagreed with by a reader who prays on the spot and ALSO by a reader who never prays, and disagreeing raises the gift. The first editor found the same hole in wisdom ("someone who is simply never asked will disagree"). So for the six new gifts I preferred two shapes of reverse item that do not have it: a real trade-off in which the other end is a different strength ("bring them a meal"), and a plain report in which agreeing is the ordinary Christian's honest answer ("things I hear about from others, not things I have been part of"). Where a conditional reverse is offered anyway, the table says so.
3. **A cessationist who prays for sick friends can honestly agree with "People have told me they got better after I prayed for them."** That is the brief's own example and it is a fair statement, but it means the healing row can rise for a reader who holds that the gift has ceased and that God answers prayer. The statements cannot tell those two apart, and no wording can. The healing summary below therefore says so in one sentence, and says that both sides hold that God heals in answer to prayer, which is the Compass's own audited wording for the cessationist position ("They affirm that God still heals and answers prayer").
4. **Rule 6 and tongues.** Paul says the one who speaks in another language "speaks not to men, but to God" (1 Corinthians 14:2). So every honest statement about it is a statement about praying. None below counts how much, how long or how fervently anyone prays; each asks what the praying is like or what happened. That is the most the rule allows here, and the editor should say so to the owner in one plain sentence rather than pretend the rule is untouched.

# Part 1. The six added gifts

## 1. The word of knowledge (`knowledge`)

**Display name:** the word of knowledge (the translation's own words; there is no more familiar one).

### What the text shows

| place | what a bystander could observe |
|---|---|
| 1 Corinthians 12:8 | The only place the phrase occurs in the whole Bible on disk (`--grep "word of knowledge"`: one verse). It is "the word of" knowledge: something said. Nothing describes it. |
| 1 Corinthians 14:6 | Paul lists four ways he might speak so as to profit them: "either by way of revelation, or of knowledge, or of prophesying, or of teaching". Knowledge is named beside teaching and beside revelation, as a different thing from both. |
| 1 Corinthians 1:4-7 | The Corinthians were enriched "in all speech and all knowledge", so that they "come behind in no gift". Knowledge and speech are paired, and counted among their gifts. |
| Romans 15:14 | The Romans are "filled with all knowledge, able also to admonish others". What an onlooker sees: people who know the thing, and therefore can put one another right. |
| 1 Corinthians 8:1-11 | Knowledge here is knowing a fact of the faith (an idol is nothing) and acting on it in front of others. Its cost is in the text: "Knowledge puffs up, but love builds up." |
| 2 Corinthians 11:6 | Paul of himself: unskilled in speech, not unskilled in knowledge. Knowing is separate from being good at saying it. |
| Acts 18:24-28 | Apollos, "mighty in the Scriptures", publicly showing by the Scriptures that Jesus was the Christ. Luke does not call it this gift. |
| Acts 5:3-4 | Peter states what Ananias had done with the money. Luke records what Peter said and not how he knew, and does not name a gift. |

Christians explain this gift in two ways, and the text settles neither: as knowing what God has made known and supplying it when it is needed, and as being given a fact about a person or a situation that nobody had told you. The statements below serve one reading each, and the summary says both exist without saying who holds which, because I could not open a source for that on disk and will not name one from memory.

**Neighbours.** Teaching is making a thing understood and checking it landed; knowledge here is having the fact and where it is written. Prophecy is a message delivered and weighed; K2 and K3 are a fact, not a message. Discernment weighs what others claim; these do not weigh anything. Acts 5:1-4 already stands under discernment and should stay in one place only (see Part 3).

### Listing passage, verbatim

- 1 Corinthians 12:8: "and to another the word of knowledge according to the same Spirit"

### Summary for the gift's page

> The passage names a word of knowledge, something said, and nowhere describes it. Some Christians take it as supplying what God has made known at the moment it is needed, others as being given a fact that nobody had told you, and these statements ask about both. Many Christians hold that this gift is given today, to be welcomed and tested; many hold that it was given for the time of the apostles.

### Recorded in the early church

Neither Acts nor the letters call any recorded act a word of knowledge, and the page should say that sentence first. These are the places where Paul speaks of knowledge among the churches' gifts:

- **1 Corinthians 1:4-7.** Paul thanks God that the Corinthians were enriched in Christ in all speech and all knowledge, so that they come behind in no gift.
- **1 Corinthians 14:6.** Paul asks what he would profit them by coming with other languages unless he spoke by way of revelation, or of knowledge, or of prophesying, or of teaching.
- **Romans 15:14.** Paul writes that he is persuaded the Christians at Rome are full of goodness, filled with all knowledge, and able also to admonish others.

Not offered: 1 Corinthians 13:8 ("Where there is knowledge, it will be done away with"). It is one of the passages the two sides argue from (the Compass lists 13:8-12), so printing it as an "act" would be taking a turn in the argument.

## 2. Healing (`healing`)

**Display name:** healing. The translation says "gifts of healings", both words plural.

### What the text shows

| place | what a bystander could observe |
|---|---|
| Acts 3:1-12, 3:16 | Peter speaks to a man lame from birth in the name of Jesus Christ, takes his hand; the man walks and leaps; people who knew him as the beggar at the gate recognise him. Peter at once refuses the credit: "as though by our own power or godliness we had made him walk". |
| Acts 4:14-16, 4:22 | It is tested by hostile people: the council see the man standing there, "could say nothing against it", and say "we can’t deny it". Luke gives the man's age, over forty. |
| Acts 5:12-16 | People carry their sick into the street where Peter will pass; crowds bring the sick from other towns. The sick are BROUGHT to particular people. |
| Acts 9:32-35 | Peter to Aeneas, eight years bedridden: "Jesus Christ heals you". He gets up at once. The town sees him and turns to the Lord. |
| Acts 9:38 | Disciples send two men to fetch Peter, imploring him not to delay. |
| Acts 14:8-15 | Paul tells a man who had never walked to stand; he leaps up; the crowd credits Paul and Barnabas, who tear their clothes and say "We also are men of the same nature as you". |
| Acts 28:7-9 | Paul goes in to a sick man, prays, lays hands on him, heals him; then the rest of the island's sick come. |
| James 5:14-16 | The sick person calls the elders, who pray over him and anoint him with oil; "pray for one another, that you may be healed". |
| 2 Timothy 4:20; Philippians 2:25-27; 1 Timothy 5:23 | The same Paul leaves Trophimus sick, nearly loses Epaphroditus, and advises Timothy about his stomach. Healing is not recorded as happening every time. |

What repeats: it happens there and then, at a word or with prayer and hands laid on; the sick are brought, or the person is sent for; onlookers who knew the sick person can see the change; the one through whom it happened says it was not his doing.

### Listing passages, verbatim

- 1 Corinthians 12:9: "and to another gifts of healings by the same Spirit"
- 1 Corinthians 12:28: "then gifts of healings"
- 1 Corinthians 12:30: "Do all have gifts of healings?"

### Summary for the gift's page

> The translation quoted here says gifts of healings, with both words in the plural. In Acts the sick are healed at a word, or with prayer and the laying on of hands, and Peter asks the crowd why they stare “as though by our own power or godliness we had made him walk” (Acts 3:12). These statements ask only what has happened and what people have said, and they cannot tell this gift apart from an answer to prayer. Many Christians hold that this gift is given today, to be welcomed and tested; many hold that it was given for the time of the apostles; both hold that God heals in answer to prayer.

### Recorded in the early church

- **Acts 3:1-12.** At the temple gate Peter told a man lame from birth, in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, to get up and walk, and took him by the right hand; his feet and ankle bones received strength and he went into the temple walking, leaping and praising God. The people recognised him and ran together, and Peter asked why they stared as though he and John had made the man walk by their own power or godliness.
- **Acts 9:32-35.** At Lydda Peter found Aeneas, bedridden for eight years because he was paralysed, told him that Jesus Christ healed him, and told him to get up and make his bed. He got up immediately, and all who lived at Lydda and in Sharon saw him and turned to the Lord.
- **Acts 28:7-9.** On Malta the father of Publius, the chief man of the island, lay sick with fever and dysentery. Paul went in to him, prayed, laid his hands on him and healed him, and then the rest on the island who had diseases came and were cured.
- **Acts 5:12-16.** Many signs and wonders were done among the people by the hands of the apostles. The sick were carried into the streets so that Peter’s shadow might fall on some of them, crowds from the cities around Jerusalem brought the sick and those tormented by unclean spirits, and they were all healed.

For the editor to weigh, not offered as an act: 2 Timothy 4:20 ("I left Trophimus at Miletus sick"). It is true, neutral and in the text, and a reader who has prayed and seen no change would be helped by it; but placed under "what it has looked like" it could read as the site arguing. If it goes anywhere it belongs in the low-row sentence.

**Display name:** prophecy.

|---|---|

| 1 Corinthians 14:24-25 | An outsider comes in and "the secrets of his heart are revealed". |

Spare: "When I believe God has given me something to say, I ask others to weigh it." (+1, 16). 1 Corinthians 14:29, 32. It is the most Pauline sentence here, and the weakest as measurement: it scores good order, and a reader with the pattern and poor order would be marked down.

**My three:** P1, P2 (O), P4.

**The honest difficulty.** P1, P2 and P3 all carry the words "I believed God" or "from God". **Neighbours.** Encouraging pushes someone towards what they are avoiding and tells them what they are capable of; nothing in it claims to come from God. The word of knowledge is a fact; these are messages. Wisdom is being asked which option to take. Discernment is the other half of 1 Corinthians 14:29: prophecy speaks, discernment weighs, and no statement here weighs anything.

Both verbatim checks passed.

### Summary for the gift's page

> Paul says what it does: “he who prophesies speaks to men for their edification, exhortation, and consolation” (1 Corinthians 14:3), and he has it weighed: “let the others discern” (14:29). In Acts prophets also say what is coming (Acts 11:28). These statements ask whether you have said what you believed God gave you to say, and what came of it. Many Christians hold that this gift is given today, to be welcomed and tested; many hold that it was given for the time of the apostles.

### Recorded in the early church

- **Acts 11:27-30.** Prophets came down from Jerusalem to Antioch, and one of them, Agabus, stood up and indicated by the Spirit that a great famine was coming, which Luke says happened in the days of Claudius. The disciples each determined to send relief to the brothers in Judea, and sent it by Barnabas and Saul.
- **Acts 13:1-3.** In the church at Antioch there were prophets and teachers. As they served the Lord and fasted, the Holy Spirit said to separate Barnabas and Saul for the work to which he had called them; they fasted, prayed, laid their hands on them and sent them away.
- **Acts 21:9-14.** Philip had four virgin daughters who prophesied. Agabus came down from Judea, bound his own feet and hands with Paul’s belt, and said that the Holy Spirit said the Jews at Jerusalem would bind its owner so and hand him over to the Gentiles. The company begged Paul not to go; he would not be persuaded, and they ceased, saying that the Lord’s will be done.
- **1 Timothy 1:18.** Paul commits his instruction to Timothy according to the prophecies which had been given about him before, so that by them he may wage the good warfare.

Acts 21:8 (Philip the evangelist) is already evangelism's, so this range starts at verse 9.

# Part 3. Discernment

## The ruling, from the passage

1 Corinthians 12:10 says "discerning of spirits". 14:29 gives the same verb an object: two or three prophets speak, and "let the others discern". 1 John 4:1: "test the spirits, whether they are of God, because many false prophets have gone out into the world", and verses 2-3 give the test, which is what the spirit confesses about Jesus Christ. 1 Thessalonians 5:20-21: "Don’t despise prophecies. Test all things, and hold firmly that which is good." Acts 17:11: the Beroeans were "examining the Scriptures daily to see whether these things were so". Revelation 2:2: the Ephesians "have tested those who call themselves apostles, and they are not, and found them false".

In every one of these the thing weighed is a message, a teaching or a claim to speak for God, and what a bystander sees is the outcome: it was held or it was refused, and someone said so. In none of them is it a first impression of a person's character. So **"I decide something is off about a person before I could say what it is" goes.** The two reviewers who wanted it gone were right about its object (people), and the third was right that a perception nobody taught you may be how the gift works. Both can be satisfied, because the text records WHAT was discerned and never HOW. A statement that reports the event and is silent on the means is one a Pentecostal can agree with because God showed him, and a cessationist because she opened her Bible.

## Three candidates for the replacement (all +1)

| id | statement | words | O | pattern |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Da | I have warned people about a teaching or a claim that was later shown to be false. | 17 | | Revelation 2:2 ("tested ... and found them false"); 1 John 4:1. What happened. Silent on how the reader knew. |
| Db | People have told me later that I was right to warn them about a teaching or a claim. | 18 | O | The same event, with what others later said, as the brief hoped for. |
| Dc | I have said that a popular teaching was wrong while the people around me still welcomed it. | 17 | | 2 Corinthians 11:4 ("you put up with that well enough"); Galatians 2:11-14. The cost is in the sentence, but there is no outcome in it, so a reader who simply likes to disagree will agree strongly. |

**My pick depends on the next finding.** If statement 20 is reworded as below, discernment already has its one "others" statement, so take **Da**. If statement 20 is left alone or dropped, take **Db**.

## The other two statements, checked for the same fault

- **Statement 20, "Friends ask me whether someone can be trusted before they rely on them." It has the same fault.** Its object is a person's character. Proposed: **"Friends send me a talk or a book and ask whether it can be trusted."** (+1, 15 words, O). Acts 17:11; 1 Corinthians 14:29. Plain, present-day, and the object is now a message. It also moves discernment further from wisdom: wisdom is asked which of two options to take, discernment whether a message holds up.
- **Statement 9, "I take a new idea as it sounds rather than look into where it came from." (-1). It stands.** Its object is an idea, and "where it came from" is 1 John 4:1's own question ("whether they are of God").

A prejudiced reader was the psychometrician's worry. Da, Db and the new 20 give that reader nothing to agree with: none of them is about sizing up a person.

## The recorded acts

All three Acts scenes name a person (Simon, Ananias, Elymas), so I checked them against the ruling. None is a first impression. Peter answers what Simon had just said (Acts 8:18-23); Elymas is called "a false prophet" and was opposing the message (13:6-10), which is 1 John 4:1's case exactly; Ananias had made a claim by his act, and Peter names what was behind it (5:1-4). They stay. **Acts 5:1-4 should appear under discernment only**: the cessationist reviewer noted that Pentecostal teaching files it under the word of knowledge, Luke names no gift, and printing one act under two gifts would break the rule about neighbours. Acts 17:11 stays as the act of testing.

## The summary

"These statements can see only that everyday pattern" is no longer right: with the fix the statements are about weighing teaching and claims, which is what the passage names. Proposed:

> The passage says discerning of spirits: telling what is behind a message, a teaching or a claim to speak for God. Paul has prophecy weighed, “let the others discern” (1 Corinthians 14:29), and John writes “test the spirits, whether they are of God” (1 John 4:1). These statements ask what has happened when you weighed such things and say nothing about how you knew. Christians who hold that some gifts were given only for the time of the apostles differ on whether this is one of them.
