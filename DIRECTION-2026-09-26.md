# What to build next: the Bible, one door at a time (2026-09-26)

Written in answer to the owner's question of 2026-09-26: keep expanding saints and quizzes, or build something new
that makes the Bible engaging in a way that "almost disguises itself" (not a traditional Bible study tool: that space
is saturated). Context: the site is about three weeks old; the goal is to rank on search engines and AI answers and
become a real hub; a super chat to Father Moses had him take the Bible figure quiz on stream (he got Deborah), which
brought about 40 visitors and one sign-up.

## I think the idea you're reaching for is already half-built

Look at what the quizzes really are. The Psalm quiz is a door into the Psalms. "How You Love" will be a door into
1 Corinthians 13. The gifts test is Romans 12 and 1 Corinthians 12, and the figure quiz is the Bible's people. Nobody
starts one of them to "study the Bible". Yet each one ends on a page of Scripture, explained by the saints. That is
the disguise you're describing.

So I wouldn't build a Bible tool beside the quizzes. I'd make this the named idea of the whole site: **the Bible,
entered through your own life, one door at a time.** Every door is personal: a quiz, a question, a painting. Every
door opens onto a rich page of Scripture. Over time those pages add up to an enriched Bible. Few people would sit down
to study it, but many would wander through it.

## Four ways in, best first

1. **The Bible through the great paintings.** Each famous story gets a page built like a figure page, but led by
   art. Think the Prodigal Son, Jacob wrestling the angel, the road to Emmaus. The page tells the story in plain words
   and gives the text itself. Then come 8 to 12 public-domain paintings from across 600 years, showing what each
   artist noticed. It adds what the Fathers said and what people get wrong about the story. Every picture is large,
   credited and free to use, so a pastor or teacher can put it on a Sunday slide.
   - **Why it's new:** Bible sites treat art as decoration, and art sites treat the Bible as a caption. Nobody walks
     you through a story with the masters as guides.
   - **Why it fits:** it's the visual experience you want, and it's what the site already does best. People come for
     a painting and leave having read Luke 15.
   - **Why it helps search:** church sites link to free, credited Bible art, and links are what a new domain needs
     most. Tissot's 350 paintings of Christ's life (public domain, in order) give it a ready spine.

2. **"Is it in the Bible?"** One short page per saying, each with a clear verdict:
   - "God helps those who help themselves": no, that's Aesop and Ben Franklin.
   - "Money is the root of all evil": almost, but the verse says *the love of* money.
   - The reverse too: phrases people don't know are Bible, like "by the skin of your teeth".

   People type these exact questions into Google and ChatGPT every day, and the competition is thin. A one-line
   verdict with its source is exactly what AI answers quote. These pages would also give the game (Sounds Like
   Scripture) the reveal pages it never got. This is the cheapest of the four, because the pages are short and the
   site's scripts already check wording against the source texts.

3. **More doors like the Psalm quiz**, whose recipe the owner loved. For example: "Which line of the Lord's Prayer do
   you need right now?" Cyprian, Tertullian and Gregory of Nyssa each wrote whole treatises on the prayer. These come
   after How You Love, and one at a time.

4. **One verse, every tradition in its own words.** Take Matthew 16:18, John 6, James 2:24 or Romans 9. Show what
   Catholics, Orthodox, Lutherans, Reformed and Baptists each say it means, quoted from their own catechisms and
   confessions. It's less fun, but it's the Compass's fairness promise applied to Scripture. It's also what Father
   Moses's audience argues about.

**What I'd avoid:** a Bible reader, reading plans or streaks (YouVersion owns that, and it counts devotion). Also maps
and timelines (BibleProject and Logos own those), verse of the day, and any AI chat.

## The order I'd do things in

Search takes months to trust a new site. It rewards being clearly the best at a few things more than doing many
things. The strongest cluster is the saints: about 50 pages deeper than anything ranking for those names. Demand is
real (Nicholas alone gets about a million Wikipedia views a year), and feast days bring a spike every year.

1. **Saints to 40**, batch by batch as usage allows, with Nicholas up before Advent. Then two pieces that tie the
   saints together:
   - A "How to choose a patron saint" guide. Catholics picking a confirmation name and Orthodox converts picking a
     patron search for this a lot.
   - At the end, a "Which saint are you?" quiz across the whole hub. That's a popular search, and every result would
     land on a page nobody else has.
2. **Pilot the new ideas cheaply**, with one real example of each before anything is built: one art-led story page
   (the Prodigal Son) and ten "Is it in the Bible?" verdicts. The owner judges them with the real thing in front of
   him. That's the lesson from Golden Chain and Same Move.
3. **Quiz #5, How You Love**, as the next quiz.
4. **Articles** only where they tie a cluster together, like the patron-saint guide. Standalone articles would compete
   with GotQuestions and Crosswalk and won't rank yet.
5. **Games later**, once there's an audience.

## On Father Moses

That's the most useful thing we've learned about what spreads the site. One super chat brought about 40 people and a
sign-up. The laugh came from a surprising result: a very built Orthodox priest getting Deborah. The figure quiz is
clearly the most stream-friendly piece, because it's instant, surprising and funny.

Search will take months to pay off, and a few more moments like that would bridge the gap. Each one is a five-minute,
one-off task, not building a following.
