# structured-data

The structured data on wiserwalk.com is unusually clean for a three week old site: I ran all nine page templates through the official Schema Markup Validator at validator.schema.org and eight came back with zero errors and zero warnings. BreadcrumbList is present, absolute and correct on every indexable template, the WebSite plus Organization graph on the home page resolves properly through its @id references and is exactly what Google's site-name system asks for, the favicon set exceeds Google's current rule, and all 42 og:image files are 1200x630, absolute, reachable and under 210 KB with twitter:card set to summary_large_image everywhere. The weakness is that the markup stops at the minimum. Article markup on the eleven articles carries only headline, description, datePublished, url and a bare publisher: no image, no author, no dateModified, which is most of what Google's Article documentation recommends and most of what Google Discover keys off. The four reference templates (axis, tradition, figure, method) are typed Article with nothing but headline, description and url, while their og:type says website, so the two descriptions of the same page disagree. One page has a real validation error: numberOfQuestions on /q/theology-compass/ is not a schema.org property at all (schema.org/numberOfQuestions is a 404) and the Quiz type there claims eighteen questions that are nowhere in the page's HTML, which is the one thing on the site that brushes against Google's rule against marking up content readers cannot see. The single most important thing is the one surface the site is not using at all: roughly sixty public domain Dore engravings and paintings, each with its own descriptive alt text on a topically matched page, and a complete provenance file already sitting in the repo, with no ImageObject markup anywhere. Google Images is a separate index with far less competition than web search, it is the one place a zero backlink domain can realistically rank within weeks, and the honest data to claim it already exists. Nothing here needs invented data, and I found nothing that looks spammy today, though I have listed five markup types that would be a waste of time or an actual risk if anyone adds them later.

## 1. [high impact / medium effort] No image metadata anywhere, so about sixty public-domain artworks are invisible to Google Images
**Evidence:** Not one ImageObject block exists on the site: I extracted every JSON-LD block from nine live templates and found only Article, BreadcrumbList, Quiz, CollectionPage, AboutPage, WebPage and WebSite/Organization. Meanwhile the site is full of distinctive, uniquely-alt-texted public-domain art: /figure/david/ serves /img/figures/david.jpg with alt "Gustave Doré's engraving of David standing crowned and bowed as he mourns Absalom", and /articles/how-to-become-more-grateful/ serves a 1200x996 Millet with alt "Two field workers stand with heads bowed in prayer at dusk" and a visible credit line "Jean-François Millet, The Angelus, 1857-59". site/public/img/CREDITS.md already records creator, plate title and the exact Wikimedia Commons filename for every engraving and painting. Google's image metadata documentation requires contentUrl plus at least one of creator, creditText, copyrightNotice or license, and says "If you're using structured data to specify an image, you must include the license property for your image to be eligible to be shown with the Licensable badge." Image metadata is still in the current structured-data gallery (I confirmed the string is present in the live gallery page's feature list).

**Do:** Make Article.image an ImageObject rather than leaving it absent, so one block satisfies both Article's image recommendation and the image metadata feature. For /articles/how-to-become-more-grateful/: "image": { "@type": "ImageObject", "contentUrl": "https://wiserwalk.com/img/articles/how-to-become-more-grateful.jpg", "width": 1200, "height": 996, "creator": { "@type": "Person", "name": "Jean-François Millet" }, "creditText": "Jean-François Millet, The Angelus, 1857-59 (Wikimedia Commons)", "license": "https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/" }. Do the same on figure pages from the CREDITS.md rows, with creator "Gustave Doré". Drive it from data, not by hand: add imageCreator and imageLicense (or a boolean imagePublicDomain) to the articles schema in site/src/content.config.ts and to the figure data, and emit the creator/creditText/license trio ONLY where the source is genuinely public domain. This matters because CREDITS.md line 3 says the owner holds an Envato Elements subscription and may swap in licensed photographs, and lines 98-100 list Unsplash photos: neither is public domain, and marking them with the Public Domain Mark would be a false licence claim. Use Public Domain Mark 1.0, not CC0, because the site did not create these works and cannot dedicate them.

**Where:** site/src/pages/articles/[slug].astro:71-79; site/src/pages/figure/[slug].astro:204-212; site/src/content.config.ts (articles schema, add imageCreator/imageLicense); site/public/img/CREDITS.md (the source data, already complete)

## 2. [high impact / small effort] Article markup is missing image, author and dateModified, which is most of what Google recommends
**Evidence:** The live Article block on /articles/how-to-become-more-grateful/ is exactly: @context, @type Article, headline, description, datePublished "2026-09-03", url, and publisher { @type Organization, name "Wiser Walk" }. Google's Article documentation says "There are no required properties" and then lists as recommended: author ("Person or Organization"), author.name, author.url, dateModified, datePublished, headline and image. So three of the seven recommended properties are absent, and the publisher has no logo and no link to the site's own Organization node. Google's own summary of what the markup buys you is "show better title text, images, and date information for the article in search results". The site has no dateModified because site/src/content.config.ts defines published but no updated or modified field. On authorship: Google states plainly "Use the Person type for people, and the Organization type for organizations", so an Organization author is allowed and keeps the owner faceless as he wants.

**Do:** In site/src/pages/articles/[slug].astro:71-79 add: "author": { "@type": "Organization", "name": "Wiser Walk", "url": "https://wiserwalk.com/about/" }, upgrade publisher to { "@type": "Organization", "name": "Wiser Walk", "url": "https://wiserwalk.com/", "logo": { "@type": "ImageObject", "url": "https://wiserwalk.com/icon-512.png", "width": 512, "height": 512 } }, add "inLanguage": "en-GB", add "isPartOf": { "@id": "https://wiserwalk.com/#website" }, and add the ImageObject from the finding above. For dates, add updated: z.coerce.date().optional() to the articles schema in site/src/content.config.ts and emit "dateModified": (article.data.updated ?? article.data.published) so the value is always real and never a build timestamp pretending to be an edit. Google's byline guidance says "Ensure that the date (and optional time and timezone) match between the equivalent user-visible and structured values", so also wrap the visible line (it currently renders as plain text "Published 3 September 2026") in <time datetime="2026-09-03">. Consider @type "BlogPosting" instead of "Article": Google accepts both and its specificity guideline prefers the most precise type, though the gain is small.

**Where:** site/src/pages/articles/[slug].astro:71-79; site/src/content.config.ts (articles schema, no updated field today)

## 3. [medium impact / small effort / VISIBLE] Three article lead images are below Google's 1200px width, which blocks the large Discover image
**Evidence:** Google's Discover documentation gives the image requirement as "At least 1200 px wide" with "High resolution of more than 300,000 total pixels", enabled by max-image-preview:large (which the site correctly sets on every indexable page). Three fall short: how-to-become-more-humble.jpg is 511x768, how-to-be-content.jpg is 1100x864, how-to-love-difficult-people.jpg is 1100x1358. The other eight are 1200 wide or better. The og:image files are all exactly 1200x630 and therefore sit on the Discover minimum rather than above it. I also noticed the article band image is the only <img> on the page with no width and height attributes (compare the same page's other images, which all carry them).

**Do:** Re-download larger scans of those three plates and paintings from Wikimedia Commons, which holds much higher resolution originals of all of them, and re-export at 1600px wide or more, keeping the same filenames so nothing else changes. Do the same for dore-paul.jpg, which CREDITS.md already marks as needing it. While in that file, add width and height to the band image in the article template so the lead image does not shift the layout as it loads. Note honestly: Discover is not something a three week old domain should expect, and Google says structured data is not required for it, but these are the prerequisites and they are cheap to meet now rather than later.

**Where:** site/public/img/articles/how-to-become-more-humble.jpg (511x768), how-to-be-content.jpg (1100x864), how-to-love-difficult-people.jpg (1100x1358); site/public/img/dore-paul.jpg (565x696); site/public/img/CREDITS.md line 3 and the Doré table

## 4. [medium impact / small effort] The Organization node has no sameAs, so nothing machine-readable ties the site to a real entity
**Evidence:** The validated graph from the home page is: WebSite (name, alternateName x2, url, description, inLanguage, publisher) linked by @id to Organization (name, url, logo ImageObject 512x512). That is all. Google's Organization documentation lists sameAs, description, email, contactPoint and foundingDate among its recommended properties and describes sameAs as links to "social/review profiles". The site does have two genuine owned profiles: the Ko-fi page, which the live /support/ page links to as https://ko-fi.com/sunjolol, and the public repository github.com/sunjolol/wiser-walk named in the project notes. On a domain with no backlinks, sameAs is close to the only structured evidence Google gets that this is a real, owned thing rather than an abandoned generator. Note the current markup is not wrong, just thin: the validator reported zero errors and zero warnings on the home page.

**Do:** In site/src/pages/index.astro:77-88 add to the Organization node: "description": "Free quizzes, Bible games and articles for understanding yourself, and your walk with God, a little better.", "sameAs": ["https://ko-fi.com/sunjolol", "https://github.com/sunjolol/wiser-walk"], and "foundingDate": "2026-09-03" (the first deploy, a real date). Only list profiles he actually controls; never add a profile that does not exist or that he does not own. Also tie the about page to the same entity: in site/src/pages/about.astro add "mainEntity": { "@id": "https://wiserwalk.com/#organization" } to the AboutPage block, so the two pages describe one entity rather than two. Google says to put Organization markup on "your home page or a single about us page", so keep the full node on the home page only and reference it by @id elsewhere. sameAs works far better when reciprocal, which is one of the owner actions below.

**Where:** site/src/pages/index.astro:63-90 (the @graph); site/src/pages/about.astro (AboutPage block)

## 6. [medium impact / small effort] Axis, tradition, figure and method pages are typed Article but carry none of Article's recommended properties
**Evidence:** Live extraction: /tradition/anabaptist-mennonite-peace-church/ publishes exactly { @type Article, headline, description, url } and nothing else. /figure/david/ is the same four keys. /axis/theology-compass/grace/ adds isPartOf (a Quiz) and a citation array of seven works, and /method/ is Article with headline, description and url. They also contradict their own head: every one of these pages sends og:type "website" (confirmed on all four live pages) while its JSON-LD says Article. All four validate cleanly, so this is incompleteness rather than error. The citation array is fine and is not a marking-up-invisible-content problem: I checked all seven works (Bondage of the Will, Chosen by God, Exact Exposition, Declaration of Sentiments, Arminian Theology and the rest) and every one appears in the rendered body of the axis page.

Add a real dateModified only if you have a real date to give: a build timestamp is not an edit date and would be worse than nothing. Set ogType="article" on these four templates so the head and the JSON-LD agree. For figure pages specifically, consider adding "about": { "@type": "Person", "name": "David" } since the page genuinely is about a person, which is honest and helps entity matching. **Where:** site/src/pages/tradition/[slug].astro:46-52; site/src/pages/figure/[slug].astro:204-212; site/src/pages/axis/[quiz]/[axis].astro:120-130; site/src/pages/method.astro

## 7. [medium impact / small effort] numberOfQuestions is not a schema.org property, and the Quiz block marks up questions that are not on the page
**Evidence:** This is the site's only structured-data error. The official Schema Markup Validator returns for https://wiserwalk.com/q/theology-compass/: numErrors 1, errorType INVALID_PREDICATE, args ["numberOfQuestions", "Quiz"], isSevere true. Confirmed independently: https://schema.org/numberOfQuestions returns HTTP 404, and the string does not appear anywhere on https://schema.org/Quiz, where Quiz is defined as "A test of knowledge, skills and abilities" and inherits from LearningResource. Second problem: the markup asserts eighteen questions, and I stripped every script tag from the live page and confirmed none of the eighteen statements is in the rendered HTML (the page shows the axis names and the tradition list, and the statements are revealed one at a time by the quiz app). Google's structured data guidelines say "Don't mark up content that is not visible to readers of the page", and the penalty for breaking that is a manual action which strips rich result eligibility sitewide, which would cost the breadcrumbs the site already earns. The realistic likelihood is small; the downside is not local to this page, which is why it is worth ten minutes.

**Do:** In site/src/pages/q/[quiz].astro:75-86 delete the numberOfQuestions line outright. Keep @type Quiz (it is an accurate description and harmless), keep name, description, url, educationalLevel and about, and add "isPartOf": { "@id": "https://wiserwalk.com/#website" } and "inLanguage": "en-GB". If the question count matters, express it in a property that exists and that the page actually shows: the stat tiles on the live page already display "18 Statements", so "about" or simply the visible tile carries it honestly. Do not try to make this eligible for Google's Education Q&A feature: that documentation is written for flashcard pages, requires hasPart of Question objects each with eduQuestionType "Flashcard", text and acceptedAnswer, requires the questions be "immediately visible to users on the page", and requires "education related questions and answers". A quiz whose answers are a five-point agreement scale with no right answer does not qualify, and forcing it would be exactly the misleading markup the guidelines prohibit.

**Where:** site/src/pages/q/[quiz].astro:83 (numberOfQuestions: quiz.items.length), inside the JSON-LD at :75-86

## 8. [medium impact / small effort] The two game pages publish a two-step breadcrumb that skips /games/, and omit two Open Graph tags
**Evidence:** Breadcrumb is one of the very few rich results this site can actually earn, and it is the one that shows in the result itself. Every other template publishes a full trail (the tradition page publishes Home > Quizzes > Theology Compass > Anabaptist / Mennonite). Both game pages publish only Home > Sounds Like Scripture, confirmed live and in source at site/src/pages/play/sounds-like-scripture.astro:54-56 and site/src/pages/play/who-said-it.astro:54-55, even though /games/ is a real, indexable, sitemapped hub page with its own SEO title "Free Bible games: Sounds Like Scripture and Who Said It?". Because these pages are standalone documents that do not use Base.astro, their hand-written heads have also drifted: they carry og:site_name, og:type, og:title, og:description, og:url, og:image, og:image:width, og:image:height and the four twitter tags, but are missing og:image:alt and og:locale, both of which every Base.astro page sets. Everything else about their heads is correct: canonical, max-image-preview:large, twitter:card summary_large_image, and a reachable 1200x630 card.

**Do:** In both files insert a middle step so the trail reads Home > Games > the game: { '@type': 'ListItem', position: 2, name: 'Games', item: new URL('/games/', Astro.site).href } and move the game itself to position 3. Then add <meta property="og:image:alt" content={socialTitle} /> and <meta property="og:locale" content="en_GB" /> beside the existing og tags at sounds-like-scripture.astro:91-102 and the matching block in who-said-it.astro. Longer term the duplication is the real bug: the two heads are a hand-maintained copy of Base.astro's and will drift again. Extract the head into a shared component that both Base.astro and the two game documents import, so the next tag added lands in three places at once.

**Where:** site/src/pages/play/sounds-like-scripture.astro:54-56 and :91-102; site/src/pages/play/who-said-it.astro:54-55 and its og block

## 9. [medium impact / small effort] Five markup types to rule out now, before someone wastes a week on them or earns a manual action
**Evidence:** I read the current feature list on Google's structured data gallery rather than recalling it, and three features that SEO advice still recommends are simply gone from it: How-to, Sitelinks search box and Practice problems are absent from the feature-guide list, and FAQ is absent too (the word survives only in a stale meta description). The FAQ documentation URL now 301-redirects to /search/updates#removing-faq-rich-result, where Google's own changelog reads: 15 June 2026, "Removed documentation for the FAQ rich result feature" because "The FAQ rich result feature is no longer shown in Google Search results", with the 8 May 2026 deprecation notice "This feature will no longer appear in Google Search starting May 7, 2026". How-to was removed on 14 September 2023 ("this rich result is no longer shown in search results, on both desktop and mobile devices") and the sitelinks search box on 29 November 2024. This matters here because eight of the eleven articles are titled "How to...", which makes HowTo markup the single most tempting wrong move on this site.

**Do:** Do not add, and record why: (1) FAQPage, dead for everyone since 7 May 2026. (2) HowTo, dead since September 2023, despite the article titles. (3) A WebSite potentialAction SearchAction for the sitelinks search box, retired November 2024. (4) SoftwareApplication or VideoGame with aggregateRating or offers on the game pages: Google's software app result needs a rating, the site has no real ratings, and inventing one is precisely the "unsubstantiated ratings" the spam policy names, with a sitewide manual action as the cost. (5) Speakable, which Google restricts to news publishers and to "users in the U.S. that have Google Home devices set to English", and which is still in beta. I also looked at and rejected three more: ItemList carousel (Google supports it for Course, Movie, Recipe and Restaurant only, so the /articles/ and /quizzes/ CollectionPage hasPart lists cannot earn a carousel and should stay as harmless machine-readable description), structured data carousels beta (EEA, Türkiye and South Africa only, LocalBusiness/Product/Event only, and requires applying through a form), and ProfilePage (Google frames it for forums and creator profiles; mainEntity may be an Organization, but there is no realistic result for a quiz site's about page).

**Where:** site/src/pages/articles/[slug].astro (do not add HowTo); site/src/pages/index.astro (do not add SearchAction); site/src/pages/play/*.astro (do not add aggregateRating)

## 10. [low impact / small effort] The game pages describe themselves to machines as nothing but a breadcrumb
**Evidence:** /play/sounds-like-scripture/ and /play/who-said-it/ carry exactly one JSON-LD block each, a BreadcrumbList. There is no type at all saying what the page is, while every other template at least declares itself. The two games are the site's most distinctive assets and the ones most likely to be described secondhand, and schema.org Game and VideoGame are real types that fit them honestly (each is a browser game with a name, a description, a genre and a publisher). Be clear about the ceiling: Google has no rich result for a web game, so this earns nothing in the SERP. Its only value is that Bing, Copilot and answer engines read schema.org to work out what a page is, and Bing's guidance leans on Organization and entity markup for exactly that.

**Do:** Add a modest, true block to each game page beside the existing breadcrumb: { "@context": "https://schema.org", "@type": "VideoGame", "name": "Sounds Like Scripture", "description": <the page's existing meta description>, "url": <canonical>, "gamePlatform": "Web browser", "applicationCategory": "Game", "genre": "Trivia", "inLanguage": "en-GB", "isAccessibleForFree": true, "publisher": { "@id": "https://wiserwalk.com/#organization" }, "image": <the og card URL> }. Every one of those values is already true and already on the page. Do NOT add aggregateRating, offers, downloadUrl or interactionStatistic: there is no real data behind any of them. Rank this last. It is the cheapest item here and also the one least likely to change anything.

**Where:** site/src/pages/play/sounds-like-scripture.astro:50-60 (beside breadcrumbLd); site/src/pages/play/who-said-it.astro:50-58

- Canonicals: every page carries a self-referencing canonical on the apex with a trailing slash, matching the site value in astro.config.mjs, so canonical, og:url and the sitemap entries all agree with no redirect hop.
- Robots directives: max-image-preview:large on indexable pages (the prerequisite for a large image in results and in Discover), and noindex, follow on /me/, the draft seven-deadly-sins quiz, /r/ and /c/. robots.txt deliberately does not Disallow those paths, with a comment explaining that a blocked crawler never reads the noindex and never follows the links out to the axis and tradition pages. That reasoning is correct.
- Article dates: the visible line on /articles/how-to-become-more-grateful/ reads "Published 3 September 2026" and the markup says datePublished "2026-09-03". Google's byline guidance asks for a user-visible, prominently placed, clearly labelled date that matches the structured value, and this does. The only gap is that it is plain text rather than a <time> element.
- Axis page citations: the citation array on /axis/theology-compass/grace/ lists seven works, and I confirmed all seven appear in the rendered body of the page, so it is not marking up content readers cannot see. citation accepts Text as well as CreativeWork on schema.org, so plain strings are valid.
- Titles and descriptions: every page I sampled has a unique <title> and a unique meta description, and the social title correctly reuses the search title from site/src/lib/seo.ts rather than the shorter on-page name.
- The draft seven-deadly-sins quiz correctly publishes no JSON-LD and no breadcrumb at all when noindex, so it cannot leak into the index through markup (site/src/pages/q/[quiz].astro:75, jsonLd is null when isDraft).
- og:locale is set to en_GB on every Base.astro page, matching the site's British English copy.
