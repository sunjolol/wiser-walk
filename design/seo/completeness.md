# completeness

The seven auditors between them covered the plumbing, the copy, the markup and the second index thoroughly, and their technical verdict is right: this site is unusually clean for three weeks old. What none of them looked at is how a faceless site with no backlinks actually becomes discoverable, which is not by being well-formed but by owning something other people want to point at. On that axis the site is empty. It publishes no downloadable data, states no permission to reuse anything (the only rights line on 97 pages is "© 2026 Wiser Walk"), has no print path at all (zero @media print rules outside node_modules), answers no question in a sentence anywhere, and has not one page aimed at a date in the church calendar, with Reformation Day forty days out and the Compass already carrying Luther, Marburg and the Lutheran, Reformed and Catholic traditions. The strongest unclaimed surface is Google Dataset Search: the 18-tradition by 6-axis position table is a dataset by Google's own definition, and a search there for Christian denomination beliefs returns a hundred demographic tables from Statista and Kaggle and nothing describing doctrine. Two architectural assumptions also need correcting rather than praising. The noindex on result pages is the right call, but robots.txt states in writing that result-page links are "how authority reaches" the axis and tradition pages, and Google has said a long-term noindex eventually stops following links, so that channel should not be counted on. And the two things people actually share, the result card and the game's challenge link, both waste the share: the card prints a noindex result URL in its footer and the challenge link is a fragment, so its preview can never say what the challenge was. Finally, nobody told the owner how to know whether any of this is working: Vercel's analytics report pages and referrers but no queries at all, and Bing's free Keyword Research gives the real search volumes every auditor said they could not obtain. The single most important thing is to give the site something citable and say it may be reused, because on a domain with no links the on-page work the other six auditors specified cannot pay for itself until somebody points at it.

## 4. [high impact / small effort] robots.txt states in writing that result-page links are how authority reaches the reference pages, and Google has said long-term noindex stops following links
**Evidence:** https://wiserwalk.com/robots.txt carries a five-line comment explaining the deliberate decision not to Disallow /r/, ending: result-page links "are how authority reaches" the axis and tradition pages. I verified the mechanics hold today: https://wiserwalk.com/r/theology-compass/01ZO4A/ returns robots "noindex, follow" with a self-referencing canonical, and it does carry real links out, 18 internal targets including all six /axis/theology-compass/ pages, five /tradition/ pages and two articles. So the implementation is correct and the reasoning about robots.txt blocking is right. What is not right is depending on it. Google's John Mueller has said that a long-term noindex will effectively become noindex, nofollow: once Google sees the noindex for longer than expected and decides the page does not want to be in search, it drops the page and stops following its links, with the timing depending on crawl frequency. Result pages are permanently noindex by design, which is precisely the case he described. The same applies to /c/ comparison pages, which I confirmed also carry "noindex, follow". So the site has written a link channel into its own robots.txt that will quietly close.

**Do:** Change nothing about the noindex, which is the right call (millions of permutation URLs in the index would be the scaled-content pattern Google's spam policy names, and the pages are per-person). Change the reliance. (1) Rewrite the robots.txt comment so it states the real reason, which is that a crawler blocked in robots.txt never reads the noindex, and stop claiming the links carry authority. (2) Make sure every tradition and axis page is reachable and linked from indexable pages, so nothing depends on the result page: /q/theology-compass/ already links all 18 traditions and all 6 axes, so the Compass is safe, but check the same holds for the gifts and figure families as those pages get built out. (3) Since a result page's links will stop counting, stop treating /r/ as part of the internal link graph in any future planning. This costs one comment rewrite today and prevents a wrong assumption being built on later.

**Where:** https://wiserwalk.com/robots.txt (the comment above the Sitemap line; source in site/public/robots.txt); verified live on https://wiserwalk.com/r/theology-compass/01ZO4A/ and https://wiserwalk.com/c/theology-compass/01ZO4A.01ZO4A/

## 5. [high impact / medium effort / VISIBLE] No page on the site answers a question in a sentence, which is what AI answer engines extract and quote
**Evidence:** I pulled every heading from https://wiserwalk.com/axis/theology-compass/table/, the site's best page on a question people genuinely type. ## 6. [medium impact / small effort] Nothing in place tells him which queries he is starting to appear for, and the one free source of real search volumes is unused
**Evidence:** Two gaps. First, what the site measures: site/astro.config.mjs enables Vercel Web Analytics, and Vercel's own docs say it reports "top visited pages, referrers for a specific page, and demographics like location, operating systems, and browser information", with panels, a bounce rate, and a CSV export of up to 250 entries. Search queries are not in that list and are not available at all, because the analytics run in the browser and Google strips the query from the referrer. So the cookieless analytics are good for what they do and cannot answer "what am I ranking for". Google Search Console is therefore the only query source in place, and it is the owner's to read. Second, and this is what every one of the seven auditors said they could not get: real search volumes. Bing Webmaster Tools includes a free Keyword Research tool, and Bing's own help page says it gives search volume data sourced directly from Microsoft Advertising, filterable by country, language and device over any timeframe in the last six months, with related and question keywords. Every demand judgement in all seven reports is qualitative guesswork from autocomplete because nobody had a number. This tool is free, and it is already on the other side of the Bing import five auditors independently recommended.

**Do:** Two things. (1) Write down a five-minute monthly check so the numbers get read rather than accumulated. In Google Search Console: Performance, last 28 days, look at Queries sorted by impressions and note anything new near the top, then Pages to see which ones are getting impressions at all; then Indexing, Pages, and read the "Crawled - currently not indexed" and "Discovered - currently not indexed" counts, because on a domain this young those two numbers are the whole story. In Vercel: Web Analytics, Referrers panel, which is the only place a real inbound link will show up. That is the whole check. (2) Once Bing Webmaster Tools is imported, spend ten minutes in its Keyword Research tool on the phrases the site is aimed at ("what denomination am I", "spiritual gifts test", "am i calvinist or arminian", "is it in the bible game") and write the numbers into the repo, so the next round of work is ranked by demand rather than by guessing. Bing volumes are smaller than Google's but the relative order between phrases is the part that matters and it is free.

**Where:** site/astro.config.mjs (webAnalytics enabled, with the beforeSend hook in site/src/layouts/Base.astro:248-277 correctly stripping result and account codes); no query-level measurement exists anywhere in the repo or the owner's tooling beyond Search Console

## 9. [medium impact / medium effort / VISIBLE] Eleven articles is a body of work, not an engine, and there is no stated cadence for a site that will never be marketed
**Evidence:** The site holds 11 articles, all published within an 18-day window (site/src/content/articles/, with seven sharing published: 2026-09-20), and nothing in the repo or the notes says what comes next or how often. That matters specifically because of the owner's constraint: with no marketing, no social following and no ads, the only thing that compounds is a growing surface of pages that answer real questions, and Google's picture of a new domain is built from repeated crawls finding new work. The relevant fact about timing is that domain age itself does almost nothing (John Mueller has said plainly it helps nothing); what the lag actually is, on the accounts I read, is an absence of signals, with low-competition niches typically showing movement in two to three months and moderate ones in three to six. A site that stops publishing at week three gives the crawler no reason to come back during exactly the window when it is forming that picture. Against that, this site's cheapest new pages are not articles at all: 2,168 verified lines sit in demos/sounds-like-scripture/pool.json and demos/who-said-it/pool.json, 25 figure pages and 18 tradition pages already exist as data, and the audited compass data supports whole page families that require no new writing.

**Do:** Set a cadence he can keep without it feeling like marketing, and make most of it come from data rather than from writing. A realistic minimum engine: one new article a fortnight, which is 26 a year and doubles the article count by March, plus one data-driven page family per month built from material already in the repo (the six which-denominations pages, the /figures/ index, the reveal pages for the non-biblical lines in the game pool). Put the next six article titles in a file now, chosen from what the existing articles link to and what the quizzes need as companions, so the decision is made once rather than fortnightly. Publish on a fixed day so the crawl learns the rhythm, and let the sitemap lastmod (which the crawl auditors have already specified) carry a truthful date so the rhythm is visible. Do not chase volume: eleven substantive articles at 8.8% mutual overlap is a better base than forty thin ones, and the thing to protect is that every new page answers a question somebody actually asks.

## 11. [low impact / medium effort] Embeddable quiz widget or result badge: tested, and the answer is not yet, with a specific trigger for when it changes
**Evidence:** The brief asked me to test this rather than assume it, so I did. There is no embed anywhere: grepping site/src for iframe or embed returns nothing, and there is no /embed/ route. The case for one is real in principle, because an embed is the classic way a tool earns links without its owner asking. The case against it here is arithmetic. An embed earns links only when somebody wants to put the thing on their page, and nobody currently knows the site exists: the brand search returns the GitHub repo and competitors, not wiserwalk.com. An embed also costs real work done properly (a framed route, postMessage height negotiation, a separate lightweight stylesheet, a copy-the-code panel, and the same phone-first standard as everything else) and it is not free of risk: an embedded quiz shows the result inside someone else's page, which is exactly where the fairness caveats and both-pole labels are most likely to be cropped out, and CLAUDE.md is explicit that a discernment-minded audience will screenshot anything unfair. The result share card already does the cheap version of the job, and the finding above fixes the one thing wrong with it.

**Do:** Do not build it now. Build it when one condition is met: Vercel's Referrers panel shows a real inbound link from a site that is not GitHub or Ko-fi, meaning somebody has chosen to point at this of their own accord. At that point an embed has a first customer and is worth the week. If it is ever built, the non-negotiables are that both pole names render inside the frame at every width, the honesty caveat renders with the result rather than behind a link, and the embed code contains a visible credit link to wiserwalk.com outside the iframe, because a link inside an iframe is a link to the iframe's own URL and passes nothing to the parent. Until then, the cheaper substitutes are the share card fix above and the downloadable dataset, both of which earn a citation without asking anyone to paste code.

**Where:** No embed route exists anywhere in site/src/pages/; the nearest equivalent is site/src/components/ShareBlock.astro

# FINE
- Result pages being noindex is the right call, not a mistake. Verified live: /r/theology-compass/01ZO4A/ returns "noindex, follow" with a correct self-referencing canonical, and /c/ comparison pages the same. Letting result permutations into the index would produce an effectively unbounded set of near-identical pages, which is the scaled-content pattern Google's spam policy describes. The related question of whether they pass links today is answered yes: that page carries 18 internal links including all six theology-compass axis pages, five tradition pages and two articles. The only problem is relying on that forever, which is a separate finding above.
- The decision not to Disallow /r/ in robots.txt is correct and should not be reversed. A crawler blocked in robots.txt never fetches the page, so it never reads the noindex, and the URL can still be indexed from external links with no content. Keeping it crawlable and noindexed is the textbook handling.
- Single-language handling needs no work. The site is <html lang="en"> with no hreflang anywhere, and that is correct: hreflang exists to point between alternate language or regional versions, and there is only one version. Do not translate and do not add hreflang. The one real language issue, the mix of British spellings (I found honour and recognise in the live article copy) against American denominational vocabulary (denomination, Southern Baptist, COGIC), is cosmetic and was already flagged by another auditor; leave it alone.
- A Wikidata item is not available and should not be attempted. Wikidata accepts an item on one of three grounds: a sitelink to a Wikipedia or sister project, being a clearly identifiable entity describable with "serious and publicly available references", or filling a structural need. A three-week-old site with no independent coverage meets none of them, the item would be nominated for deletion, and creating one about your own project is the kind of self-referential entry that policy's reference requirement exists to exclude. Revisit only if something independent writes about the site.
- Nothing can be built to win featured snippets. Google's featured snippets documentation answers the question of whether you can mark up for one with "You can't. Google systems determine whether a page would make a good featured snippet", and offers no formatting guidance at all, only max-snippet as a way to opt out. The widely repeated 40-to-50-word rule is folklore, not documentation. Write short direct answers for the reason in the finding above, which is answer engines and readers, and do not expect a snippet from it.
- Video is genuinely not available and should not be planned for. YouTube and TikTok search both need a stream of uploads and a creator identity, which is ongoing work the owner has ruled out and cannot do without a following. There is no passive video route: an auto-generated clip of a game run would be marketing work by another name. This one is closed and can stay closed.
- The game's challenge links create no duplicate-URL problem. The payload rides in the fragment (site/src/games/sounds-like-scripture.html:1574 reads location.hash), which never reaches the server and is never part of a URL any crawler indexes. The canonical is clean and the analytics hook strips the fragment before the view is counted. The only issue is the preview, covered above.
- The cookieless analytics are correctly implemented and privacy-preserving. site/src/layouts/Base.astro:248-277 strips the result code from /r/ paths, both codes from /c/ paths, and the query and hash from /account/ paths before any view is sent, so no result code and no pair of codes is ever recorded. Vercel identifies visitors by a hash valid for one day that cannot follow anyone between sites or between days. Nothing here needs changing.
- The share card itself is well built and does not need redesigning. It is drawn on canvas at 1080x1350 from the same numbers and geometry as the page, always in the light palette so it looks the same on a stranger's timeline, and it carries the outcome's note with the outcome so a name never travels without its sentence. Only the footer URL is wrong.
