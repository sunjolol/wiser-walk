# content-and-linking

The strong half of this site is genuinely strong: the six Theology Compass axis pages (1,146 to 1,963 words each) and the 25 figure pages (776 to 1,312 words) carry real cited content that nothing else on the open web is doing this carefully, the 11 articles are substantive and distinct from one another (only 25.8% six-word-shingle overlap between siblings, which is just site chrome), the head is clean on every template (canonical, robots, og:image with width, height and alt, breadcrumbs), and seo.ts has already done the hard work of rewriting titles into searchers' words. The weak half is where the search demand actually is. The 18 tradition pages are 68.1% identical to each other word for word and contain not one sentence describing what the tradition believes, so a page titled "Eastern Orthodox beliefs on six questions that divide Christians" delivers six labels and a chart. The two game pages, which have the clearest natural demand on the site, are the thinnest indexable pages on it at 177 and 180 words with three internal links each. Nine of the 19 spiritual-gift pages sit under 240 words, and they are exactly the gifts people search for (teaching, mercy, giving, serving) while the long treatment went to the contested ones. Internal linking runs one way only: every article links out to quizzes and games, and not one quiz, axis, tradition or figure page links back to an article, while the 11 articles contain zero inline links inside their prose. The 25 figure pages are reachable only through six thin axis pages because /q/bible-figure/ lists no figures at all, where /q/theology-compass/ lists all 18 traditions. But the single most important thing is none of that: it is that nobody is behind this site. There is no name, no byline, no contact address, and no author in any Article JSON-LD anywhere on 99 indexable pages. A site whose entire proposition is "we describe your opponents fairly" is currently asking a brand-new domain with no backlinks to be trusted by an anonymous stranger, and Google's own guidance puts trust above every other signal.

## 1. [high impact / small effort / VISIBLE] Nobody is behind the site: no name, no contact, no author in any markup
**Evidence:** Fetched /about/ (404 words, nav and footer stripped): it describes the site, never a person, and gives no contact route. Grepped the whole source tree for mailto/contact/author/byline and found no match outside unrelated account and email-provider code. The only "who" on the entire site is one sentence on /support/: "Wiser Walk is made by one person, not a company. There is no team and no investor behind it." Article JSON-LD on /articles/how-to-become-more-patient/ is {"@type":"Article","headline":...,"datePublished":"2026-09-20","publisher":{"@type":"Organization","name":"Wiser Walk"}} with no author property at all. Google's Article structured data page states author is a recommended property and that "To help Google better understand who the author is, we strongly recommend using the type and url (or sameAs) properties." Google's helpful-content page frames this as the "Who" question and states plainly that of Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trustworthiness, "trust is most important". The only contact channel that exists anywhere is a GitHub issue link on tradition, figure and axis pages.

**Do:** Three changes, all small. (1) Add an author to Article JSON-LD in site/src/pages/articles/[slug].astro:71-79: author: { '@type': 'Person', name: '<his name>', url: 'https://wiserwalk.com/about/' }. (2) Add a real contact address to /about/. The domain's mail already works (MX at SiteGround, SPF present), so hello@wiserwalk.com costs nothing. Suggested copy for /about/, no em dashes, no jargon: "Wiser Walk is written and built by one person. If something here is described unfairly, or you have spotted a mistake, write to hello@wiserwalk.com and it will be read." (3) Add sameAs to the Organization node in site/src/pages/index.astro:77 pointing at the two real profiles that already exist and are his: ["https://ko-fi.com/sunjolol", "https://github.com/sunjolol/wiser-walk"]. HOUSE-RULE TENSION, flagging it rather than deciding it: his stated goal is faceless distribution. A full name and a photo are not required. A first name plus a working address at the domain satisfies the "Who" question without building a personal brand, and it is real data rather than an invented editorial persona, which would be a fabrication and is not an option.

**Where:** site/src/pages/about.astro; site/src/pages/articles/[slug].astro:71-79; site/src/pages/index.astro:77; site-wide

## 2. [high impact / large effort / VISIBLE] The 18 tradition pages are 68% identical to each other and say nothing about the tradition

HOUSE-RULE FLAG, important: CLAUDE.md states the tradition entries hold only a name and a position vector, that tradition pages get no sources block until real citations exist, and that inventing confession references is forbidden. So this is not a writing task that can be done from the existing data alone. If that work is not going to happen soon, the honest alternative is to noindex the tradition pages (keep them crawlable and followed, exactly as /r/ already is) so that 18 near-duplicate pages are not what a new domain is judged on, and let the six axis pages carry the denominational queries, which they already do well.

## 6. [high impact / small effort / VISIBLE] The 11 articles contain no inline links at all inside their prose
**Evidence:** Isolated the <article> element on /articles/how-to-forgive-someone-who-hurt-you/ and counted anchors inside it: 0 links in 4,872 characters of prose. The same holds across the family. **Do:** Add two to four inline links to the body of each article, in the sentences where the subject genuinely comes up. Rule to hold: the anchor text is the words already in the sentence, and a link only goes in where a reader would actually want to follow it. No link blocks dressed up as prose, which reads as written for search engines.

## 13. [medium impact / small effort] Not one URL in the sitemap carries a lastmod
