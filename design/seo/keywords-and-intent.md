# keywords-and-intent

The technical floor is genuinely good: robots.txt is correct (including the deliberate decision not to Disallow /r/), all 97 sitemap URLs return 200 with matching self-referencing canonicals, www and http both 308 to the apex, noindex is applied exactly where intended, BreadcrumbList is everywhere, and a full crawl of the site found zero orphan pages. The weakness is not technical, it is aim. The site's best content, the six axis pages with 1,300 to 2,100 audited words each, sits under one-word H1s like "Table", "Grace" and "Kingdom" that contradict the good title tags above them, and Google's own title-link documentation names the H1 as a source it uses and warns that vague headings get rewritten. The eleven articles carry no entry in seo.ts at all, so they are aimed at secular head terms ("how to become more grateful") where Google's autocomplete shows the bare phrase pulls Reddit and Islam results, while the Christian tail people actually type ("...according to the bible", "...bible verse") appears nowhere in their titles. The 18 tradition pages are 378 words each with 36% eight-gram overlap between any two, built from a six-number vector, while real confessional citations for all 18 already sit unused in site/src/data/compass-audit.json. Against that, three new page families have autocomplete-confirmed demand and visibly thin competition: "what denominations believe in X" (predestination, tongues, transubstantiation, dispensationalism), "am I Calvinist or Arminian" (ranked today by ProProfs, GoToQuiz and forum threads), and a printable spiritual gifts test (seven of the top ten "spiritual gifts test" suggestions are printable or PDF variants). The single most important thing, though, is that none of this ranks without links: searching the brand returns only the GitHub repo, and a three-week-old domain with zero inbound links cannot win anything on on-page work alone. The one-time owner actions below are therefore ranked with link acquisition first, because it is the one input nobody else can supply.
