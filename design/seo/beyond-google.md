# beyond-google

The technical foundation is genuinely strong: clean canonicals, a 97-URL sitemap with drafts and result pages correctly excluded, `max-image-preview:large` everywhere, BreadcrumbList on every page, good real OG cards that all six social crawlers fetch with a 200, favicons at every size Google honours, and a robots.txt that lets every AI crawler in, which is exactly right for being cited. The weakness is that none of that is reaching anything except Google. I verified today that DuckDuckGo returns "No results found for site:wiserwalk.com" and Brave returns "Too few matches were found", with a control site returning normal results in both, so three weeks after launch the site is in zero non-Google indexes and Bing Webmaster Tools has never been set up. That one gap costs Bing, DuckDuckGo, Yahoo, Ecosia, AOL and ChatGPT's search, all at once, and it is a five-minute fix that needs no new verification because Bing imports the already-verified Google Search Console property. Below that, six axis pages ship meta descriptions cut off mid-word, which is the text Bing, Brave, Slack, Discord, WhatsApp and every AI answer engine actually read. There is no RSS feed at all, so Feedly, Inoreader and every aggregator have nothing to subscribe to. The article pages lack `author`, `dateModified` and `article:published_time`, which is what Pinterest Rich Pins reads for a byline and what AI engines look for to decide a page is authored and dated, and three of eleven article images are under Google Discover's 1200px minimum. The single most important thing is the Bing Webmaster Tools import: everything else in this report compounds on top of an index the site is currently absent from.

## 1. [high impact / small effort] The site is in no non-Google index at all: Bing Webmaster Tools has never been set up
**Evidence:** Checked live in a real browser today (2026-09-21). DuckDuckGo for `site:wiserwalk.com` returns the literal text "No results found for site:wiserwalk.com". Brave Search for the same query returns "Too few matches were found". Both were controlled against `site:clearerthinking.org`, which returned normal result listings on both engines, so the `site:` operator works and the zero is real. DuckDuckGo's web results are served from the Bing index, so this means Bing has no pages from wiserwalk.com. Brave runs its own independent index and also has nothing. Separately confirmed that nothing on the site is blocking them: bingbot, Googlebot, GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Applebot, YandexBot, CCBot and Bytespider all get HTTP 200 on both the home page and the sitemap. The cause is simply that the site was never submitted anywhere but Google.

**Do:** Owner action, listed in full below: import wiserwalk.com into Bing Webmaster Tools from the already-verified Google Search Console property. This needs no DNS change, no new meta tag and no new file, because Bing accepts the Google verification. It unlocks Bing, DuckDuckGo, Yahoo, Ecosia, AOL and Bing-backed AI answers in one step, and Bing Webmaster Tools also gives a free backlink report, a site scan and IndexNow submission. Then submit https://wiserwalk.com/sitemap-index.xml inside Bing Webmaster Tools. Separately, submit the home page once at https://search.brave.com/submit-url, which is free and needs no account, because Brave crawls its own index and is not reached by the Bing import.

**Where:** Nothing in the repository. This is an account setup the owner must do. The sitemap it needs is already correct at https://wiserwalk.com/sitemap-index.xml

## 5. [medium impact / small effort] Article pages carry no author, no modified date and no article image in their structured data, which is what Pinterest Rich Pins and AI answer engines read
**Evidence:** Fetched https://wiserwalk.com/articles/how-to-become-more-grateful/ live. Its Article JSON-LD is exactly: `{"@context":"https://schema.org","@type":"Article","headline":"...","description":"...","datePublished":"2026-09-03","url":"...","publisher":{"@type":"Organization","name":"Wiser Walk"}}`. There is no `author`, no `dateModified`, no `image` and no `mainEntityOfPage`, and the `publisher` does not point at the `#organization` node the home page defines, so the two are unlinked entities. The page also has `og:type` set to `article` (correct) but no `article:author` and no `article:published_time`. Pinterest's own Rich Pins documentation lists og:title, og:description and og:type=article as the Article requirements, and current guidance is that `article:author` and `article:published_time` are what make the pin show a byline and a date. Every article image on the page is also absent from the structured data even though the paintings are the site's best visual asset.

**Do:** In the article page template, extend the Article JSON-LD with `"author": {"@type": "Person", "name": "<his name or the site name>"}`, `"dateModified"` from the front matter, `"image": ["https://wiserwalk.com/img/articles/<slug>.jpg"]`, `"mainEntityOfPage": {"@type": "WebPage", "@id": "<canonical>"}`, and change `publisher` to `{"@id": "https://wiserwalk.com/#organization"}` so it resolves to the node the home page already publishes. Add two OG tags on article pages only: `<meta property="article:published_time" content="<ISO date>" />` and `<meta property="article:author" content="<name>" />`. A named author is also the single cheapest signal that a page was written by someone, which is what AI answer engines weigh when deciding whether to cite a three-week-old domain.

**Where:** site/src/pages/articles/[slug].astro (the jsonLd object passed to Base); site/src/layouts/Base.astro around lines 230 to 243 for the two article-only OG tags

# FINE
- robots.txt is correct for AI citation and needs no change. It is `User-agent: *` / `Allow: /` plus the sitemap line, which permits GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, CCBot, Applebot, Bytespider and every other AI crawler. I tested ten bot user agents live and all ten got HTTP 200 on both the home page and the sitemap. Do not add AI crawler blocks: OpenAI's own documentation is explicit that OAI-SearchBot is what surfaces a site in ChatGPT search and that sites opting out will not appear in ChatGPT answers. Allowing them is the correct trade for a site with no other distribution.
- Do NOT add llms.txt. Google's AI features documentation states plainly "You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup to appear in these features" and adds "There's also no special schema.org structured data that you need to add". No major AI company has committed to reading llms.txt in production. Adding one would be a file nobody reads.
- The sitemap is genuinely clean: 97 URLs, no noindex page among them, result pages and comparison pages and /me/ and all /account/ pages excluded, and the seven-deadly-sins draft excluded across all nine of its URLs by the registry-derived filter in astro.config.mjs. This is better than most sites manage.
- Open Graph is correct and the cards are good. All six social crawlers I tested (facebookexternalhit, Twitterbot, Slackbot, Discordbot, WhatsApp, LinkedInBot) fetch both the page and the OG image with HTTP 200. I looked at two cards directly and they are strong: readable at thumbnail size, correct 1200x630, brand name and domain present, no cropping problems.

BetaList is for pre-launch startups and the site is already live. AlternativeTo's free queue runs months and a Christian quiz site is not a software alternative to anything, so it would sit unlisted.
- Curlie is dead enough to skip. Its category pages return HTTP 500 today (I tested both /Christianity and /Christianity/Denominations). It is not worth a submission to a directory whose own pages do not load.
- The old-style Christian link directories are not worth it and may be harmful. I checked christian-directory.net, whose submit page is blocked by its own mod_security and returns Not Acceptable, and christian-links-directory.com, which is a 2000s-era reciprocal link farm. These carry no traffic and links from them look like exactly the pattern search engines discount.
- Wikipedia and Wikidata are out, and should not be attempted. Wikipedia forbids adding links to sites you own or are connected with, and a three-week-old site with no independent coverage fails notability for either project. An attempt would be reverted and could attach a spam flag to the domain. The honest route is that if the site is ever written about by an independent publication, someone else may add it.
