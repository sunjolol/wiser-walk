# Personality Quiz (quiz #1): the reading of Book III

Source: Gregory the Great, *The Book of Pastoral Rule* (c. 590), Book III, James Barmby's translation (NPNF 2nd
series vol. 12, 1895, public domain), New Advent 36013.htm (Book II is 36012.htm). Read whole by the main session on
2026-09-23, all 40 chapters. The plain text is not committed; fetch it again from New Advent if needed.

The owner's brief: call it simply "Personality Quiz"; explain the source only on its how-it-works page; the goal is
accuracy that wows, an "aha" that tells people something true they had not consciously noticed.

## What the book gives

- Chapter 1 lists 36 pairs of opposite hearers ("differently to be admonished are..."). Chapters 2-35 take them one
  pair at a time. Gregory corrects BOTH sides of every pair; no pole is the good one.
- For each side he gives three things: the visible trait; the thing next to it the person does not see; how that
  person is best spoken to. Chapter 3 states the principle: consider "not only what he suffers from his peculiar
  temperament, but also what worse thing presses on him in connection with it".
- He links his own pairs (so combination readings are his, not ours):
  sad by temperament -> anger; joyful -> lust (ch 3). Meek -> sloth, "kindred" (ch 16). Abstinent -> impatience and
  pride, judging those who eat (ch 19). Gluttonous -> much talk and levity (ch 19). Silent -> judging in private,
  pride (ch 14). Humble -> fear passing as humility; haughty -> pride passing as frankness (ch 17). Impatient ->
  showing off, because they cannot bear being looked down on (ch 9). Obstinate <- pride; fickle <- undervaluing
  oneself (ch 18). Over-giving -> want -> grumbling -> grasping (ch 20-21). At variance <- pride in a gift that sets
  one apart, such as knowledge or self-denial (ch 22). Much talk -> gossip -> detraction -> quarrels (ch 14).
- Ch 36: preaching to a crowd is a two-edged sword; his shortlist there is proud/timid, listless/unquiet,
  wrathful/soft-hearted, niggardly/prodigal (a ready backbone for a Quick version if one is ever wanted).
- Ch 37: one person can carry contrary traits (a joyful constitution with sudden sadness; a habit of haste with
  sudden fear, and the reverse). Ch 38: when there are several, treat the one "through which there is the more
  rapid tendency to death" first. So the result gives ONE thing to start with.
- Book II ch 9: vices pass themselves off as virtues (niggardliness as frugality, prodigality as liberality, laxity as
  loving-kindness, wrath as zeal, haste as promptness, tardiness as deliberation).

## The pairs, by chapter

Kept (temperament and character):
- 3 joyful / sad (short, but the thesis sentence is here)
- 7 impudent / bashful: hard rebuke, "by many" / a gentle side-stroke is enough
- 8 forward / faint-hearted: count all they do eminent / think it despised; praise first, forbid the future rather
  than blame the past
- 9 impatient / patient: do what they grieve for afterwards, wreck in a moment what they built / calm at the time,
  then recall it, exaggerate it, are "sorry that he did not return insults"; "guard their heart after victory"
- 10 kindly / envious: "eager backers, but inert spectators" / pine at others' rejoicing; what you love in others
  becomes yours
- 11 simple / insincere: must learn to be silent about some truths / the labour of duplicity, the hedgehog of excuses
- 14 silent / talkative: "more grievous loquacity in the heart", judging in private, "closed sores torment the more",
  withholding needed correction; "a door, not a wall" / scattered, cannot return to self-knowledge, idle words ->
  detraction -> strife, "a city without walls"
- 15 slothful / hasty: finds something to fear so as to stay still ("would not plough by reason of the cold"), the
  idle man is "wholly in desires" / sees only afterwards that it should not have been done
- 16 meek / passionate: laxity beside the virtue / anger believed to be zeal; the passionate bring on strife, the
  impatient cannot bear it (his own distinction); dove and fire; Titus and Timothy
- 17 humble / haughty: see the fear/frankness point above; the haughty are moved by praise first and by being asked
  for help (Moses and Hobab)
- 18 obstinate / fickle
- 19 gluttonous / abstinent
- 20-21 givers / takers; keepers who "commit no wrong" / the lavish who also take ("a bag with holes")
- 22 at variance / at peace: "Break outward peace ... but guard in your heart's core internal peace"
- 23 sowers of strife / peacemakers (the peacemaker must know between whom to make peace)
- 29-30 those who lament but repeat / stop but never lament (generalises to "after a mistake": dwells / moves on;
  the self-soothing excuses in ch 29: others did worse, it was nothing, God is merciful, there is time later; ch 30:
  a writer who stops writing has not rubbed out what he wrote)
- 33 small frequent faults / careful in small things but sometimes a grave fall (gnat and camel)
- 34 never begin / never finish (a ship going upstream never stands still)
- 35 bad in secret, good in public / good in secret, careless of bad repute (modern form: better in public / better
  in private)
- 6 wise / dull (argued into things / moved by example): used for "what helps you", not as a pair

Left out (station or particular sins): 1 men/women, young/old; 2 poor/rich; 4 subjects/prelates; 5 servants/masters;
12 whole/sick; 13 those who fear scourges; 24 rude in sacred learning; 25 preaching; 26 prosperity/adversity
(circumstance); 27 married/single; 28 experience of the flesh; 31 praising wrong / condemning but doing it; 32
deliberate sin.

## The design proposed to the owner (2026-09-23, first look)

- One quiz on Gregory alone, about 36 two-sided questions over 18 pairs, about six minutes. No Quick version for now.
- Every question: two real ways of being, a five-point slide between them, neither side the good one.
- Three kinds of question per pair: what people see; what happens inside or afterwards (Gregory's hidden side); what
  helps you (his mode of admonition).
- The result: a plain headline from the strongest leanings; "What you might not have noticed" joins the person's own
  answers ("You said... You also said... Those are the same thing") using only Gregory's links; "What helps you";
  "Start here" (one thing, ch 38) with one short line of his; "Worth watching" (his cross-links); every pair on rails
  with both poles named.
## Round 2 (2026-09-23, late): the full report

His verdict on the first look: good, but the result is too thin next to Clearer Thinking's Ultimate Personality Test.
He wants a Christian report as rich as that one, from saints and Fathers, Orthodox sources preferred (memory:
personality-quiz-ultimate). An 8-agent research run checked every candidate layer against primary texts:
`research.json` (reports: passions, powers, motives, martha-mary, pastoral-typology, figures-bible, figures-saints,
benchmarks; every quote machine-checked; saved texts were in the session scratchpad and are not committed).

Findings that shape the design:
- Gregory sorts "conspersio" (the make-up of souls) twice in the Moralia: four dispositions, cheerful / morose /
  timid / proud, each with the trap fitted to it (XXIX.45: "the ancient enemy first beholds the character ... and
  then applies the snares"), and tranquil / restless minds (VI.57). 4 x 2 = the eight types. Only these are honest
  "types"; no Father gives 16 clusters.
- Nazianzen, Oration 2.28-32 (Gregory names him as his source) = the "how to help me" layer: doctrine or example,
  spur or curb, praise or blame, public or private, watch closely or "seeing not to see".
- Cassian (Conference 5.13-14, 5.27): all eight thoughts attack everyone but each has a "besetting sin" = the private
  "where the fight is for you now" page (a current state, not a type). Gregory's speeches of the vices (Moralia
  XXXI.90) = "how it talks to you". Overlaps the live seven deadly sins quiz: make that quiz point here.
- Small grids in Gregory: four speeds of anger (V.80), four sorts of speakers (VIII.58). Gifts that guard each other
  (I.32.45), vices passing as virtues (Pastoral Rule II.9, Moralia XXXI.86), redeemed temperament (III.70).
- Left out: fear / reward / love (a ladder everyone climbs; scoring it grades holiness); the three powers of the soul
  as types (anatomy, not typology); Evagrius as a named foundation (not a saint; carry his list through Cassian).
- Saints with temper on record: Nazianzen (quiet "from the first", "I am weary of being charged with my gentleness"),
  Chrysostom (hot-tempered from youth), Ephrem (quick to anger by nature, changed), Martin (never seen angry),
  Antony (cheerful), Gregory the Great ("sad and melancholy disposition"), young Basil (proud of his speaking).
  Some poles have no saint: say so, never guess.
- Benchmarks: two-sided wording and more items per trait matter more than 5 vs 7 points; the market runs 60-130
  items and 10-15 minutes; drop-off rises about 2 points per 100 items (Hoerger 2010).

Design page (ten report pages for the same example person, "The Still Water"; the eight types; question budget 50
vs 72; five decisions): https://claude.ai/artifact/TuppmFsRVEY1r5LWWRsj6D (source `full-report.src.html`; portraits
are injected from site/public/img/early-christians at build time). AWAITING his five decisions: keep the 8 types
(names are drafts), 50 or 72 questions (recommended 72, 7 answer points), the private fight page with the sins quiz

## Round 3 (2026-09-23, night): writing the quiz

His rulings on the design: keep the 8 types; 72 questions; the private fight page (the seven deadly sins result may ADD
context there, never change it; if not taken, suggest it in a NEW TAB so nobody loses their place); "how common your
type is" built now, a live "N so far" counter until 50 real results, then it switches on by itself (leaning
percentiles at about 200); fear / reward / love out (maybe a separate quiz). Page 10 must name ONE clear line. A full
design pass (colour, images, every portrait) comes once the framework is done.

Files (source of truth, all written by the main session):
- `items.mjs`: the 72 questions and the scales. 12 decide the type (mood, nerve, make-up x 4), 34 for the other 17
  leanings, 2 anger grid, 2 speaker grid, 7 help card (Nazianzen), 12 head-to-head for Cassian's eight thoughts (each
  thought meets three others), 3 for the line in the Body (Cassian Conf. 14.4). Side A is balanced across poles.
- `results.mjs`: all result text. Every quotation is `Q(text, cite)`; `check-quotes.mjs <corpus dirs>` verifies
  them (118 of 118 on 2026-09-23, against the research's saved texts plus b2/b3.txt; the corpus is not committed).
- `score.mjs`: deterministic scoring (pure ES module for preview and site). A link prints only when both answers lean
  its way AND agree with the person's overall reading on those scales. 20,000 random sheets: all 8 types, 8 thoughts
  and 7 lines appear in fair shares.
- `render-text.mjs`: plain-text report for the simulated-people judges.
- Next: simulated people (24 written in everyday terms, answered in character, judged against a friend's view),
  then the design pass and a tap-through preview for him.

Simulated people (`sim/`): 24 people (`people-1.json`, written without the quiz in view, each with a friend's view),
answered in character from their own self-view; fresh judges read person + report.
